# Prime factorization

Every integer greater than 1 can be uniquely expressed as a product of prime numbers, up to ordering. This is called the **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic**.

### Example

\[
12 = 2^2 \times 3
\]
\[
100 = 2^2 \times 5^2
\]
\[
144 = 2^4 \times 3^2
\]
\[
2024 = 2^3 \times 11 \times 23
\]

### Finding prime factors

**Factor tree method:** Repeatedly break the number into factor pairs until all factors are prime.

\[
\begin{array}{c}
 60 \\
 / \\
 2 \quad 30 \\
 / \\
 2 \quad 15 \\
 / \\
 3 \quad 5
\end{array}
\]

So \(60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5\).

**Division method:** Divide by the smallest prime repeatedly, then move to the next prime until the result is 1.

Prime factorization is used for finding GCD, LCM, simplifying fractions, and many cryptographic algorithms.
