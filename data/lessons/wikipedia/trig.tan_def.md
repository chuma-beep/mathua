# Tangent definition and ratio identity

The **tangent** of an angle is one of the six fundamental trigonometric functions. In a right triangle, the tangent of an acute angle is defined as the ratio of the length of the opposite side to the length of the adjacent side:

\[
\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite{\text{adjacent
\]

On the unit circle, the tangent is defined as the ratio of the sine to the cosine:

\[
\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}, \quad \cos \theta \neq 0
\]

The tangent is periodic with period \(\pi\) (180°), and is undefined at angles where \(\cos \theta = 0\), i.e., \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi\) for integer \(n\). Its graph has vertical asymptotes at these points.

### Key identities

\[
\tan(-\theta) = -\tan \theta \quad \text{(odd function)}
\]
\[
\tan(\theta + \pi) = \tan \theta \quad \text{(periodicity)}
\]
\[
\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{\tan \alpha + \tan \beta}{1 - \tan \alpha \tan \beta} \quad \text{(angle sum)}
\]
\[
\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan \theta}{1 - \tan^2 \theta} \quad \text{(double angle)}
\]
