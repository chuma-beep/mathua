# Hundreds place value

In the decimal system, the third digit from the right is the **hundreds** place, representing \(10^2 = 100\).

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{Hundreds} & \text{Tens} & \text{Units} & \text{Value} \\
\hline
2 & 3 & 7 & 200 + 30 + 7 = 237 \\
5 & 0 & 4 & 500 + 0 + 4 = 504 \\
1 & 6 & 2 & 100 + 60 + 2 = 162
\end{array}
\]

A three-digit number expands as:

\[
100 \times (\text{hundreds}) + 10 \times (\text{tens}) + 1 \times (\text{units})
\]

For example, \(237 = 2 \times 100 + 3 \times 10 + 7 \times 1\). Each place value is 10 times the value of the place to its right, which is the core principle of the base-10 system.
