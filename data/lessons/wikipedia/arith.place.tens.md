# Tens and units place value

In the decimal (base-10) number system, each digit's position represents a power of 10. The rightmost digit is the **units** (or ones) place, and the next digit to the left is the **tens** place.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Tens} & \text{Units} & \text{Value} \\
\hline
3 & 5 & 30 + 5 = 35 \\
7 & 2 & 70 + 2 = 72 \\
1 & 0 & 10 + 0 = 10
\end{array}
\]

A two-digit number can be written in expanded form as:

\[
10 \times (\text{tens digit}) + 1 \times (\text{units digit})
\]

For example, \(47 = 4 \times 10 + 7 \times 1\). Understanding place value is essential for addition, subtraction, and all arithmetic operations with multi-digit numbers.
