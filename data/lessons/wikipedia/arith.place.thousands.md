# Thousands place value

In the decimal system, the fourth digit from the right is the **thousands** place, representing \(10^3 = 1000\).

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{Thousands} & \text{Hundreds} & \text{Tens} & \text{Units} & \text{Value} \\
\hline
3 & 4 & 5 & 2 & 3000 + 400 + 50 + 2 = 3452 \\
1 & 0 & 0 & 7 & 1000 + 0 + 0 + 7 = 1007 \\
5 & 8 & 0 & 1 & 5000 + 800 + 0 + 1 = 5801
\end{array}
\]

A four-digit number expands as:

\[
1000 \times (\text{thousands}) + 100 \times (\text{hundreds}) + 10 \times (\text{tens}) + 1 \times (\text{units})
\]

For example, \(3452 = 3 \times 1000 + 4 \times 100 + 5 \times 10 + 2 \times 1\). The pattern continues indefinitely: ten thousands, hundred thousands, millions, and so on, with each place value 10 times the one to its right.
