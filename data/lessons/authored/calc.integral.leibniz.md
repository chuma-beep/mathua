# Differentiation under the integral sign

The Leibniz rule differentiates an integral with respect to a parameter. It moves the derivative inside the integral and adds boundary terms when the limits depend on the parameter.

## Differentiating under the integral

For a parameter a inside the integrand, differentiate the integrand with respect to a. Integrate that derivative over the same interval. The result is the derivative of the original integral when the integrand is sufficiently smooth.

## Evaluating at a parameter

After differentiating, substitute the chosen parameter value. The resulting integral is usually simpler than the original. This is the standard way to evaluate an integral that depends on a parameter.

## Integrating after differentiating

Sometimes it is easier to integrate the derivative first and then differentiate. The two operations commute under the same regularity conditions. Check the boundary terms when the limits depend on the parameter.

Practice connects the three sections: state the relation, carry out the computation and interpret the result. A second method is a useful check when one is available.
