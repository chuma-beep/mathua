# The Hodge star operator

The Hodge star pairs a \(k\)-form with an \((n-k)\)-form using the metric and the volume form.

## The definition on a basis

On an oriented space with a metric the star of a basis form is the complementary basis form, with a sign.

The defining identity is that a form wedged with the star of another gives their inner product times the volume form. Applying the star twice to a one-form multiplies by -1 in two dimensions and by 1 in three dimensions.

## The star in three dimensions

The other basis one-forms follow by cyclic symmetry. The star of the constant function is the volume form.

The star converts a vector field into a two-form. A field with components 2, 3 and -1 becomes \(2\,dy\wedge dz+3\,dz\wedge dx-dx\wedge dy\).

Its integral over the unit cube is 4, since each basis two-form integrates to 1 there.

## The codifferential

The codifferential repeats the star around the derivative, with a sign.

On one-forms it gives minus the divergence, so a divergence-free field is co-closed. For \(x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz\) the star has derivative \(3\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz\), so the codifferential is -3.

The Laplacian is then two first-order operators. That splitting is why the star earns its place.
