# Measurable functions

A measurable function pulls measurable sets back to measurable sets. Indicators, continuous compositions and suprema of measurable functions are measurable.

## Indicator functions

An indicator is one on its set and zero outside it. At a point it answers a membership question. It is measurable when its set is measurable.

## Continuous composition

If f is measurable and g is continuous, the composition g of f is measurable. The preimage of an open set under g is open, and its preimage under f is measurable. Continuity supplies the bridge.

## Suprema

The supremum of finitely many measurable functions is measurable. At each point it is the largest of the values. The preimage of a ray is a finite union of measurable preimages.

The three sections form one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.

A complete answer names the relation, substitutes the given values and states the result in the language of the problem. Where a second method is available, compare the two results before moving on.
