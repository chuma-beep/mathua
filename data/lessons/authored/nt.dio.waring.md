# Waring's problem

Waring's problem asks how many k-th powers are needed to represent every positive integer. For every k the answer is finite, and the exact value is known.

## The function g(k)

The least number of k-th powers that represents every positive integer is g of k. Hilbert proved it exists, and the formula was settled later.

## The formula

For k at least two, g of k equals two to the k plus the floor of three halves to the k, minus two. The formula is exact, not an upper bound.

## Small cases

Four squares suffice by Lagrange, nine cubes suffice, and the count grows quickly. The growth is dominated by the power of two.

To check your understanding, define the least count, raise two to k and recall four squares.

Waring's function is one of the rare exact formulas in additive number theory, and its main term is a power of two.
