# Sums of roots of unity

The n-th roots of unity sum to zero, and so does any power sum whose exponent is not a multiple of n.

## The roots

The roots are \(\zeta^{k}\) for \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}\) and \(k=0,\ldots,n-1\), and they form a regular polygon.

## Power sums

\(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{kj}\) equals \(n\) when \(n\) divides \(j\) and zero otherwise.

## Geometric proof

The sum is a finite geometric series with ratio \(\zeta^{j}\), and the ratio is not one unless \(n\) divides \(j\).

To check your understanding, list the roots, test divisibility and form the ratio.

Roots of unity cancel in complete sets, and only multiples of the order survive.
