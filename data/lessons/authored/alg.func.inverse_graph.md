# Graphs of inverse functions

An inverse function undoes another function. On a graph the relationship is geometric: the graph of \(f^{-1}\) is the reflection of the graph of \(f\) across the line \(y = x\). Every point on one graph has a mirror point on the other.

## Swapping the coordinates

If the graph of \(f\) contains the point \((a, b)\), then \(f(a) = b\). Applying the inverse reverses that step, so \(f^{-1}(b) = a\). The point \((b, a)\) therefore lies on the graph of \(f^{-1}\).

The rule is exactly a swap. The input and the output exchange roles, and nothing else changes. A point on the diagonal, where the two coordinates are equal, stays where it is under reflection.

This is why the inverse is read backwards. A question that gives \(f^{-1}(5) = 2\) is telling you that \(f(2) = 5\), even though the original function was never written down.

## Reading the inverse backwards

The same swap answers questions in the other direction. If the graph of \(f^{-1}\) contains \((a, b)\), then the graph of \(f\) contains \((b, a)\). So \(f(b) = a\) and \(f^{-1}(a) = b\).

A learner who confuses the two functions can check one pair of points. If the pair satisfies the original function, the inverse pair must satisfy the reflected version, and the coordinates have to be reversed.

The domain and range exchange as well. The domain of \(f^{-1}\) is the range of \(f\), and the range of \(f^{-1}\) is the domain of \(f\). A value that never appears as an output of \(f\) cannot be an input to \(f^{-1}\).

## The inverse of a line

A line of slope 1 has the form \(f(x) = x + c\). Its inverse is \(f^{-1}(x) = x - c\), because adding \(c\) and then subtracting \(c\) returns the original input.

If the line passes through \((a, b)\), then \(b = a + c\), so \(c = b - a\). The inverse is \(f^{-1}(x) = x - (b - a)\), and its y-intercept is \(a - b\).

The reflection picture confirms the result. The original line crosses the y-axis at \(b\), and the reflected line crosses the y-axis at the reflected intercept. For a line of slope 1 the two intercepts are opposites once the shift is taken into account.
