# Work by pumping a liquid

## Lifting a slice

Pumping a liquid means lifting each thin slice to the rim, so every slice has its own distance. A cubic metre of water weighs \(1000 \times 9.8 = 9800\) newtons. Lifting it 3 metres takes \(9800 \times 3 = 29400\) joules. Slices at different depths therefore do different amounts of work.

## The work integral

The work integral adds the slices: \(W = \int \rho g A(y)(h - y)\,dy\), where \(h - y\) is the lift. For a tank of constant area 2 square metres and water 4 metres deep with the rim at \(y = 0\), the integral gives \(9800 \cdot 2 \cdot 8 = 156800\) joules.

## A cylindrical tank

A cylindrical tank of radius 2 and height 5, full to the rim, needs \(W = 9800\pi(4)\int_0^5 y\,dy = 9800\pi \cdot 50 \approx 1,539,000\) joules. The empty tank gives zero work and a half-full tank gives one quarter, since both the depth and the lift halve.

A complete answer names the relation, substitutes the data and states the result in the language of the problem. When a second method is available, compare the two results before moving on.
