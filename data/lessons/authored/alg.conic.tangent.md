# Tangent lines to conics

A tangent line touches a conic at exactly one point, and its slope is the slope of the curve there. For circles and parabolas the tangent has a compact algebraic form that avoids a full derivative calculation.

## A tangent to a circle

For the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\), the tangent at a point \((x_0, y_0)\) on the circle is \(x_0 x + y_0 y = r^2\). Rearranging gives the slope \(-x_0/y_0\).

The formula follows from the radius being perpendicular to the tangent. The radius from the center to \((x_0, y_0)\) has slope \(y_0/x_0\), so the tangent slope is its negative reciprocal.

At \((3, 4)\) on the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\), the tangent is \(3x + 4y = 25\). Its slope is \(-3/4\), and it meets the x-axis at \(25/3\).

## A tangent to a parabola

For the parabola \(y = x^2\), the derivative is \(2x\). The tangent at \(x = a\) therefore has slope \(2a\), and its equation is \(y = 2ax - a^2\).

The point of tangency is \((a, a^2)\). The constant term is negative because the tangent meets the y-axis below the vertex.

At \(a = 3\) the point is \((3, 9)\), the slope is 6, and the tangent is \(y = 6x - 9\). Substituting \(x = 3\) returns 9, which checks the point.

## The intercept of a tangent

For the parabola \(y^2 = 4px\), a point with parameter \(t\) is \((pt^2, 2pt)\). The tangent there is \(ty = x + pt^2\), so its x-intercept is \(-pt^2\).

The parameter form keeps the algebra short. The slope is \(1/t\), and the intercept records where the tangent crosses the axis of symmetry.

For \(p = 2\) and \(t = 3\), the point is \((18, 12)\). The tangent is \(3y = x + 18\), and its x-intercept is -18. The intercept is negative because the tangent meets the axis behind the vertex.
