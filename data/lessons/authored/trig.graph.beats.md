# Beats in combined waveforms

Two waves of nearly equal frequency add to a fast wave whose loudness rises and falls at the difference of the two frequencies; that slow rise and fall is the beat.

## Adding two close frequencies

The sum-to-product identity does the work. Taking waves of 440 and 444 cycles per second,
\(\cos(880\pi t) + \cos(888\pi t) = 2\cos(4\pi t)\cos(884\pi t)\). The first factor is slow. The
second carries the average of the two frequencies, 442 cycles per second. The sum is a single fast
wave whose amplitude is \(2|\cos 4\pi t|\).

## The beat frequency

The amplitude factor completes a cycle every 0.5 seconds. Its absolute value peaks twice per
cycle, so the loudness peaks 4 times per second. That count is the difference of the two
frequencies, 444 - 440 = 4. Two forks at 256 and 260 cycles per second beat 4 times per second.
The same pair an octave higher, at 512 and 516, also beats 4 times per second.

## Counting beats over time

Beats accumulate at the difference frequency, so the number heard in a span is that frequency
times the length of the span. The 440 and 444 pair gives 4 beats in one second and 40 in ten
seconds. The 256 and 260 pair gives 3 beats in three quarters of a second. As the difference
shrinks the beats slow down. At zero difference the loudness is steady.

The beat rate is the frequency difference and nothing else.
