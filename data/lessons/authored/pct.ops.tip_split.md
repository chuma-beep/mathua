# Splitting a bill that includes a tip

## Computing the Tip

A tip is a percent of the bill before any tip is added. For a bill of 84 dollars and a tip rate of 18 percent, the tip is \\(0.18 \\times 84 = 15.12\\) dollars.

Compute the tip on the food and drink subtotal, not on the total that already includes tax, unless the custom in that place says otherwise. Use a benchmark to check: 10 percent of 84 is 8.40, so 18 percent must be a little less than double that value.

## Splitting the Total

The amount shared is the bill plus the tip, which is 99.12 dollars. Four diners each pay \\(99.12 \\div 4 = 24.78\\) dollars.

Split after adding the tip, never before. Splitting the bill first and tipping separately gives the same total only when every diner tips at the same rate. Rounding each share can leave the group a few cents short.

## Tip, Tax, and Shares

Some bills carry both tax and tip. Each is a percent of a different base in strict practice. A meal of 60 dollars with 5 percent tax and a 20 percent tip gives tax of 3.00 and a tip of 12.00, so the total is 75.00 dollars.

Five diners then pay 15.00 each. Write the three amounts separately before dividing, because a single combined percentage hides which base produced it and makes the check impossible.
