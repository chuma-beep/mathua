# Tauberian theorems

A Tauberian theorem recovers ordinary convergence from a generalized sum under an extra condition. The condition rules out oscillation.

## Tauberian implication

A generalized method may sum a divergent series. A Tauberian theorem adds a hypothesis that forces ordinary convergence. The conclusion is then the same sum.

## The condition

A common condition bounds the terms or their partial sums. Without such a condition, a generalized sum does not imply convergence. The condition is the price of the implication.

## A counterexample

The series with terms alternating between plus one and minus one has a Cesaro mean but does not converge. The terms do not tend to zero. This shows why the Tauberian hypothesis is necessary.
