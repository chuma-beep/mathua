# Logarithmic scales and orders of magnitude

A logarithmic scale reports how many powers of ten separate two values. Equal distances on the scale mean equal multiplicative factors.

## Orders of magnitude

One order of magnitude is a factor of 10. A value \(10^k\) times another differs by \(k\) orders of magnitude.

Orders of magnitude compare size, not difference. Going from 100 to 1000 is one order, while going from 100 to 200 is less than one.

## Decades and factors

A decade is a factor of 10, so \(k\) decades is a factor of \(10^k\). Two decades is a factor of 100.

The same idea appears in pH, decibels and Richter magnitude, where a fixed change on the scale multiplies the underlying quantity.

## Reading a logarithm

\(\log_{10}(N)\) is the exponent \(k\) such that \(10^k=N\). So \(\log_{10}(1000)=3\).

A logarithm of a number between 1 and 10 lies between 0 and 1. Negative logarithms describe values below 1.

A factor of 1000 is three decades, and a factor of 0.01 is two decades downward. The sign of the exponent records the direction.
