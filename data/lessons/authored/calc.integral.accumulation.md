# Accumulation functions

An accumulation function adds up a rate from a fixed starting point. The Fundamental Theorem connects it to an antiderivative.

## The definition

\(F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)dt\) is the accumulated change from \(a\) to \(x\). The variable \(t\) is a dummy variable.

Changing the starting point changes \(F\) by a constant, so the derivative is unchanged.

The accumulation is signed: area below the axis counts as negative change.

## The derivative

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, \(F'(x)=f(x)\). The rate of accumulation is the integrand.

For \(F(x)=\int_a^x 2t\,dt\), \(F'(x)=2x\).

The derivative does not depend on the lower limit, which is why the starting point only shifts the function.

## Solving for an endpoint

To find \(x\) with \(F(x)=V\), first compute the antiderivative, then solve the resulting equation.

Check that the solution lies in the domain of the integrand and beyond the starting point.

A second solution may lie on the other side of the starting point, where the accumulated change is negative.
