# Equations that need distributing first

## Distributing Before Solving

A bracket with a multiplier outside it blocks the usual steps, so distribute first. In \\(3(x + 4) = 27\\), multiply both terms inside by 3 to get \\(3x + 12 = 27\\).

Subtract 12 to reach \\(3x = 15\\), then divide by 3 for \\(x = 5\\). Dividing both sides by 3 before distributing also works here, but the bracket form is safer once the right side is not a multiple of the multiplier.

## A Negative Multiplier

A negative multiplier changes the sign of every term inside the bracket. For \\(-2(x - 5) = 14\\), distribute to get \\(-2x + 10 = 14\\), because a negative times a negative is positive.

Subtract 10 for \\(-2x = 4\\), then divide by -2 to get \\(x = -2\\). Check it: \\(x - 5 = -7\\) and \\(-2 \\times (-7) = 14\\). Forgetting the sign change is the usual error here.

## Two Steps After Distributing

Distribution can produce a coefficient that still needs two operations. Take \\(4(2x - 1) = 20\\). Distributing gives \\(8x - 4 = 20\\), then \\(8x = 24\\), so \\(x = 3\\).

Both steps are needed. The order follows the usual rule: undo the subtraction, then undo the multiplication. Verify by substituting 3 back into the bracket, which gives \\(4 \\times 5 = 20\\).
