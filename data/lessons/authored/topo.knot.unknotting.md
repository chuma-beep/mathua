# Unknotting numbers

The unknotting number is the minimum number of crossing changes needed to turn a knot into the unknot. It measures how far a knot is from being trivial.

## The trefoil

The trefoil has unknotting number one. Changing either crossing of its standard diagram produces the unknot.

## The figure-eight

The figure-eight knot also has unknotting number one. One crossing change is enough, although the knot is amphichiral.

## Connected sums

Unknotting numbers are subadditive: \(u(K_1 \# K_2) \le u(K_1) + u(K_2)\). The sum of two trefoils has unknotting number at most two.

To check your understanding, change one crossing, recall its unknotting number and add the two numbers.

The unknotting number counts crossing changes rather than crossings.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
