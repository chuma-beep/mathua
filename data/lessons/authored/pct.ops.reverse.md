# Reverse percentages

## Finding the whole

A part and its percentage can recover the whole. Divide the part by the percentage written as a decimal. If 30 is 25 percent of a number, the number is 30 / 0.25 = 120.

The whole must be larger than the part when the percentage is below 100. If the answer is smaller, the decimal point or the division is wrong. Check by taking the percentage of the recovered whole.

## Finding the part

When the whole and the percentage are known, multiply the whole by the decimal form of the percentage. This is the forward direction of the same relationship.

Keep the percentage and the whole in the same units. A percentage has no units, so it multiplies a quantity and returns a quantity of the same kind.

## Finding the percent

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100 to express the comparison as a percent. The part may be larger than the whole when the percentage is above 100.

Check that the percent describes the intended comparison. The denominator is the quantity that represents the whole, not automatically the larger number.

A final check is to restate the answer in the units and context of the original question. If the result is plausible and the units match, the calculation is ready.
