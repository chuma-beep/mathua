# Subtracting a decimal from a fraction

A fraction and a decimal subtract once both are written in the same form. The exact route uses fractions and the decimal route is quicker when the fraction terminates.

## The method

Convert the decimal to a fraction over a power of ten and reduce or convert the fraction to a decimal by dividing. Subtract in the chosen form. Keep the sign in view, since the decimal may be the larger term. For \(\frac{1}{2} - 0.2\) the difference is 0.3.

## A worked difference

Compute \(\frac{7}{8} - 0.5\). The decimal is \(\frac{1}{2}\), so the fraction route gives \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{4}{8} = \frac{3}{8}\). The decimal route gives \(0.875 - 0.5 = 0.375\), which is the same value. A second case is \(0.9 - \frac{2}{5}\), where the fraction is 0.4 and the difference is 0.5, equal to \(\frac{1}{2}\).

## Checking the result

Add the difference to the decimal: \(0.375 + 0.5 = 0.875\), which is \(\frac{7}{8}\). In fraction form, \(\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{8} + \frac{4}{8} = \frac{7}{8}\). For the second case, \(0.5 + 0.4 = 0.9\). The number of decimal places in the answer matches the longest input, which is three places for the first case.

One form for both terms turns the subtraction into a single step.
