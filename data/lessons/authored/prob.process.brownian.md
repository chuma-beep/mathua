# Brownian motion

Brownian motion is the continuous-time limit of a random walk. Its increments are Gaussian, independent over disjoint intervals and stationary.

## Gaussian increments

Standard Brownian motion starts at zero. The increment over an interval of length t has mean zero and variance t. Disjoint increments are independent, which makes the process a continuous Gaussian process.

## Covariance

The covariance of B_s and B_t is the smaller of s and t. If s is smaller, the later increment is independent of the earlier value. This covariance kernel is the defining positive-definite kernel of Brownian motion.

## Hitting probabilities

For driftless Brownian motion started at zero, the probability of hitting a before negative b is b divided by a plus b. The scale function is linear, which makes the hitting probability a ratio of distances. Multiplying by a plus b clears the fraction.
