# Helmholtz decomposition

The Helmholtz decomposition writes a vector field as a curl-free part plus a divergence-free part. On a simply connected domain the decomposition is unique up to a harmonic term.

## Divergence-free part

The divergence measures the source density of a field. A field with zero divergence has no sources or sinks. The curl of a vector potential is always divergence-free.

## Curl-free part

The curl measures the local rotation of a field. A gradient field has zero curl. The gradient part of the decomposition carries the sources.

## Component count

In three dimensions a vector field has three components. The decomposition separates one scalar potential and one vector potential, with gauge freedom in the vector potential. A finite example can track each component separately.

The three sections form one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.
