# The method of multiple scales

The method of multiple scales introduces slow times beside the fast time so that secular terms can be absorbed rather than growing without bound. It turns a weakly nonlinear oscillation into a slow amplitude equation.

## Slow times

The solution is allowed to depend on the fast time and on one or more slow times scaled by powers of the parameter. The ordinary derivative becomes a sum of partial derivatives with respect to each time.

## Removing secular terms

At each order the equation is a forced oscillator. A forcing at the natural frequency produces a response that grows linearly in time, and setting that resonant coefficient to zero is the solvability condition.

## The amplitude equation

The solvability conditions are a first-order system for the amplitude and phase on the slow time. Its solutions describe how the oscillation grows, decays, or shifts frequency over long times.

To check your understanding, introduce the slow time, identify the resonant forcing and collect the conditions.

Multiple scales is the systematic way to derive an amplitude equation, and the secular terms are the bookkeeping that makes it work.
