# The discriminant

The discriminant b^2-4ac decides whether a quadratic has distinct roots, a double root or a conjugate pair.

## Formula

For \(az^{2}+bz+c\) the discriminant is \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\).

## Sign

A positive discriminant gives two real roots, a negative one gives a conjugate pair and zero gives a double root.

## Double roots

A double root occurs exactly when the discriminant vanishes, and then the root is \(-b/(2a)\).

To check your understanding, square the linear coefficient, test the sign and set the discriminant to zero.

One integer decides the entire root picture of a quadratic.
