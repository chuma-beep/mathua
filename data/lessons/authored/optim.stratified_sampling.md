# Stratified sampling

A population can be split into groups whose internal variation is small. Sampling inside each group and combining the group estimates removes the between-group component of the variance.

## The strata

Each stratum is sampled separately and weighted by its share of the population. The combined estimator is unbiased and its variance is the weighted sum of the within-stratum variances.

## Allocation

Samples can be allocated in proportion to stratum size or in proportion to size times standard deviation. The second rule minimises variance for a fixed budget when the strata differ in spread.

## The gain

The reduction equals the between-stratum variance that the unstratified design would have carried. Strong separation between strata therefore gives a large gain, and overlapping strata give almost none.

To check your understanding, sample each stratum, use proportional allocation and remove between-group variance.

Stratification is useful when the data has natural blocks, for example by class or by region, and the block means differ while the within-block spread stays modest.
