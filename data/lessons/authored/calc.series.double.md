# Convergence of double series

## Sums over a grid

A double series sums \(a_{mn}\) over the grid of positive integers. Taking \(a_{mn} = 2^{-m-n}\), the two-by-two block totals \(0.75 \times 0.75 = 0.5625\) while the full grid totals 1. Each larger square block adds the missing outer row and column, so the block totals increase toward the limit.

## Absolute convergence

Absolute convergence means \(\sum |a_{mn}|\) is finite. Then every exhaustion of the grid gives the same total. With \(a_{mn} = (-1)^{m+n}2^{-m-n}\) the absolute sum is 1. The signed sum separates into the square of \(\sum (-1)^m 2^{-m} = -1/3\), giving \(1/9\). The order of summation cannot change it.

## Separating the factors

When \(a_{mn} = b_m c_n\) and both factor series converge absolutely, the double sum is the product of the sums. With \(b_m = 3^{-m}\) and \(c_n = 2^{-n}\) the factors total \(1/2\) and 1, so the grid totals 0.5. The two-by-two block gives \(0.4444 \times 0.75 = 0.3333\), on the way to that value.
