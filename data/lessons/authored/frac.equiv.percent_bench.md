# Common fraction and percent equivalents

A percent is a fraction with denominator 100, so familiar fractions have familiar percent values. Knowing a few of them turns many comparisons into mental arithmetic.

## The rule

The common benchmarks are \(\frac{1}{2} = 50\) percent, \(\frac{1}{4} = 25\) percent, \(\frac{1}{5} = 20\) percent, \(\frac{1}{10} = 10\) percent and \(\frac{1}{8} = 12.5\) percent. Any fraction converts by dividing numerator by denominator and multiplying by 100. The reverse writes the percent over 100 and reduces.

## A worked example

Compare \(\frac{3}{8}\) with \(\frac{7}{20}\). The first is \(3 \times 12.5 = 37.5\) percent and the second is \(7 \times 5 = 35\) percent. So \(\frac{3}{8}\) is the larger value. Another useful pair is \(\frac{5}{6}\), which is about 83.33 percent, against \(\frac{4}{5}\), which is 80 percent and the first is again larger.

## Checking the result

Convert 37.5 percent back: \(\frac{37.5}{100} = \frac{375}{1000} = \frac{3}{8}\), which returns the original fraction. For 35 percent, \(\frac{35}{100} = \frac{7}{20}\). A percent that does not reduce to the starting fraction signals an arithmetic slip and a value above 100 percent signals a fraction above 1. A percent above 100 corresponds to an improper fraction and a percent below 1 to a fraction below one hundredth.

The benchmarks are worth memorising because they convert a comparison into one subtraction.
