# Handle decompositions

A handle decomposition builds a surface by attaching handles of index zero, one and two. The counts of handles are read directly from the genus and the number of boundary circles.

## Handle counts

A closed orientable surface of genus \(g\) has one 0-handle, \(2g\) 1-handles and one 2-handle. The total is \(2g + 2\), which matches the Euler characteristic after cancellation.

## Boundary

Each boundary circle is created by omitting a 2-handle or by attaching a 1-handle between two boundary components. A surface of genus \(g\) with \(b\) boundary circles has \(b\) fewer 2-handles than its closed completion.

## Reading the genus

The number of 1-handles equals twice the genus for an orientable surface without boundary. A single 1-handle attached with a twist produces a Möbius band and changes orientability rather than genus.

To check your understanding, count the 1-handles, relate boundary circles to omitted handles and halve the 1-handle count.

Handles turn the classification into arithmetic on three small counts.
