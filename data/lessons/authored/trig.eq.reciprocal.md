# Equations with reciprocal trigonometric functions

A reciprocal trigonometric equation contains secant, cosecant or cotangent, so rewriting those ratios as cosine, sine and tangent reduces it to an equation in the three basic functions.

## Rewriting in sine and cosine

Each reciprocal has a direct definition to substitute. The equation \(\sec x = 2\) becomes
\(\cos x = 1/2\), whose solutions in one turn are 60 and 300 degrees. Likewise \(\csc x = -2\)
becomes \(\sin x = -1/2\), giving 210 and 330 degrees. Cotangent is the quotient
\(\cos x/\sin x\), so a cotangent equation is really a ratio of two basic functions.

## Clearing denominators

Cleared of fractions, a reciprocal equation often becomes a polynomial in one ratio. The equation
\(\csc^{2}x = 4\) gives \(\sin^{2}x = 1/4\), so \(\sin x = 1/2\) or \(\sin x = -1/2\). The four
angles 30, 150, 210 and 330 degrees all satisfy it. Clearing is safe only after recording that no
denominator can ever vanish.

## Rejecting undefined values

A value that makes a denominator zero is not a solution, however well it fits the cleared
equation. The equation \(\sec x = 0\) has no solution at all, because \(1/\cos x\) is never zero.
In \(\tan x + \cot x = 2\), multiplying by \(\tan x\) leaves a quadratic in \(\tan x\) whose only
root is 1. The angles are then 45 and 225 degrees. Neither makes a denominator vanish in the
original equation.

Record the excluded angles beside the solution set itself.
