# Mixed number comparison word problems

A comparison word problem asks which of two measured quantities is larger. When both quantities are mixed numbers in the same unit, the comparison follows the same whole-then-fraction rule.

## The method

Read both quantities in the same unit before comparing. Compare the whole parts and compare the fractional parts only when the wholes are equal. To report how much larger, subtract the smaller quantity from the larger. The lengths \(2\frac{1}{2}\) and \(2\frac{3}{4}\) differ by \(\frac{1}{4}\). Keeping the unit fixed prevents a false comparison between unlike measures.

## A worked example

Two ropes measure \(3\frac{2}{3}\) metres and \(3\frac{3}{4}\) metres. The wholes are equal, so compare the fractions over twelfths: \(\frac{8}{12}\) against \(\frac{9}{12}\). The second rope is longer and the difference is \(\frac{1}{12}\) of a metre. A second pair is \(2\frac{5}{8}\) kilograms and \(2\frac{2}{3}\) kilograms, where the fractions are \(\frac{15}{24}\) and \(\frac{16}{24}\), so the second is heavier by \(\frac{1}{24}\) of a kilogram.

## Checking the result

Add the difference back to the shorter quantity: \(3\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{12} = 3\frac{8}{12} + \frac{1}{12} = 3\frac{9}{12} = 3\frac{3}{4}\), which is the longer rope. For the weights, \(2\frac{5}{8} + \frac{1}{24} = 2\frac{15}{24} + \frac{1}{24} = 2\frac{16}{24} = 2\frac{2}{3}\).

The whole parts settle most cases and the difference states the gap in the same unit.
