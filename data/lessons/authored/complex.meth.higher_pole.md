# Poles of higher order

A pole of order m needs the coefficient of (z-c)^{-1} in the Laurent expansion.

## Order

A pole has order \(m\) when the denominator vanishes to order \(m\) and the numerator does not.

## Coefficient index

In the Laurent series the residue is the coefficient of \((z-c)^{-1}\), the term with exponent minus one.

## Simple case

Order one reduces to the simple-pole formula, and order two needs one derivative of the numerator.

To check your understanding, factor the denominator, expand in powers and reduce to order one.

Higher-order poles are handled by reading one Laurent coefficient.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
