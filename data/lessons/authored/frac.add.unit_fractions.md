# Sums of unit fractions

A unit fraction has numerator 1 and two of them add by a formula rather than by counting equal parts. The sum of \(\frac{1}{a}\) and \(\frac{1}{b}\) is rarely a unit fraction, so the result needs its own reduction.

## The method

For positive whole numbers \(a\) and \(b\), the identity \(\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a + b}{ab}\) holds. The product \(ab\) is a common denominator and each numerator becomes the other denominator. Reduce the result afterward. The product is always a common denominator, though it need not be the least one. The common error is to add the denominators, which would give \(\frac{2}{a+b}\) and is wrong.

## A worked sum

Add \(\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{1}{6}\). The product 24 is a common denominator, so the sum is \(\frac{6}{24} + \frac{4}{24} = \frac{10}{24} = \frac{5}{12}\). The formula gives \(\frac{4 + 6}{24} = \frac{10}{24}\) directly. A second case is \(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{8}{15}\). When the denominators match, the sum doubles the numerator: \(\frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{2}{5}\).

## Checking the result

Subtract one addend from the sum: \(\frac{5}{12} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{5}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}\), which returns the other addend. A decimal check gives \(0.25 + 0.1667 = 0.4167\) and \(\frac{5}{12}\) is 0.4167.

The denominators multiply and only the numerators add.
