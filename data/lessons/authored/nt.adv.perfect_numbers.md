# Perfect, abundant and deficient numbers

Perfect numbers equal the sum of their proper divisors. Abundant and deficient numbers sit on either side, and the classification is a first exercise in divisor arithmetic.

## The classification

Compare the proper-divisor sum with the number. Equality gives perfect, a larger sum gives abundant and a smaller sum gives deficient. Every positive integer falls into exactly one class.

## Counting examples

The perfect numbers begin 6, 28 and 496. Abundant numbers begin 12, 18 and 20. Counting within a range is a matter of testing each candidate or using the known classification.

## The next perfect number

After a given bound, search upward for the first number whose proper divisors sum to itself. The search is finite for small bounds, and the perfect numbers grow quickly.

The three sections are one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.
