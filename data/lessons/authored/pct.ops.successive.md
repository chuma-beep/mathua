# Successive percentage changes

## Two increases

Successive changes multiply. After a 20 percent increase the multiplier is 1.20. A second 10 percent increase multiplies that result by 1.10, so the combined multiplier is 1.32.

Apply the changes in order and keep each intermediate value. Adding the two percentages gives 30 percent, but the true increase is 32 percent because the second increase also applies to the first increase.

## Increase then decrease

An increase followed by a decrease uses one multiplier above 1 and one below 1. A 20 percent increase followed by a 20 percent decrease has multiplier 1.20 x 0.80 = 0.96, so the net result is a 4 percent decrease.

Equal percentage changes in opposite directions do not cancel. The decrease applies to the larger intermediate value, which is why the final value is below the starting value.

## The net multiplier

Multiply all the individual multipliers to get the net multiplier. Subtract 1 and multiply by 100 to express the result as a net percent change. A net multiplier below 1 is a net decrease.

Check the sign and the size. A net multiplier of 0.96 means 96 percent remains, so the change is a 4 percent decrease. The order of multiplication does not change the net multiplier.
