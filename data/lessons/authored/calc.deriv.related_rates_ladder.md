# Related rates: sliding ladder

A sliding ladder links the horizontal and vertical distances through the Pythagorean theorem. Differentiating that relation connects the rates.

## The geometric relation

For a ladder of length \(L\), \(x^2+y^2=L^2\). The length is constant, so its derivative is zero.

Differentiating gives \(2x\,dx/dt+2y\,dy/dt=0\).

The relation holds at every instant, which is why differentiating it produces a relation between the rates.

## Solving for a rate

Substitute the current position and the known rate. Solve for the unknown rate.

Choose a Pythagorean triple so the arithmetic is exact. The signs tell whether a distance is increasing or decreasing.

A rate is instantaneous, so use the position at that instant, not an average position.

## Direction and speed

As the top descends, the base moves away from the wall. The speeds are related by the ratio of the two distances.

A common error is to forget that the top’s rate is negative when it moves downward.

The base moves faster when the ladder is nearly vertical, because the ratio \(y/x\) is large.
