# The Poincare-Bendixson theorem

In the plane a bounded trajectory that stays inside a closed region without a fixed point must approach a closed orbit. The theorem rules out chaos in two dimensions and proves that a limit cycle exists.

## The trapping region

A trapping region is compact and the flow enters it across the boundary and never leaves. The boundary is usually built from nullclines or from a large circle where the field points inward.

## The statement

A non-empty compact limit set of a planar flow that contains no fixed point is a closed orbit. The trajectory starting inside the trapping region therefore spirals onto a periodic solution.

## Consequences and limits

The theorem proves existence without producing a formula, and it fails in three dimensions where a bounded orbit can wander without repeating. That failure is the opening for chaotic attractors.

To check your understanding, construct the boundary, rule out fixed points and state what the theorem does not give.

Poincare-Bendixson is an existence theorem with a topological proof, and its dimensional restriction is as important as its conclusion.
