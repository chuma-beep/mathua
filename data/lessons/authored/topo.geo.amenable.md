# Amenable groups

Amenability is the existence of translation-invariant finitely additive means on bounded functions. Abelian and finite groups are amenable, while a free group of rank two is not.

## Abelian groups

Every abelian group is amenable. The group \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) is the standard example, and its Følner sets are large boxes.

## Free groups

A free group of rank at least two is not amenable. The two-generator free group already contains a paradoxical decomposition.

## Finite groups

Every finite group is amenable. The uniform measure is already invariant, so the mean is immediate.

To check your understanding, recognise amenability, recognise non-amenability and use the uniform measure.

Amenability separates lattices from free groups.
