# Continued fraction factorisation

The continued fraction of the square root of n produces convergents whose squares are small modulo n. Smooth convergents combine into a factor.

## The expansion

The continued fraction of the square root of n is periodic. Its convergents give rational approximations whose error is small.

## The congruence

Each convergent p over q satisfies p squared minus n q squared equals a small integer. That integer is a candidate smooth value.

## Combining relations

Smooth values are collected until their exponent vectors have a dependency modulo two. The dependency yields a congruence of squares and a factor.

To check your understanding, expand the root, square the numerator and collect smooth values.

Continued fraction factorisation is the quadratic sieve's ancestor, and the periodicity of the expansion supplies the relations.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
