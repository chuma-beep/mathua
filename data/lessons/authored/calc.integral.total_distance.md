# Total distance from velocity

Distance travelled is the integral of speed, not velocity. A sign change in velocity splits the motion into separate pieces.

## Sign and direction

Velocity is positive when the particle moves forward and negative when it moves backward. Speed is \(|v(t)|\).

A zero of velocity is a turning point. The particle stops instantaneously and reverses direction.

Displacement uses velocity, while distance uses speed.

## Splitting the integral

Integrate the absolute value of velocity. Equivalently, split the interval at each zero and integrate the velocity with the correct sign.

Integrating velocity without splitting gives displacement, which can be smaller than the distance travelled.

A particle that moves forward and back can have zero displacement and positive distance.

## Computing the total

For \(v(t)=t-a\) on \([0,b]\), the distance is the sum of the two areas. The first piece is \(a^2/2\) and the second is \((b-a)^2/2\).

The total distance is never negative. A negative result signals a sign error in one of the pieces.
