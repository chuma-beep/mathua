# Depreciation as a percent decrease

## Value After One Year

Depreciation is a percent decrease applied to the value of an object. The loss is the rate multiplied by the starting value. A car worth 18,000 dollars that loses 15 percent in a year loses \\(0.15 \\times 18000 = 2700\\) dollars.

Subtract the loss from the starting value to find the new value, which is 15,300 dollars. Equivalently, multiply by 0.85, since the car keeps 85 percent of its value. Both routes give the same answer when the arithmetic is correct.

## Finding the Loss

The loss is the difference between the two values. From 18,000 down to 15,300 the loss is 2,700 dollars. Reporting a loss as a percent means dividing it by the starting value, not by the new value: \\(2700 \\div 18000 = 0.15\\).

Divide by the new value and the result is about 17.6 percent, which overstates the decline. The starting value is always the base of a percent decrease.

## Repeated Annual Loss

A second year of 15 percent applies to the value at the start of that year, not to the original price. The second loss is \\(0.15 \\times 15300 = 2295\\) dollars, leaving 13,005 dollars.

Two annual losses of 15 percent do not total 30 percent. Thirty percent of 18,000 would be 5,400, while the actual two year loss is 4,995. The shrinking base makes each later loss smaller than the one before it.
