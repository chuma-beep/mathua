# Factoring out a common factor

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor

Factoring undoes distribution, so it starts by finding a factor shared by every term. The greatest common factor of the coefficients is the largest number that divides each one. For 12 and 18 the divisors 1, 2, 3 and 6 are shared. The largest is 6.

Include variables only when every term contains them. Take the smallest power present. A term with \\(x^2\\) beside a term with \\(x\\) shares one \\(x\\), never two. The shared factor must divide each term exactly.

## Factoring Two Terms

Divide each term by the common factor and write the factor outside a bracket. Since 12 and 18 share 6, the expression \\(12x + 18\\) becomes \\(6(2x + 3)\\). Check each inside term: six times two is 12. Six times three is 18.

If a term equals the factor itself, its place inside the bracket is 1, not 0. The expression \\(5x + 5\\) factors as \\(5(x + 1)\\). Dropping that 1 changes the value at every input.

## Checking by Distributing

Distribution reverses factoring, so it is the check. Multiply the outside factor by each inside term and confirm the original expression returns. For \\(6(2x + 3)\\), the product is \\(12x + 18\\), which matches.

A second example: \\(8x - 20\\) factors as \\(4(2x - 5)\\). Distributing gives \\(8x - 20\\). When the check fails, the usual cause is a sign, because the negative term needs a negative inside the bracket.
