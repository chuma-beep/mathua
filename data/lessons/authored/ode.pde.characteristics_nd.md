# Characteristics in higher dimensions

A first-order linear partial differential equation transports its initial data along curves. In more than one space dimension the curves are characteristics, and their geometry decides whether a solution exists near a surface.

## Characteristic surfaces

A surface is non-characteristic when the coefficient vector of the equation is not tangent to it. Data posed on a non-characteristic surface determine the normal derivative and hence a Taylor series for the solution.

## Transport in several variables

For a constant coefficient equation with no source term, the solution is constant along every line whose direction is the coefficient vector. The initial data therefore propagates unchanged along those lines.

## Breakdown

Two characteristics that meet carry the data from different initial points. If the data disagree the solution cannot be single-valued at the meeting point, and a classical solution stops existing there.

To check your understanding, write the coefficient vector, write the characteristic lines and find where two characteristics meet.

The characteristic geometry is the same in any dimension; what changes is that surfaces, not just curves, can be characteristic.
