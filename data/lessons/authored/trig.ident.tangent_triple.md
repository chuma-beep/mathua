# The tangent triple-angle formula

The tangent triple-angle formula writes \(\tan 3x\) in terms of \(t = \tan x\) alone, as \((3t - t^{3})/(1 - 3t^{2})\).

## The triple-angle formula

The formula comes from adding \(2x\) and \(x\). A good test is an angle whose triple is already
known. At 45 degrees the tangent is 1. The formula gives -1, which is the tangent of 135. At 63.4
degrees the tangent is 2; the formula gives 2/11, about 0.1818, which is the tangent of 190.3
degrees. The denominator vanishes at 30 degrees, where the triple is 90 and the tangent is
infinite, so the formula reports that case correctly.

## The reciprocal tangent case

For cotangent, invert the formula and substitute \(c = \cot x\). The result is
\(\cot 3x = (c^{3} - 3c)/(3c^{2} - 1)\). At 45 degrees the cotangent is 1; the formula gives -1,
matching the cotangent of 135. At an angle whose cotangent is 2, near 26.6 degrees, the formula
gives 2/11, about 0.1818; the cotangent of 79.7 degrees agrees.

## Signs and reductions

The formula has period 60 degrees in \(x\), because the triple angle has period 180. At 15 degrees
the tangent is 0.2679; the formula gives about 1, which is the tangent of 45. A negative tangent
gives a negative result, so quadrant information passes through without extra cases. That is why
this formula needs no case analysis, unlike the sine and cosine triple-angle forms.

Check the denominator before trusting the formula.
