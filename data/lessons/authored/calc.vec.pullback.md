# Pullback of a differential form

A pullback transports a differential form through a map. It substitutes the map into the form and applies the chain rule to the differentials.

## Pullback of one-forms

For a map phi, the pullback phi star of dx is the differential of the x-component of phi. If x is a times u, then phi star dx is a du. The coefficient is the corresponding partial derivative.

## Pullback of two-forms

A two-form pulls back by wedging the pulled-back one-forms. For the linear map (u,v) to (a u,b v), dx wedge dy becomes a b du wedge dv. The determinant of the linear map is the coefficient.

## The Jacobian factor

The pullback of a top-degree form contains the Jacobian determinant. That determinant is exactly the factor by which the map scales oriented area. A negative determinant reverses orientation.

The three sections form one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.
