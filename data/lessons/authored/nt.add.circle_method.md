# The circle method

The circle method converts a counting problem into an integral of an exponential sum over the unit circle. Near rational points the sum is large, and elsewhere it cancels.

## The integral

A representation count is the integral of a product of exponential sums. The integrand is periodic, so the integral can be split into arcs.

## Major and minor arcs

Arcs near rationals with small denominators contribute the main term. The remaining minor arcs are bounded by cancellation estimates.

## The singular series

The major-arc contribution factors into a product over primes. That product is the singular series, and it carries the arithmetic of the equation.

To check your understanding, write the exponential sum, choose small denominators and factor over primes.

The circle method is Fourier analysis on the circle, and the split between major and minor arcs is where the arithmetic separates from the analysis.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
