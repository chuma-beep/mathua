# Distance between two fractions on the number line

The distance between two fractions is the absolute value of their difference. It is a length, so it is never negative and it does not depend on which point is named first.

## The method

For points \(x\) and \(y\), the distance is \(\lvert x - y \rvert\). Subtract in either order and take the positive value. When one point is negative, the distance is the sum of the two magnitudes, which the formula handles automatically. The points \(\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{3}{4}\) are \(\frac{1}{2}\) apart.

## A worked difference

Find the distance between \(\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{5}{6}\). Over twelfths the points are \(\frac{3}{12}\) and \(\frac{10}{12}\), so the distance is \(\frac{7}{12}\). A case that crosses zero is \(-\frac{1}{2}\) and \(\frac{3}{4}\), where the distance is \(\frac{3}{4} - (-\frac{1}{2}) = \frac{5}{4}\). That is larger than 1, as it must be for points on opposite sides of zero.

## Checking the result

Measure from zero to the first point and add the gap: \(\frac{3}{12} + \frac{7}{12} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}\), which is the second coordinate. For the crossing case, \(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{5}{4} = \frac{7}{4}\) and the point \(\frac{3}{4}\) sits at distance \(\frac{3}{4}\) from zero, so the signed difference is consistent.

Distance is a positive length and the sign of the subtraction disappears inside the absolute value.
