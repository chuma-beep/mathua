# Differential forms

A differential form is a field of alternating multilinear maps. One-forms eat one vector, two-forms eat two vectors, and the number of independent components is a binomial coefficient.

## One-forms and their action

A one-form a dx plus b dy plus c dz acts on a vector (p,q,r) by a p plus b q plus c r. The coefficients are functions on the space. At a point, the result is a scalar. The coordinate one-forms dx, dy and dz read the corresponding components of the vector.

## Two-forms on pairs of vectors

A two-form is alternating, so swapping the two vectors changes its sign. The basic forms dy wedge dz, dz wedge dx and dx wedge dy evaluate as 2 by 2 determinants. A general two-form is a linear combination of those determinants.

## The dimension of k-forms

On R^n, a k-form is determined by its values on every k-tuple of coordinate directions. Alternation removes the order and leaves one independent component for each k-element subset. The count is therefore the binomial coefficient C(n,k).

The three sections form one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.
