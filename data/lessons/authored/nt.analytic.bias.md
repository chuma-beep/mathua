# Chebyshev's bias

Chebyshev noticed that primes seem to prefer some residue classes over others. The bias is real but delicate, and it reverses infinitely often.

## The observation

Among odd primes, those congruent to three modulo four usually outnumber those congruent to one. The lead is small and not monotone.

## The logarithmic density

The bias is not a statement about a limiting proportion. It is a statement about the logarithmic measure of the set of heights where one class leads.

## Counting a range

For a finite bound, the counts in the two classes can be compared directly. The comparison is a finite computation that need not follow the asymptotic bias.

To check your understanding, compare two classes, distinguish density from limit and sieve a finite range.

Chebyshev's bias is a warning that the first few million primes can mislead, and that density statements need their exact mode of convergence.
