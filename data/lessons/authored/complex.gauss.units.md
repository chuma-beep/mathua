# Units of the Gaussian integers

A unit is a Gaussian integer whose inverse is also a Gaussian integer. There are exactly four: 1, -1, i and -i.

## The four units

A unit must have norm one, and the only integer solutions of \(a^{2}+b^{2}=1\) are \((\pm1,0)\) and \((0,\pm1)\).

## Multiplication by a unit

Multiplying by \(i\) rotates a Gaussian integer by a quarter turn. Multiplication by \(-1\) negates both parts.

## Associates

Two Gaussian integers are associates when they differ by a unit. Every nonzero element has four associates, all with the same norm.

To check your understanding, count the units, apply a quarter turn and identify an associate.

Units are the only Gaussian integers with an inverse, and there are four of them.
