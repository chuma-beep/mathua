# Estimating with mixed numbers

Mixed numbers are estimated by rounding each fractional part to a familiar benchmark and carrying the rounding into the whole part. The estimate gives the size of the answer before exact arithmetic.

## The method

Round \(\frac{7}{8}\) to 1 and add that whole to the whole part or round a small fraction to 0 and drop it. A fraction near \(\frac{1}{2}\) may stay as a half. Then add or subtract the rounded mixed numbers. Keep the direction of each rounding, because the estimate may sit above or below the exact value.

## A worked example

Estimate \(4\frac{7}{8} + 2\frac{1}{6}\). Rounding the first fraction to 1 gives 5 and rounding the second to 0 leaves 2, so the estimate is 7. The exact sum is \(4\frac{21}{24} + 2\frac{4}{24} = 6\frac{25}{24} = 7\frac{1}{24}\). For a difference, \(7\frac{9}{10} - 3\frac{1}{5}\) estimates as \(8 - 3 = 5\), while the exact value is 4.7.

## Checking the result

The exact sum is \(7\frac{1}{24}\), so the estimate of 7 is low by one twenty fourth. The two roundings moved in opposite directions, which kept the error small. The difference estimate is high by 0.3, since the minuend was rounded up by a tenth and the subtrahend was left exact.

Rounding a mixed number moves the whole part, so the error is easier to see.
