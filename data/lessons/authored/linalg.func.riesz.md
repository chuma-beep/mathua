# Riesz representation for inner products

Every linear functional on a finite-dimensional inner product space is evaluation against a unique vector. This identifies the dual with the space itself.

## Existence

Choose an orthonormal basis and read the coefficients of the functional. The vector built from those coefficients represents the functional.

## Uniqueness

If two vectors represent the same functional, their difference pairs to zero with every vector. Pairing with itself then forces the difference to vanish.

## Consequences

The identification is conjugate-linear in the complex case. It is the tool behind adjoints, least squares and the normal equations.

To check your understanding, read coefficients, assume two vectors and track conjugation.

Riesz representation is why a finite-dimensional inner product space can be treated as its own dual.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
