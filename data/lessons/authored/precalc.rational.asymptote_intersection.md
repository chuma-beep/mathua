# Intersections with an oblique asymptote

A rational function can cross its oblique asymptote. The crossing points are the finite solutions of a polynomial equation.

## The oblique asymptote

Divide the numerator by the denominator to find the linear part. That line is the oblique asymptote. The remainder controls the approach at infinity.

## Solving the intersection

Set the rational function equal to the asymptote line. Multiply by the denominator and simplify. The resulting equation has degree equal to the remainder degree.

## When intersections are absent

If the simplified equation has no real solution, the graph never crosses the asymptote. A positive constant remainder keeps the graph on one side. The asymptotic approach is still present.
