# The fundamental theorem for complex polynomials

Every nonconstant polynomial with complex coefficients has a root, so degree n counts n roots with multiplicity.

## Statement

A polynomial of degree \(n\ge1\) factors into \(n\) linear factors over the complex numbers.

## Counting roots

The number of roots counted with multiplicity equals the degree, so a quintic always has five roots in some sense.

## Multiplicity

A repeated root contributes one factor per repetition, and those repetitions add up to the degree.

To check your understanding, state the factorisation, count with multiplicity and read a repeated factor.

The complex numbers are algebraically closed, and degree measures the full root count.
