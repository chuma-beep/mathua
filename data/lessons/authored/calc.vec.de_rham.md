# The de Rham cohomology

De Rham cohomology measures the closed forms that are not exact.

## Closed and exact forms

A form is closed when its exterior derivative is zero. It is exact when it is the derivative of another form.

Every exact form is closed because two derivatives give zero. The converse can fail. The failure is geometric, since a closed form that is not exact goes around a hole.

On the plane every closed form is exact, so \(x\,dx+y\,dy\) has the primitive \((x^{2}+y^{2})/2\).

## The cohomology groups

The \(k\)-th cohomology group is the quotient of closed \(k\)-forms by exact ones.

The zeroth group counts connected components, since a closed function is locally constant. A space with one component has a one-dimensional zeroth group, so the circle has Betti numbers 1 and 1.

The dimension is a topological invariant, so it survives any continuous deformation.

## The circle and the punctured plane

On the circle the one-form \(d\theta\) is closed and not exact, since \(\theta\) is not single valued.

The first cohomology group is one dimensional. The punctured plane has the same group, generated by \((x\,dy-y\,dx)/(x^{2}+y^{2})\).

Its integral around the unit circle is \(2\pi\), so no exact form can match it. Exact forms integrate to zero around closed loops.

The circle and the punctured plane are homotopy equivalent, which is why their groups agree.
