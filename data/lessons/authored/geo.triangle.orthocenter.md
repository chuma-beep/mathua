# The orthocenter

## Altitudes and the orthocenter

An altitude is a segment from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side or its extension. The three altitudes of a triangle meet at one point called the orthocenter.

In an acute triangle the orthocenter lies inside. In a right triangle it is the right-angle vertex. In an obtuse triangle it lies outside the triangle.

## Coordinates of the orthocenter

With coordinates, find two altitudes and intersect them. A horizontal base makes one altitude vertical, which simplifies the algebra. The intersection of the two altitude lines is the orthocenter.

Check the result by testing the third altitude. If the point does not lie on all three altitude lines, the intersection was computed from the wrong slopes.

## Right and obtuse cases

A right triangle has two sides that are already altitudes, so the orthocenter is the right-angle vertex. For an obtuse triangle, extend the sides before drawing the altitudes and expect the intersection to be outside.

The location of the orthocenter is a useful check. An inside answer for an obtuse triangle is a sign that a side or an altitude was drawn incorrectly.
