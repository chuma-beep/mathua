# Counterfactuals

A counterfactual asks what would have happened to a unit under a treatment it did not receive. It is a unit-level quantity, not an average.

## Potential outcomes

Each unit has one outcome under treatment and one under control. Only one is observed, which is the fundamental problem of causal inference.

## The individual effect

The individual effect is the difference of the two potential outcomes. It is never observed directly.

## Assumptions

Stable unit treatment value and consistency connect the observed outcome to the potential outcomes. Without them the notation has no empirical content.

To check your understanding, write both outcomes, subtract the outcomes and state SUTVA.

Counterfactuals are potential outcomes indexed by treatment.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller numbers and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
