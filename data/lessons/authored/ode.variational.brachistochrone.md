# The brachistochrone

The brachistochrone asks for the curve of fastest descent under gravity. The Euler-Lagrange equation of the travel-time functional yields a cycloid, and the problem is the historical origin of the calculus of variations.

## The functional

The travel time is the integral of arc length divided by speed. The speed follows from conservation of energy, so it is proportional to the square root of the vertical drop.

## The first integral

The integrand does not depend on the horizontal coordinate. The Beltrami identity therefore gives a first integral, which is a separable first-order equation for the slope.

## The solution

The separable equation is solved by a cycloid parametrised by a rolling angle. The curve starts vertical, bends, and reaches the lowest point after a finite horizontal distance.

To check your understanding, write the arc length, note the missing coordinate and parametrise the cycloid.

The brachistochrone shows that the fastest path is not the shortest, and the cycloid answer is the standard demonstration of the method.
