# The variational Euler-Lagrange equation

Stationarity of a functional under fixed endpoints gives the Euler-Lagrange equation. It is the differential equation every smooth extremal must satisfy, and its order equals the order of the Lagrangian's derivatives.

## Derivation

Differentiate the functional with respect to the variation parameter, then integrate the term containing the derivative of the perturbation by parts. The boundary term vanishes because the perturbation is zero at both endpoints.

## The equation

The remaining integral must vanish for every perturbation, so its coefficient is zero. That coefficient is the derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the position minus the total derivative of its derivative with respect to the slope.

## Special cases

If the Lagrangian does not depend on position, the slope derivative is constant. If it does not depend on the independent variable, the Beltrami identity gives a first integral and reduces the order by one.

To check your understanding, differentiate the functional, write the coefficient and remove the position dependence.

The Euler-Lagrange equation is the variational analogue of setting a derivative to zero, and its first integrals are the variational analogue of a stationary point.
