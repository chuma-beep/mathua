# The exponent of a group

The exponent is the least positive integer killing every element. It is the least common multiple of the element orders.

## Definition

The exponent \(e\) satisfies \(g^e=1\) for every \(g\), and divides \(|G|\) by Lagrange's theorem.

## Examples

The cyclic group \(C_n\) has exponent \(n\), and \(S_n\) has exponent the least common multiple of \(1,\dots,n\).

## Dihedral case

\(D_n\) has exponent the least common multiple of 2 and \(n\).

To check your understanding, take the lcm, compute the cyclic case and combine rotations.

The exponent is the universal order of the group.
