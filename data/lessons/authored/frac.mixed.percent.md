# Mixed numbers and percentages

A mixed number converts to a percent by first becoming an improper fraction. The percent then reports how many hundredths the value contains and values above 1 give percents above 100.

## The method

Write \(w\frac{a}{b}\) as \(\frac{wb + a}{b}\), multiply by 100 and divide. Equivalently, convert to a decimal and move the decimal point two places, which is the same multiplication. A mixed number always exceeds 1, so its percent always exceeds 100.

## A worked example

Convert \(2\frac{1}{2}\). The improper fraction is \(\frac{5}{2}\) and multiplying by 100 gives \(\frac{500}{2} = 250\), so the value is 250 percent. A second case is \(1\frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{4}\), which gives \(\frac{700}{4} = 175\) percent. A third is \(3\frac{1}{5} = \frac{16}{5}\), which is 320 percent.

## Checking the result

Write 175 percent over 100 and reduce: \(\frac{175}{100} = \frac{7}{4} = 1\frac{3}{4}\), which returns the mixed number. A useful second check takes the percent of a convenient whole: 175 percent of 4 is 7 and \(1\frac{3}{4} \times 4\) is also 7. A percent between 100 and 200 corresponds to a mixed number between 1 and 2.

The percent of a mixed number is above 100 and the conversion back must restore the mixed form.
