# Ceva's theorem

## The Ceva product

A cevian joins a vertex to a point on the opposite side. Ceva's theorem says three cevians are concurrent exactly when the product of three directed side ratios equals 1.

The ratios must be taken in a consistent direction around the triangle. Reversing one ratio changes the sign of the product and changes the conclusion.

## Solving for a ratio

If two ratios and the product are known, divide the product by the two known ratios. The result is the missing ratio. Keep the ratios in the same order as the theorem.

A product equal to 1 means the three cevians meet. A product different from 1 means they do not meet at a single point.

## Testing concurrency

To test a proposed concurrence, compute the three ratios and multiply them. The cevians are concurrent exactly when the product is 1. This is a numerical test, not just a visual one.

Check the direction of each ratio before multiplying. A sign error can turn a concurrent configuration into a non-concurrent one.

A final check is to restate the answer in the units and context of the original question. If the result is plausible and the units match, the calculation is ready.
