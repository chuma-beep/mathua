# The envelope of a modulated wave

When a fast wave is multiplied by a slow one, the product lies inside a pair of curves that touch it once per cycle; those curves form the envelope.

## Maximum and minimum of the envelope

For \(y = (3 + 2\sin t)\cos 10t\) the amplitude at time \(t\) is \(3 + 2\sin t\), which runs from
1 to 5. The wave therefore reaches 5 and -5 at its widest. At its narrowest it reaches 1 and -1.
The envelope proper is the pair \(y = \pm(3 + 2\sin t)\).

## The spread of a modulated wave

The distance between the upper and lower envelope is twice the amplitude. It is \(2(3 + 2\sin t)\)
, so 10 when \(\sin t = 1\), which happens at 90 degrees. It is 2 when \(\sin t = -1\), at 270
degrees. At \(t = 0\) the spread is 6. A signal whose spread changes this way carries its
information in the envelope, not in the rapid carrier.

## Sketching the envelope

Plot the two slow curves first, then fit the fast wave between them. Draw \(y = 3 + 2\sin t\) and
its negative, note that the carrier period is \(2\pi/10 \approx 0.628\), then let each crest touch
the upper curve. At \(t = 0\) a crest sits at 3 on the upper curve. Half a period later, at
\(t = 0.314\), a trough sits on the lower curve at about -3.618.

The envelope is the slow curve; the wave is the fast one.
