# Dividing by a fraction twice

A chain of two divisions inverts both divisors. The first division is performed on the dividend and the second is performed on that quotient.

## The method

Compute \(\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} \div \frac{e}{f}\) from left to right. Replace each division by multiplication by a reciprocal: \(\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} \times \frac{f}{e}\). Division is not associative, so the order of the divisors matters, although the product of reciprocals may be arranged in any order. The first divisor acts on the dividend and the second acts on the quotient, so the two roles are not interchangeable. For \(\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{3} \div \frac{1}{4}\) the result is 6.

## A worked quotient

Compute \(\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} \div \frac{5}{6}\). The first division gives \(\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{8}\). The second gives \(\frac{9}{8} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{54}{40} = \frac{27}{20}\). A second case is \(\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{10}{9} \div \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{9}{10} \times \frac{4}{3} = 1\).

## Checking the result

Multiply the quotient by the second divisor and then by the first. For the first case, \(\frac{27}{20} \times \frac{5}{6} = \frac{135}{120} = \frac{9}{8}\), then \(\frac{9}{8} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{18}{24} = \frac{3}{4}\), which is the dividend. The second case returns 1 times \(\frac{3}{4}\) times \(\frac{10}{9}\), which simplifies back to \(\frac{5}{6}\).

Each divisor is undone in reverse order and the dividend returns.
