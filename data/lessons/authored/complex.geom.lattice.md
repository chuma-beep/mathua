# The Gaussian lattice

The Gaussian integers form the square lattice, so counting lattice points is counting Gaussian integers in a region.

## Lattice points

An \(n\times n\) square of unit cells with corners on the lattice contains \(n^{2}\) cells and \((n+1)^{2}\) lattice points.

## Distances

The squared distance between two lattice points is the norm of their difference.

## Areas

A lattice parallelogram has integer area equal to the absolute determinant of its two edge vectors.

To check your understanding, count the cells, subtract the points and form the determinant.

Gaussian integers are lattice points, so their arithmetic answers geometric counting questions.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
