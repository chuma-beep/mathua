# Subtracting three fractions

A chain of two subtractions is performed in order or combined over one denominator. The first term is the minuend and each later term is removed from the running value.

## The method

Compute \(\frac{a}{b} - \frac{c}{d} - \frac{e}{f}\) from left to right. Subtract the second fraction from the first, then subtract the third from that result. Alternatively, write all three over a common denominator and subtract both numerators from the first. Subtraction is not associative, so the order must not be rearranged. With \(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{6}\) the remainder is \(\frac{1}{2}\).

## A worked difference

Compute \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{8}\). The quarter becomes \(\frac{2}{8}\), so the chain is \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{8}\), then \(\frac{5}{8} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}\). A second case is \(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4}\), which becomes \(\frac{10}{12} - \frac{4}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}\).

## Checking the result

Add the result back to both subtrahends: \(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{2}{8} + \frac{2}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{7}{8}\), which is the minuend. For the second case, \(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}\) combines to \(\frac{5}{6}\). A mismatch in either check points to the order of subtraction.

Two subtractions remove two parts and the remainder plus both parts restores the start.
