# The WKB approximation

The WKB method approximates solutions of a slowly varying oscillator by an amplitude and a rapidly accumulating phase. The phase is the integral of the local wave number, and the amplitude follows from conservation of a flux.

## The ansatz

The solution is written as an exponential of a phase divided by a small parameter. Expanding the phase in powers of the parameter turns the differential equation into a sequence of algebraic relations.

## Eikonal and transport

The leading order gives the square of the phase derivative as the local coefficient. The next order gives a transport equation whose solution is an amplitude proportional to the inverse square root of that derivative.

## Breakdown at turning points

Where the local coefficient vanishes the two phase branches meet and the amplitude diverges. The approximation fails there and must be replaced by a local Airy analysis.

To check your understanding, write the exponential ansatz, solve the eikonal equation and find where the coefficient vanishes.

WKB is the semiclassical approximation, and its turning points are where the classical picture runs out.
