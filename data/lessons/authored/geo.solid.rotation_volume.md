# Volumes of revolution

Rotating a region about an axis produces a solid whose volume can be computed by adding up thin discs. Each disc has area pi r squared and thickness dx.

## The disc method

A slice of thickness dx at distance x from the axis has radius r(x). Its volume is pi r(x) squared dx, and the solid volume is the integral.

## Constant radius

When the radius is constant over a height h, the integral gives pi r squared h, the volume of a cylinder.

## The washer method

A region between two curves produces washers. The area of a washer is pi times the difference of the squared outer and inner radii.

To check your understanding, take a thin slice, pull out the constant and subtract squared radii.

A solid of revolution is a stack of discs.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on.
