# Permutation matrices

A permutation matrix has exactly one 1 in each row and column. Multiplying by it permutes coordinates, and its determinant is the sign of the permutation.

## The permutation-matrix shape

Start with the identity and permute its rows. The result has one 1 in each row and column and zeros elsewhere. It is an orthogonal matrix, so its inverse is its transpose.

## Sign and inversions

The determinant of a permutation matrix is plus or minus 1. It is plus when the number of inversions is even and minus when the number is odd. An inversion is a pair of positions whose values are out of order.

## Acting on a vector

Multiplying a permutation matrix by a vector reorders the vector's entries. The new i-th entry is the entry at the position named by the permutation. The operation is a relabelling, not a linear combination.
