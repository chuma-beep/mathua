# Asymptotic equivalence

Two functions are asymptotically equivalent when their ratio tends to 1. The leading term controls the growth and the lower-order terms disappear from the ratio.

## The ratio limit

Write f and g and form the quotient f over g. If the quotient tends to 1, the functions are asymptotically equivalent. The lower-order terms cancel in the ratio even when they are present in both functions.

## Leading coefficients

When f and g have the same leading power, the ratio limit is the ratio of the leading coefficients. A coefficient ratio of 1 gives asymptotic equivalence. Any other ratio gives a constant different from 1.

## Comparing growth rates

A higher power in the numerator makes the ratio diverge. A higher power in the denominator makes it tend to zero. In neither case are the functions asymptotically equivalent.
