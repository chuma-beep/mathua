# Invariant subspaces

A subspace is invariant under a linear map when the map sends it into itself. Invariant subspaces are the pieces from which a map is assembled.

## Restriction

An invariant subspace lets the map be restricted to it. The restriction is again linear, and its matrix is a diagonal block of the full matrix.

## Eigenlines

A one-dimensional invariant subspace is spanned by an eigenvector. Invariant subspaces generalise eigenspaces to higher dimension.

## Irreducibility

A map is irreducible on a subspace when no proper nonzero invariant subspace exists. Over the complex numbers every map has an eigenline, so irreducible pieces are one-dimensional.

To check your understanding, restrict the map, identify an eigenvector and rule out proper pieces.

Finding invariant subspaces is the first step toward block diagonalisation.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
