# Digital roots and casting out nines

A digital root is the single digit left after repeatedly summing digits. It is the least positive residue modulo 9, which makes it a fast arithmetic check.

## Digit sums

Add the digits of a positive integer and repeat until one digit remains. The result is between 1 and 9. For a multiple of 9 the process ends at 9 rather than at 0.

## Casting out nines

Because 10 is congruent to 1 modulo 9, every decimal digit contributes its face value modulo 9. The digital root is therefore the least positive residue of the whole number modulo 9. This gives a quick check on a long sum.

## Sums and products

The digital root of a sum is the digital root of the sum of the digital roots. The same holds for a product. These rules let a learner check an arithmetic result without recomputing it.
