# Simplifying a complex fraction

A complex fraction has a fraction in its numerator, its denominator or both. It is a division written in stacked form, so it simplifies by multiplying by the reciprocal of the lower fraction.

## The method

Write the complex fraction as \(\frac{a/b}{c/d}\). Its value is \(\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc}\), provided \(c\) is nonzero. The lower fraction is a divisor, so it must be nonzero before the inversion. Invert only that fraction, then multiply and reduce. For \(\frac{1/2}{3/4}\) the value is \(\frac{2}{3}\). A numerator fraction of zero makes the value zero, while a denominator fraction of zero leaves it undefined.

## A worked quotient

Simplify \(\frac{3/4}{9/8}\). The lower fraction is \(\frac{9}{8}\), whose reciprocal is \(\frac{8}{9}\). Multiplying gives \(\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{8}{9} = \frac{24}{36} = \frac{2}{3}\). A second case is \(\frac{5/6}{10/9}\), which becomes \(\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{45}{60} = \frac{3}{4}\).

## Checking the result

Multiply the simplified value by the lower fraction: \(\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{9}{8} = \frac{18}{24} = \frac{3}{4}\), which is the upper fraction. For the second case, \(\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{10}{9} = \frac{30}{36} = \frac{5}{6}\). The check also confirms that the lower fraction was nonzero, since a zero there would have no reciprocal.

A complex fraction is a division with the sign hidden and one inversion removes the stack.
