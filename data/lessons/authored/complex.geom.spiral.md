# Spiral similarities

A spiral similarity multiplies by a complex constant, scaling by its modulus and rotating by its argument.

## The multiplier

The map \(z\mapsto \lambda z+\mu\) sends every segment to a similar segment, with scale \(|\lambda|\) and turn \(\arg\lambda\).

## Ratio and angle

For two segments \(ab\) and \(cd\), the multiplier is \((d-c)/(b-a)\). Its modulus is the length ratio and its argument is the turn.

## Composition

Composing two spiral similarities multiplies the scale factors and adds the angles.

To check your understanding, read the scale, form the quotient and multiply the scales.

One complex multiplier carries both the scale and the rotation of a similarity.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
