# Closed forms for first-order recurrences

A linear recurrence expresses each term from earlier terms. For a first-order recurrence with a constant multiplier, the closed form is a geometric sequence.

## Iterating

Repeated substitution expresses the nth term in terms of the first term and the multiplier raised to a power.

## The closed form

A recurrence of the form a(n+1) = r a(n) has solution a(n) = a(0) r to the n, which is a geometric sequence.

## Affine recurrences

Adding a constant produces a shifted geometric sequence. The fixed point of the recurrence reveals the shift.

To check your understanding, substitute repeatedly, identify the ratio and find the fixed point.

A closed form replaces iteration, so it can be evaluated at any index directly.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
