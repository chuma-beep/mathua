# The parabolic maximum principle

A solution of the heat equation cannot exceed its initial and boundary values. The maximum principle converts that observation into a comparison tool and a short uniqueness proof.

## The interior maximum

At an interior maximum in space and time, the time derivative is non-negative and the second spatial derivative is non-positive. The heat equation sets them equal, so a non-constant solution cannot attain an interior maximum.

## Boundary data

The maximum is therefore attained on the parabolic boundary, which is the initial line together with the lateral boundary. The value there is read from the data rather than computed from the equation.

## Comparison principle

If two solutions are ordered on the parabolic boundary then they stay ordered inside. Applying that to a solution and to a constant gives uniqueness and an explicit bound on the solution.

To check your understanding, write the sign conditions at an interior maximum, identify the parabolic boundary and compare two ordered solutions.

The maximum principle is the parabolic analogue of an energy estimate, and it gives pointwise bounds that an energy method cannot.
