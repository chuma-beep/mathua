# Common tangents

Two circles can share tangent lines. External tangents touch both circles on the same side, while internal tangents cross the segment joining the centres.

## External tangents

Two disjoint circles always have two external tangents. They meet at the external homothety centre.

## Internal tangents

Internal tangents exist only when the circles are far enough apart: the centre distance must exceed the sum of the radii. Touching circles have one internal tangent.

## Counting the tangents

Disjoint circles have four common tangents, externally tangent circles have three, intersecting circles have two, and one circle inside another has none.

To check your understanding, count the outside lines, compare distance and radii and add both families.

The centre distance decides how many internal tangents exist.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
