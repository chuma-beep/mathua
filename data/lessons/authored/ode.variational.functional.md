# Functionals and variations

A functional assigns a number to a whole curve, and a variation is a one-parameter family of curves through a candidate. The first variation is the derivative of the functional at that candidate, and stationarity is the condition for an extremal.

## The functional

The functional integrates a Lagrangian that depends on position, slope and the independent variable. Changing the curve changes the integral, so the functional is a function of a function rather than of a number.

## The variation

A variation adds a small multiple of an admissible perturbation to the candidate. The perturbation vanishes at the endpoints, which keeps the comparison inside the class of curves with the prescribed boundary values.

## Stationarity

The derivative of the functional with respect to the variation parameter must vanish at zero for every admissible perturbation. That condition is what produces the Euler-Lagrange equation in the next concept.

To check your understanding, write the integral, choose an admissible perturbation and differentiate with respect to the parameter.

A functional is the object that makes the calculus of variations a calculus, and the variation is its derivative.
