# Green's identities for the Laplacian

Green's identities are integration by parts for the Laplacian. They move a derivative from one factor to the other and produce the boundary terms that carry flux.

## The divergence theorem

The identities all follow from the divergence theorem applied to a vector field built from the two functions. The boundary integral is the flux of that field through the normal.

## The first identity

The first identity relates the integral of one function times the Laplacian of another to the integral of the gradients and a boundary flux. It is the weak form used to define a harmonic function.

## The second identity

The second identity is the antisymmetric difference of the first identity applied in both orders. The Laplacian terms stay and the boundary terms carry the normal derivatives, which is why it produces the jump conditions across a surface.

To check your understanding, build the vector field, integrate one Laplacian by parts and subtract the two orderings.

The identities are the reason a boundary-value problem can be rewritten as an integral equation, and every Green's function construction begins with them.
