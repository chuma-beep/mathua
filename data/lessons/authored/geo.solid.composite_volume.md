# Composite solids

A composite solid is built from pieces whose volumes add. Overlaps must be subtracted, and the pieces may be prisms, pyramids, cylinders or spheres.

## Adding the pieces

Split the solid into non-overlapping pieces, compute each volume and add them. The split is a modelling choice, not a property of the solid.

## Subtracting overlaps

When pieces overlap, the shared region is counted twice. Subtract each overlap once, and take care when three pieces meet.

## Choosing a split

A good split follows the faces of the solid. Pieces that share a flat face are usually the easiest to measure.

To check your understanding, split the solid, find the overlap and follow flat faces.

Composite volumes add, provided the pieces are disjoint.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
