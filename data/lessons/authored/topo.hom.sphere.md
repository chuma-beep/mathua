# Homology of spheres

The homology of the \(n\)-sphere is concentrated in degrees zero and \(n\). Every intermediate group vanishes, which makes spheres the simplest nontrivial examples.

## The two generators

The \(n\)-sphere has \(H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}\) and \(H_n \cong \mathbb{Z}\), with all other reduced groups zero. The total Betti number is two.

## Vanishing degrees

Between degrees zero and \(n\) the homology vanishes. For the \(n\)-sphere there are \(n - 1\) such intermediate degrees.

## Euler characteristic

The Euler characteristic of \(S^n\) is \(1 + (-1)^n\). It is two for even spheres and zero for odd spheres.

To check your understanding, count the nonzero groups, count the interior degrees and evaluate one plus the sign.

A sphere has homology only at its two ends.
