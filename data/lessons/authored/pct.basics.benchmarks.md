# Benchmark percentages

## Ten Percent as a Benchmark

Ten percent is the easiest benchmark because dividing by ten only moves the decimal point. Ten percent of 240 is 24. Build other friendly percents from that one value by multiplying or adding.

Thirty percent is three copies of ten percent, so \\(3 \\times 24 = 72\\). Fifteen percent is ten percent plus half of ten percent, which gives \\(24 + 12 = 36\\). Practise these two moves until they need no written work.

## Fifty Percent and One Quarter

Fifty percent is half, so fifty percent of 240 is 120. One quarter is half of a half, so 25 percent of 240 is 60. Seventy-five percent is three quarters, which gives 180.

These benchmarks combine well. A discount of 75 percent leaves 25 percent, so a 240 dollar item sells for 60. Finding the complement is often quicker than finding the part, especially when the percent is close to one hundred.

## One Percent of a Number

One percent moves the decimal point two places, so one percent of 240 is 2.4. From there any small whole percent is a short multiplication. Seven percent of 240 is \\(7 \\times 2.4 = 16.8\\).

Add benchmarks to reach awkward rates. Seventeen percent is ten percent plus seven percent, giving \\(24 + 16.8 = 40.8\\). Each piece stays small enough to check by eye. The sum is easy to audit against a rough estimate.
