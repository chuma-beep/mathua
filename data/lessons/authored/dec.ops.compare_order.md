# Ordering decimals

## Comparing Decimals Digit by Digit

To compare two decimals, line up their decimal points and compare place by place from the left. The first place where the digits differ decides the comparison. Compare 0.6 and 0.58. The tenths are 6 and 5, so 0.6 is greater.

Padding with zeros makes the columns visible without changing any value. Write 0.6 as 0.60, then compare 60 hundredths with 58 hundredths. A longer decimal is not automatically larger, since 0.58 is less than 0.6 even though it has more digits.

## Ordering a List of Decimals

To order a list, sort by the digits in the highest place first, then break ties with the next place. Take 0.7, 0.07 and 0.75. The tenths are 7, 0 and 7, so 0.07 is smallest. Between 0.7 and 0.75, the hundredths are 0 and 5, giving 0.7 then 0.75.

The ordered list is 0.07, 0.7 and 0.75. Check each neighboring pair, because a single misplaced value usually breaks one of them.

## Decimals Below Zero

Negative decimals follow the same rule with one reversal: the value farther from zero is smaller. Compare -0.3 and -0.8. The point -0.8 is farther left, so it is the smaller number.

Order -0.8, -0.3 and 0.05 by placing them on the line. The two negatives sit left of zero with -0.8 leftmost. The value 0.05 sits just right of zero. A negative decimal is always less than a positive one.
