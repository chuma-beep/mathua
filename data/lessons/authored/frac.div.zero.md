# Why division by zero is excluded

Division by zero has no quotient, so a fraction with denominator zero is not a number. The exclusion applies to fractions and to division written with the division sign alike.

## The method

Before dividing, check that the divisor is not zero. A divisor of zero makes the problem ask for a number \(q\) with \(0 \times q = 5\) and no such number exists. The fraction \(\frac{0}{0}\) is worse, because every number \(q\) satisfies \(0 \times q = 0\), so the answer is not unique.

## A worked quotient

Watch the divisor shrink. Five divided by \(\frac{1}{100}\) is 500 and five divided by \(\frac{1}{1000}\) is 5000. Each smaller divisor produces a larger quotient and the growth continues without bound. At a divisor of exactly zero no finite quotient appears, so the pattern has no endpoint.

## Checking the result

Multiply a proposed quotient by zero and compare with the dividend. Any number times zero is zero, which never equals 5, so no proposed quotient survives the check. For \(\frac{0}{0}\), the same check returns zero for every proposal, so it cannot choose among them.

The check fails for every candidate, which is why the quotient is undefined rather than merely unknown.
