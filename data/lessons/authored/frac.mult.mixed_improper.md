# Multiplying mixed numbers using improper fractions

Mixed numbers are multiplied by first writing each one as an improper fraction. Multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts separately gives the wrong answer.

## The method

Convert \(w\frac{a}{b}\) to \(\frac{wb + a}{b}\), then multiply the improper fractions as usual. The improper numerator counts every part of the wholes, so the whole part is no longer separate. Cancel common factors before multiplying and convert the product back to a mixed number if the question asks for that form. The conversion keeps the value and makes the numerator a single number. For \(1\frac{1}{2}\) the improper form is \(\frac{3}{2}\).

## A worked product

Compute \(2\frac{1}{2} \times 1\frac{1}{3}\). The factors become \(\frac{5}{2}\) and \(\frac{4}{3}\), whose product is \(\frac{20}{6} = \frac{10}{3} = 3\frac{1}{3}\). An estimate confirms the size, since 2.5 times 1.333 is about 3.33. A second case is \(1\frac{3}{4} \times 2\frac{2}{5} = \frac{7}{4} \times \frac{12}{5} = \frac{84}{20} = \frac{21}{5} = 4\frac{1}{5}\).

## Checking the result

Divide the product by the first factor: \(\frac{10}{3} \div \frac{5}{2} = \frac{10}{3} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{20}{15} = \frac{4}{3}\), which is \(1\frac{1}{3}\), the second factor. The check returns the other factor exactly, so the conversion and the multiplication were both correct.

Improper form is the working form and the mixed form is the answer form.
