# Solving equations that are proportions

## Cross Multiplication

A proportion states that two ratios are equal. Cross multiplication turns it into a linear equation by multiplying each numerator by the opposite denominator. For \\(\\frac{x}{4} = \\frac{6}{8}\\), the products are \\(8x\\) and 24, so \\(8x = 24\\) and \\(x = 3\\).

The method works because multiplying both sides by both denominators clears every fraction at once. Write the cross products as one equation before simplifying, so the structure stays visible.

## The Variable in the Denominator

When the variable sits in a denominator, cross multiplication still applies. Take \\(\\frac{5}{x} = \\frac{10}{6}\\). The cross products are 30 and \\(10x\\), so \\(10x = 30\\) and \\(x = 3\\).

One restriction appears here: a denominator may never be zero, so \\(x = 0\\) is excluded before any solving begins. If the algebra produces zero for such a proportion, the equation has no valid solution rather than a surprising one.

## Proportions With a Sum

A numerator may hold an expression rather than a single letter. For \\(\\frac{x + 2}{5} = \\frac{6}{3}\\), cross multiplication gives \\(3(x + 2) = 30\\).

Divide both sides by 3 to get \\(x + 2 = 10\\), then \\(x = 8\\). Divide before distributing when one side divides evenly, because the smaller numbers are easier to check. Substituting 8 recovers the ratio \\(\\frac{10}{5}\\), which equals 2 on both sides.
