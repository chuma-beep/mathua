# Isometries and their invariants

## Distance preservation

An isometry preserves distance. Translations, rotations and reflections are isometries. A dilation is not an isometry because it changes lengths.

To test whether a map is an isometry, compare the distance between two points with the distance between their images. Equal distances for all pairs are required.

## Orientation

Reflections reverse orientation, while translations and rotations preserve it. Orientation is a sign, often positive for preserved and negative for reversed.

A composition of two reflections preserves orientation. A composition of one reflection and one rotation reverses it. The parity of the number of reflections decides the sign.

## Classifying an isometry

An isometry of the plane is a translation, a rotation, a reflection or a glide reflection. Fixed points and orientation help identify which one.

A translation has no fixed points and preserves orientation. A rotation has one fixed point and preserves orientation. A reflection has a line of fixed points and reverses orientation.
