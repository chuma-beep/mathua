# Quasi-isometries

A quasi-isometry distorts distances by a bounded additive and multiplicative error. It is the right equivalence for large-scale geometry, and it preserves growth degree.

## The distortion constants

A quasi-isometry satisfies \(d(x,y)/C - D \le d(fx,fy) \le C d(x,y) + D\). The multiplicative constant \(C\) and the additive constant \(D\) are the two parameters.

## Abelian dimensions

The groups \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) and \(\mathbb{Z}^m\) are quasi-isometric exactly when \(n = m\). Growth degree distinguishes them.

## Comparing metrics

The word metric and the Euclidean metric on \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) are quasi-isometric. Their distance ratio is bounded above and below by constants independent of the point.

To check your understanding, count the constants, compare the dimensions and bound the ratio.

Quasi-isometry forgets bounded detail and keeps dimension.
