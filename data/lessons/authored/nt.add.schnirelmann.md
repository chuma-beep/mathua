# Schnirelmann density

Schnirelmann density is a worst-case density for sets of integers. It behaves well under sumsets and gives elementary additive bases.

## The definition

The density of a set is the infimum over n of the count up to n divided by n. It is smaller than the natural density when the set has gaps early.

## The sumset bound

If a set contains zero and has positive density, then a sum of enough copies covers all positive integers. The required number of copies is bounded by the reciprocal of the density.

## Applications

Schnirelmann used the density to prove that every integer is a sum of a bounded number of primes. The bound was large, but the method was elementary.

To check your understanding, count up to n, require zero and sum primes.

Schnirelmann density is a minimum rather than a limit, and that minimum is what makes the sumset bound uniform.
