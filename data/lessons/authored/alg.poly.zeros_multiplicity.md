# Zeros and multiplicity

The multiplicity of a zero is the exponent of its factor. It controls how the graph meets the axis at that zero.

## Multiplicity from factors

If \((x-r)^m\) is a factor, then \(r\) is a zero of multiplicity \(m\). A factor with exponent 1 gives a simple zero.

An even multiplicity makes the graph touch the axis and turn around. An odd multiplicity makes it cross.

## Degree from multiplicity

The degree of a factored polynomial is the sum of its multiplicities. A factor \((x-2)^3\) contributes 3 to the degree.

Multiplying by a constant does not change the degree. Only the exponents of the variable factors matter.

## Distinct zeros

Distinct zeros are the different values of \(r\), not the exponents. A polynomial with factors \((x-1)^2(x+2)^3\) has two distinct zeros.

The graph can cross or touch the axis at each distinct zero, but the number of distinct zeros counts each location once.

Multiplicity also affects the shape near the zero: a higher odd multiplicity looks flatter before crossing, and a higher even multiplicity looks flatter before turning.
