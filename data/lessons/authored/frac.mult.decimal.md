# Multiplying a fraction by a decimal

A fraction and a decimal multiply once both are written in one form. The fraction route is exact and the decimal route is convenient when the fraction terminates.

## The method

Convert the decimal to a fraction by writing it over a power of ten and reducing or convert the fraction to a decimal by dividing. Multiply in the chosen form. A decimal with two places becomes a fraction over 100, so 0.25 is \(\frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}\). A fraction that terminates as a decimal is the easier of the two routes.

## A worked product

Compute \(\frac{3}{5} \times 0.25\). The decimal is \(\frac{1}{4}\), so the product is \(\frac{3}{20}\). In decimals the same work is \(0.6 \times 0.25 = 0.15\) and \(\frac{3}{20} = 0.15\). A second case is \(0.8 \times \frac{5}{6}\), where 0.8 is \(\frac{4}{5}\) and the product is \(\frac{20}{30} = \frac{2}{3}\), about 0.6667.

## Checking the result

Divide the product by the decimal factor: \(0.15 \div 0.25 = 0.6\), which is \(\frac{3}{5}\). In fractions, \(\frac{3}{20} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{20} \times 4 = \frac{12}{20} = \frac{3}{5}\). The decimal places also agree, since 0.15 has two places and the inputs have one and two.

One form for both factors is the method and the division check restores the other factor.
