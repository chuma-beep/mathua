# The open mapping theorem

The open mapping theorem says a surjective bounded operator between Banach spaces sends open sets to open sets. It is the source of bounded inverses.

## Open maps

A map is open when the image of every open set is open. The theorem applies to a bounded linear \(T : X \to Y\) that is onto, with \(X\) and \(Y\) Banach. Multiplication by the matrix with diagonal entries 2 and 3 on \(\mathbb{R}^2\) is open, and it maps the unit disc onto an ellipse.

## Surjective operators

Surjectivity is not decorative. The identity from \((C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_\infty)\) to \((C[0,1], \|\cdot\|_1)\) is bounded and bijective, yet it is not open, because the target space with the \(L^1\) norm is not complete. The theorem does not apply there.

## Bounded inverses

A bijective bounded operator between Banach spaces has a bounded inverse. The openness gives a radius \(r\) such that the image of the unit ball contains the ball of radius \(r\). The inverse is then bounded by \(1/r\).

The theorem converts surjectivity into a statement about open sets, and bounded inversion follows from that statement.
