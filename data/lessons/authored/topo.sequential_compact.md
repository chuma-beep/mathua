# Sequential compactness

A space is sequentially compact when every sequence has a convergent subsequence. In metric spaces this is equivalent to compactness.

## Subsequences

A subsequence chooses infinitely many terms of a sequence while keeping their order. A space is sequentially compact when every sequence has a subsequence that converges inside the space. A sequence that cycles through finitely many values has one subsequential limit per value.

## Metric spaces

For metric spaces, sequential compactness is equivalent to compactness. A closed bounded interval of real numbers is therefore sequentially compact. An open interval is not, because a sequence can approach a missing endpoint.

## Counterexamples

The open interval from zero to one is bounded but not closed, and the sequence one over n has no subsequence converging inside it. This shows why both closedness and boundedness matter in the Heine-Borel theorem.
