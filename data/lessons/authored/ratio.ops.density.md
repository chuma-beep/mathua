# Density as a rate

## Mass From Density and Volume

Density is a rate that compares mass with volume, usually in grams per cubic centimetre. Multiply density by volume to get mass. Aluminium has a density of 2.7 grams per cubic centimetre, so 40 cubic centimetres weigh \\(2.7 \\times 40 = 108\\) grams.

Carry the units through the multiplication. Grams per cubic centimetre times cubic centimetres leaves grams. A result in grams is the expected kind of answer for a mass question.

## Volume From Mass and Density

Volume is mass divided by density. A piece of the same aluminium with a mass of 540 grams occupies \\(540 \\div 2.7 = 200\\) cubic centimetres.

The divisor must be the density, not the mass. Dividing density by mass gives a tiny number whose units are inverted. Only a unit check exposes that mistake. Write the units beside each number before dividing.

## Density From Mass and Volume

Density comes from dividing mass by volume. A block of mass 810 grams and volume 300 cubic centimetres has density \\(810 \\div 300 = 2.7\\) grams per cubic centimetre, which identifies the material as aluminium.

Compare the result with a table of known densities to judge plausibility. A density near 1 belongs to water. A metal near 2.7 is light, so an answer of 27 suggests the decimal point has shifted.
