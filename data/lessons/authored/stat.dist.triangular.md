# The triangular distribution

A triangular distribution is bounded on an interval with a single peak inside it, described by a minimum, a maximum and a mode.

## Three parameters

Write the parameters as a for the minimum, b for the maximum and c for the mode. The density rises linearly from a to c and falls linearly from c to b, reaching \(2/(b-a)\) at the mode. With a of 2, b of 8 and c of 5, the density at the peak is \(2/6 = 1/3\), while the density at 4 is \(2(4-2)/((5-2)(8-2)) = 4/18 = 2/9\).

## The mean

The mean is \((a+b+c)/3\), a blend that gives the mode a third of the weight. For a of 2, b of 8 and c of 5 the mean is \(15/3 = 5\), equal to the mode because 5 is the midpoint of 2 and 8. Move the mode to 4 and the mean becomes \(14/3\), about 4.67, drawn toward the shorter side of the interval, where the density is higher.

## A worked triangle

A task takes between 4 and 10 minutes, most often 5. The mean is \(19/3\), about 6.33 minutes, which is below the interval midpoint of 7 because the mode sits near the low end. The variance is \((a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-ac-bc)/18 = (16+100+25-40-20-50)/18 = 31/18\), about 1.72, so the standard deviation is about 1.31 minutes.

The distribution is popular in project planning because three numbers that a practitioner can actually supply are enough to describe it.
