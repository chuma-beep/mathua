# Spherical harmonics

On the sphere the eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator are the spherical harmonics. Their degree and order index a finite-dimensional space whose size fixes how many independent patterns share one eigenvalue.

## Separation on the sphere

The spherical Laplacian separates into a polar equation and an azimuthal equation. The azimuthal equation is periodic, so its separation constant is an integer.

## Degree and order

The polar equation becomes Legendre's equation after a change of variable. Its regular solutions exist only when the separation constant has the form of a degree times the degree plus one, and the degree bounds the order.

## Degeneracy

For a fixed degree the order runs over a finite symmetric range. The number of independent harmonics of that degree is therefore fixed, and it grows linearly with the degree.

To check your understanding, write the spherical Laplacian, transform the polar equation and count the allowed orders.

The degeneracy count explains why a spherically symmetric problem produces multiplets rather than a single mode, a fact that reappears in the quantum theory of angular momentum.
