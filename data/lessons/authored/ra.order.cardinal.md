# Cardinal arithmetic

Two sets have the same cardinal when a bijection joins them. Cardinal arithmetic studies the operations of disjoint union and Cartesian product on these sizes.

## Cardinal numbers

The cardinal of a finite set is its number of elements. The cardinal of the natural numbers is \(\aleph_0\), and the evens also have cardinal \(\aleph_0\) because \(n \mapsto 2n\) is a bijection. A set is countable exactly when it is finite or has cardinal \(\aleph_0\).

## Sums and products

The sum \(|A| + |B|\) is the cardinal of the disjoint union. The product \(|A| \cdot |B|\) is the cardinal of \(A \times B\). For infinite cardinals both operations collapse to the larger factor: \(\aleph_0 + \aleph_0 = \aleph_0\), and \(\aleph_0 \cdot \aleph_0 = \aleph_0\). The grid of pairs of naturals is countable by diagonals, so the product of two countable sets is countable.

## Counting the continuum

The real numbers have cardinal \(2^{\aleph_0}\), the cardinal of the power set of the naturals. The interval \((0,1)\) alone has this cardinal, since a tangent substitution bijects it with the line. Addition and multiplication fix it: \(2^{\aleph_0} + 2^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}\), and the plane has the same cardinal as the line. Whether \(2^{\aleph_0}\) is \(\aleph_1\) is independent of the usual axioms.

Cardinal arithmetic counts without ordering, and its laws show that infinity absorbs the operations that enlarge finite sets.
