# Volumes by known cross sections

## Slicing a solid

A solid is sliced by planes perpendicular to an axis. Each slice has a known area \(A(x)\). A block of length 3 with square sections of side 2 has area 4 at every slice, so its volume is 12. Summing thin slabs and taking the limit turns that count into an integral.

## The volume integral

The volume is \(V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx\). For a solid on the interval from 0 to 2 with square sections of side \(x\), the area is \(x^2\), so \(V = \int_0^2 x^2 dx = 8/3 \approx 2.667\). The unit check is a length cubed, here \(2^3/3\).

## Square cross sections

A square-section solid on the region between \(y = x\) and \(y = x^2\) has side \(x - x^2\). The volume is \(\int_0^1 (x-x^2)^2 dx = 1/3 - 1/2 + 1/5 = 1/30 \approx 0.0333\). The small value is right, since the widest slice has side 0.25 at \(x = 1/2\).

A complete answer names the relation, substitutes the data and states the result in the language of the problem. When a second method is available, compare the two results before moving on.
