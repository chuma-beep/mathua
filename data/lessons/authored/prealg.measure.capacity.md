# Converting units of capacity

## Cups, Pints, and Quarts

Capacity units in the customary system step by twos. One pint holds 2 cups. One quart holds 2 pints, which is 4 cups. Multiply when converting to a smaller unit. Divide when converting to a larger one.

Six cups become 3 pints, because \\(6 \\div 2 = 3\\). The same six cups are 1.5 quarts, because \\(6 \\div 4 = 1.5\\). Naming the conversion factor before dividing keeps the direction straight.

## Quarts and Gallons

One gallon holds 4 quarts. A container of 10 quarts therefore holds \\(10 \\div 4 = 2.5\\) gallons. Going the other way, 3 gallons become \\(3 \\times 4 = 12\\) quarts.

These factors are exact, so a conversion never needs rounding unless the measurement itself was rounded. Keep the decimal in the answer, since 2.5 gallons is more precise than 2 gallons or 3 gallons.

## Converting a Capacity

A two-unit measurement converts in stages. Three gallons and 2 quarts first becomes quarts: 3 gallons is 12 quarts. Adding 2 gives 14 quarts. In cups that is \\(14 \\times 4 = 56\\) cups.

Choose one unit as the target and convert each part into it before adding. Mixing the order, such as adding gallons to cups, produces a number with no meaning even when the arithmetic is correct.
