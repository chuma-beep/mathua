# Rank of a quadratic form

A quadratic form is a homogeneous polynomial of degree two. Over fields away from characteristic two, its rank equals the rank of the associated symmetric bilinear form.

## Polar form

The polar form recovers a symmetric bilinear form from a quadratic form. The factor of one half appears, so characteristic two needs separate treatment.

## Diagonalisation

A quadratic form can be diagonalised by completing squares. The number of nonzero diagonal entries is its rank.

## Zero set

The rank controls the size of the zero set. A form of full rank has a nondegenerate quadric, while a low-rank form has a cone of zeros.

To check your understanding, recover the bilinear form, complete squares and relate rank to the quadric.

Completing squares is the coordinate proof that every quadratic form has a diagonal normal form.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
