# The reciprocal rule

The reciprocal rule differentiates \(1/g\) without expanding a quotient. It is the quotient rule with a constant numerator, and it is the fastest route for negative powers.

## The formula

For \(g(x)=x^{n}\), the derivative of \(1/g\) is \(-n/x^{n+1}\). The coefficient is the negative of the exponent and the power rises by one.

## At a point

At \(x=a\) with \(g(x)=x\), the slope of \(1/g\) is \(-1/a^{2}\). Squaring the point gives the magnitude of the denominator.

## The quotient link

Writing \(1/g\) as \(1/g\) and applying the quotient rule gives \(-g'/g^{2}\). The numerator is the negative derivative of the denominator and the denominator is squared.

To check your understanding, differentiate a negative power, evaluate the denominator and apply the quotient rule.
