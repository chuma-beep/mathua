# Hamilton's equations

Hamilton's equations are the Euler-Lagrange equations rewritten in phase space. They are first order and symmetric, and the Hamiltonian is conserved whenever the Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence.

## From Lagrangian to Hamiltonian

The momentum is defined as the velocity derivative of the Lagrangian. The Hamiltonian is the Legendre transform in the velocities, and the Euler-Lagrange equation becomes two first-order equations.

## The equations

The position derivative is the momentum derivative of the Hamiltonian and the momentum derivative is minus the position derivative. The antisymmetric sign pattern is what distinguishes a Hamiltonian system from a gradient flow.

## Conservation

The total time derivative of the Hamiltonian equals its explicit time derivative. An autonomous Lagrangian therefore gives a conserved Hamiltonian, which is the energy for a mechanical system.

To check your understanding, define the momentum, write both equations and differentiate the Hamiltonian.

Hamilton's equations double the dimension of the state but halve the order, and the symmetry of the result is the reason the theory is geometrically natural.
