# Multiplication as scaling

Multiplying by a fraction rescales a quantity rather than merely counting it. The multiplier says how the size changes: a value below 1 shrinks the quantity, a value equal to 1 preserves it and a value above 1 enlarges it.

## The method

Compare the multiplier with 1 before computing. For a positive quantity \(q\) and a fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\), the product \(\frac{a}{b}q\) is smaller than \(q\) when \(a < b\), equal when \(a = b\) and larger when \(a > b\). The comparison uses only the fraction, not the quantity.

## A worked product

Start from 12. Multiplying by \(\frac{3}{4}\) gives 9, which is smaller, because the numerator 3 is less than the denominator 4. Multiplying by \(\frac{5}{4}\) gives 15, which is larger. Multiplying by \(\frac{4}{4}\) leaves 12 unchanged. The three results 9, 12 and 15 show the three cases on one number.

## Checking the result

Divide each product by the starting value. The ratios are \(\frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}\), \(\frac{15}{12} = \frac{5}{4}\) and \(\frac{12}{12} = 1\), each matching its multiplier. The direction of each change is confirmed by the ratio and the ratio is the scaling factor by definition.

A product can be predicted from the multiplier alone, before any arithmetic.
