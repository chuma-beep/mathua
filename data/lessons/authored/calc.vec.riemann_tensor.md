# The Riemann curvature tensor

The Riemann tensor measures the failure of second covariant derivatives to commute.

## The commutator of covariant derivatives

Two covariant derivatives in opposite orders differ by a term involving the tensor.

The difference is linear in the field and in the directions, so it is a tensor. In flat space the commutator vanishes at every point.

For the plane in polar coordinates the commutator applied to \(\partial_\phi\) gives 0, even though the symbols are nonzero.

## The tensor in components

The components use derivatives of the symbols and products of symbols.

The pattern is a difference of derivatives plus a quadratic correction. The tensor is antisymmetric in its first two indices and its last two.

On the unit sphere the component with two \(\theta\) and two \(\phi\) indices is \(\sin^{2}\theta\), which at the equator is 1.

## A worked plane and sphere

The plane has nonzero symbols in polar coordinates but a vanishing tensor, since the derivative terms cancel the quadratic ones.

On the sphere the sectional curvature is 1 at the equator. The scalar curvature is twice that, so 2.

For a sphere of radius \(a\) the curvature falls as \(1/a^{2}\). These numbers record curvature without an embedding.
