# Cages

A cage is the smallest regular graph of a given degree and girth. Cages are the extremal witnesses for the Moore bound.

## The Moore bound

A graph of minimum degree \(r\) and girth \(g\) has at least \(1+r+r(r-1)+\cdots+r(r-1)^{(g-3)/2}\) vertices when \(g\) is odd, and one fewer term when \(g\) is even. The bound counts the breadth-first tree around a vertex.

## Known cages

The Petersen graph is the \((3,5)\)-cage with ten vertices, the Heawood graph is the \((3,6)\)-cage with fourteen, and the McGee graph is the \((3,7)\)-cage with twenty-four. The \((4,5)\)-cage is the Robertson graph with nineteen vertices.

## Counting edges

A cage is \(r\)-regular, so it has \(vr/2\) edges for \(v\) vertices. The edge count is always an integer, which rules out a claimed cage of odd order and odd degree.

To check your understanding, sum the levels of the tree, name the Petersen cage and multiply order by degree.

The Moore bound is a counting argument, and a cage is the graph that meets or nearly meets it.
