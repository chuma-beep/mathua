# Geodesics

A geodesic is a curve that locally minimises length. In the plane the geodesics are straight lines, and on a sphere they are great circles.

## Shortest path in the plane

The straight-line distance between two points is the square root of the sum of the squared coordinate differences. A Pythagorean displacement gives an integer length. Any other path is longer.

## A scaled metric

For the metric ds squared equal to a squared dx squared plus b squared dy squared, coordinate directions have lengths a and b. The diagonal segment has length the square root of a squared plus b squared. The metric determines the geodesic length.

## The geodesic equation

The geodesic equation is a second-order differential equation. Its solutions are the locally length-minimising curves. In a flat metric the equation reduces to straight lines.
