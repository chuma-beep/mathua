# Adjoint maps

The adjoint of a linear map is defined by moving the map to the other side of an inner product. In coordinates it is the conjugate transpose.

## Definition

The adjoint T star satisfies the identity pairing Tx with y equals pairing x with T star y. Existence follows from the Riesz representation theorem.

## Matrix

With orthonormal bases the matrix of the adjoint is the conjugate transpose. Over the reals it is the transpose.

## Kernels

The kernel of the adjoint is the orthogonal complement of the image. This identity explains the four fundamental subspaces.

To check your understanding, move the map across, take the conjugate transpose and complement the image.

Adjoints convert questions about images into questions about kernels and back.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
