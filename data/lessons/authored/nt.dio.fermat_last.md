# Fermat's last theorem

Fermat's last theorem says that no positive cube or higher power is a sum of two like powers. The exponent two is the sole exception.

## The statement

For every integer n greater than two, the equation x to the n plus y to the n equals z to the n has no solution in positive integers.

## The exponent two

Pythagorean triples solve the equation when n equals two. Every primitive triple comes from a pair of coprime integers of opposite parity.

## The proof

Wiles proved the theorem through the modularity of elliptic curves. The proof is hundreds of pages and uses almost none of elementary number theory.

To check your understanding, fix an exponent above two, generate a triple and name modularity.

Fermat's last theorem is the statement that the Pythagorean equation is special to exponent two, and the proof shows why that exponent is alone.
