# Branching processes

A branching process follows the size of a population in which each individual produces a random number of offspring. The offspring distribution determines the mean growth and the probability of extinction.

## Offspring distribution

Let the offspring counts have probabilities p0, p1 and p2. The mean offspring count is p1 plus twice p2. This mean controls whether the expected population grows, stays constant or shrinks.

## Extinction probability

The extinction probability is the smallest fixed point of the generating function in the unit interval. A mean of at most one forces extinction with probability one, apart from degenerate cases. A mean above one can leave a positive survival probability.

## Generation by generation

The probability of extinction by generation n is obtained by iterating the generating function from zero. The first step gives p0. The second step includes one individual producing one or two offspring, each of which must then die out.
