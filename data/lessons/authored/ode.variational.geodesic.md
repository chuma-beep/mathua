# Geodesic equations

A geodesic is a curve of stationary length, and its equation follows from the Euler-Lagrange equation of the length functional. On a surface the geodesic equation expresses the acceleration in the coordinate basis.

## Length functional

The length of a curve is the integral of the square root of the metric applied to the tangent. Extremising length is equivalent to extremising energy, which removes the square root and simplifies the equation.

## The equation

The Euler-Lagrange equation of the energy functional produces an acceleration equal to minus a quadratic form in the velocities. The coefficients of that form are the Christoffel symbols.

## Christoffel symbols

The symbols are built from the metric and its first derivatives. They encode how the coordinate basis turns as the point moves, and they vanish in Cartesian coordinates on flat space.

To check your understanding, write the length integral, apply Euler-Lagrange and differentiate the metric.

The geodesic equation is the variational origin of the Christoffel symbols, and it reduces to a straight line when the metric is constant.
