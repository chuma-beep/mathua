# Adding a fraction and a decimal

A fraction and a decimal can be added once both are written in the same form. Either form works and the choice depends on whether the fraction terminates as a decimal.

## The method

If the fraction has a denominator whose only prime factors are 2 and 5, convert it to a decimal and add. Otherwise convert the decimal to a fraction by writing it over a power of ten, then add over a common denominator and reduce. A decimal with two places is a fraction over 100, so the conversion is immediate. Never add a numerator to a decimal digit directly.

## A worked sum

Add \(\frac{3}{4}\) and 0.4. The fraction is 0.75, so the decimal route gives \(0.75 + 0.4 = 1.15\). The fraction route writes 0.4 as \(\frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}\), then \(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{15}{20} + \frac{8}{20} = \frac{23}{20}\). Both routes agree because \(\frac{23}{20} = 1.15\).

## Checking the result

Subtract 0.4 from the total: \(1.15 - 0.4 = 0.75\), which is \(\frac{3}{4}\). In fraction form, \(\frac{23}{20} - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{23}{20} - \frac{8}{20} = \frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}\). The check also confirms the decimal places, since 1.15 has two places and 0.4 has one.

One form for both terms is the whole method and the two routes must meet.
