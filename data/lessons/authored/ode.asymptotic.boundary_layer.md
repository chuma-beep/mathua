# Boundary layers

A boundary layer is the region where a rapidly varying term balances the highest derivative. Its thickness is found by balancing the two terms that would otherwise be unbalanced.

## Balancing

Stretch the independent variable as a power of the parameter. The derivative terms scale with different powers, and the layer exponent is the value that makes the two dominant terms comparable.

## Thickness

For a second-order equation whose highest derivative carries the parameter, the layer width is the parameter itself. A smaller parameter gives a thinner layer and a steeper transition.

## Matching

The inner solution as its scaled variable tends to the outer region must equal the outer solution as the boundary is approached. That limit fixes the free constant of the inner solution.

To check your understanding, stretch the variable, solve for the exponent and take the inner limit.

The layer thickness is a scaling law, and the exponent comes from an algebraic balance rather than from solving the equation.
