# Glide reflections

A glide reflection reflects across a line and then translates along it. It is the only orientation-reversing isometry without a fixed point.

## Two steps

A glide reflection combines a reflection and a parallel translation, in that order.

## The glide distance

The translation length is the glide distance. A pure reflection is the special case of zero glide distance.

## No fixed point

Composing a reflection with a nonzero parallel translation leaves no point fixed, unlike a reflection.

To check your understanding, reflect first, measure the slide and test a point.

A glide reflection is a reflection followed by a parallel slide.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
