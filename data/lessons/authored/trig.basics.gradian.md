# Gradians and angle units

A gradian is an angle unit that divides a right angle into 100 equal parts, so a full turn is 400 gradians.

## The gradian scale

The scale is decimal by design. Since 400 gradians and 360 degrees describe the same turn, one
gradian is 0.9 degrees and one degree is \(10/9\) gradians. Surveying instruments often read in
gradians because a right angle is exactly 100 and quarter turns are easy to add. Half a right
angle is 50 gradians. A tenth of a right angle is 10 gradians, which is a convenient step to draw.

## Converting degrees and gradians

Multiply by 0.9 to go from gradians to degrees; divide by 0.9 to go the other way. Thus 200
gradians is 180 degrees. Also 50 gradians is 45 degrees, while 30 degrees is \(30/0.9 = 33.33\)
gradians. In the other direction 250 gradians is 225 degrees, which is 45 degrees past a straight
angle of 180 degrees.

## A right angle in gradians

The right angle is the anchor: 100 gradians, exactly. A triangle with angles of 50, 80 and 70
gradians has the same shape as one with 45, 72 and 63 degrees, since each angle was multiplied by
0.9. The radian value of one gradian is \(\pi/200 \approx 0.015708\), so 100 gradians is
\(100\pi/200 = \pi/2\), as it must be.

The decimal appeal of the gradian has never overcome the older degree in general use.
