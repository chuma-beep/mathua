# Grouping the terms of a series

## Parentheses in a series

Parentheses merge neighbouring terms, so a grouped series is the sequence of partial sums at selected indices. The series \(1 - 1 + 1 - 1 + \cdots\) has partial sums 1, 0, 1, 0. Grouped as \((1-1) + (1-1) + \cdots\) every partial sum is 0. The grouped series converges to 0.

## Grouping a convergent series

Grouping a convergent series never changes its sum, because the grouped partial sums are a subsequence of the original ones. The alternating harmonic series equals \(\ln 2\). Grouped in pairs it reads \(1/2 + 1/12 + 1/30 + \cdots\). The first three groups total 0.6167 against 0.6931.

## Grouping a divergent series

Grouping a divergent series can manufacture a limit. The terms of \(1 - 1 + 1 - 1 + \cdots\) never tend to zero, so the series diverges, yet one grouping makes it look like 0 and another like 1. The lesson is one-directional: parentheses are safe after convergence is known, not before.
