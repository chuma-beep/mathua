# Lebesgue measure

Lebesgue measure extends length to very general subsets of the real line. It is countably additive on disjoint measurable sets and agrees with ordinary length on intervals.

## Measure of an interval

The Lebesgue measure of the interval from a to b is b minus a. Endpoints do not change the measure, so open, closed and half-open intervals of the same endpoints have the same length. A single point has measure zero.

## Disjoint unions

The measure of a finite or countable union of disjoint measurable sets is the sum of their measures. Two separated intervals therefore add their lengths. This is the additivity that makes measure a genuine extension of length. The measure of the support of a function is the measure of the set where the function is nonzero. That is still a set measure, not an integral.

## Overlapping intervals

When intervals overlap, adding their lengths counts the overlap twice. The measure of the union is the length of the larger interval, or the sum of lengths minus the overlap. For intervals from a to b and c to d with a less than c less than b less than d, the union is a to d.
