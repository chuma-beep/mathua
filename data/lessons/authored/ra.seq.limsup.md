# The limit superior of a sequence

The limit superior is the largest value that a subsequence can approach. It exists for every real sequence in the extended real line.

## Subsequential limits

A subsequential limit is the limit of some convergent subsequence. The limit superior is the supremum of all such limits. It is not the same as the limit of the whole sequence, because the whole sequence may fail to converge. Equivalently, it is the limit of the tail suprema: \(\limsup a_n = \inf_{n\ge1}\sup_{k\ge n} a_k\). Each tail supremum can only decrease as \(n\) grows, and its limit is the largest value the sequence approaches infinitely often.

## The largest cluster point

For a sequence with two constant subsequences, the limit superior is the larger of the two constants. For a sequence that converges, every subsequence has the same limit, so the limit superior equals the ordinary limit. A shift adds the same constant to every subsequential limit.

## Convergent sequences and shifts

If a sequence converges to L, its limit superior is L. If a bounded pattern with even limit A and odd limit B is shifted by C, the new limit superior is C plus the maximum of A and B. The shift does not change which subsequence is larger.
