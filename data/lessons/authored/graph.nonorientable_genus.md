# The nonorientable genus

The nonorientable genus counts the crosscaps needed to embed a graph, and one crosscap sometimes replaces two handles.

## The Euler formula

On a nonorientable surface with \(k\) crosscaps, a connected embedding satisfies \(n-m+f=2-k\). The nonorientable genus is the smallest such \(k\).

## Projective plane

The complete graph \(K_5\) does not embed in the plane but does embed in the projective plane, so its nonorientable genus is one. The complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,3}\) also embeds there.

## Comparison with orientable genus

Every orientable embedding is also an embedding on a nonorientable surface after adding crosscaps. The nonorientable genus is therefore at most twice the orientable genus plus one, and for many graphs it is smaller.

To check your understanding, count the cells, test planarity of \(K_5\) and compare the two formulas.

Crosscaps are cheaper than handles in the Euler formula, so nonorientable embeddings can exist where planar ones cannot.
