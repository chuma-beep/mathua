# Orientation of surfaces

A parametrised surface has two possible orientations. The cross product of its parameter derivatives chooses one, and reversing the parameters chooses the other.

## Parametrised surfaces and their normals

For a parametrisation r(u,v), the partial derivatives r_u and r_v span the tangent plane. Their cross product r_u cross r_v is a normal vector. Its direction depends on the order of the parameters, not only on the surface as a set of points.

## Choosing the upward normal

For a graph z equal to f(x,y), the natural parametrisation has normal (-f_x, -f_y, 1). The positive z-component makes this the upward orientation. The downward orientation is its negative. A normal with a positive z-component therefore answers the upward question.

## Orientation as a sign

Swapping u and v reverses the cross product. The same geometric surface therefore has two orientations, and an oriented integral changes sign when the orientation is reversed. The surface itself has not changed, only the direction in which it is traversed.
