# Apollonius's theorem

## The median relation

A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. Apollonius's theorem relates the median length to the three side lengths of the triangle.

The theorem is a generalization of the Pythagorean theorem. It is useful when a median and two sides are known and the third side is not.

## Using the theorem

For a median to side a, the relation is m_a squared equals one quarter of two b squared plus two c squared minus a squared. Substitute the known sides and solve for the remaining quantity.

Keep the factor of one quarter outside the whole expression. Applying it to only one term is a common error.

## Checking with Pythagoras

An isosceles triangle gives a right triangle when the median is drawn to the base. The median, half the base and an equal side satisfy the Pythagorean theorem.

Use this special case as a check. If the general formula does not reduce to the Pythagorean relation for an isosceles triangle, the substitution is wrong.

A final check is to restate the answer in the units and context of the original question. If the result is plausible and the units match, the calculation is ready.
