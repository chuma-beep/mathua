# Connection forms and curvature

Connection forms are the components of a connection in a moving frame whose derivatives are the curvature forms.

## The connection one-form

In an orthonormal frame the connection is a matrix of one-forms. Metric compatibility makes it antisymmetric, so in two dimensions one form carries everything.

The connection form records how the frame turns as the point moves. In a coordinate frame the symbols play that role. For the plane in polar coordinates the form is \(d\phi\), whose integral around the unit circle is \(2\pi\).

## The curvature two-form

The curvature form is the derivative of the connection plus the wedge of the connection with itself. The quadratic term makes the result a two-form.

For the plane in polar coordinates the connection form is \(d\phi\). Its derivative and its wedge with itself are zero, so the curvature is 0.

## Cartan's structure equations

The first equation gives the torsion and the second gives the curvature. The Levi-Civita connection has no torsion.

On the unit sphere the frame forms are \(d\theta\) and \(\sin\theta\,d\phi\). The connection form is \(-\cos\theta\,d\phi\). The curvature form is \(\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi\), the area form itself.

At the equator the coefficient is 1, so the curvature is 1. The frame computation and the tensor computation agree.

The equations compute curvature on a surface and scale to any dimension.
