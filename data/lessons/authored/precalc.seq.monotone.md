# Monotonicity of sequences

A monotone sequence always moves in one direction. A bounded monotone sequence converges, which is the main reason monotonicity matters.

## Increasing and decreasing

Compare consecutive terms. If every term is at least the one before, the sequence is increasing; if at most, it is decreasing.

## Boundedness

A sequence is bounded above when some number is at least every term and bounded below when some number is at most every term.

## Convergence

A sequence that is increasing and bounded above converges to its least upper bound. The decreasing case is the mirror statement.

To check your understanding, compare consecutive terms, find an upper bound and use monotonicity.

Monotonicity plus boundedness is enough for convergence, even without a formula for the limit.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
