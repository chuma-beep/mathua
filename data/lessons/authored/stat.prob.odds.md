# Odds and probability

Odds compare the number of ways an event can happen with the number of ways it can fail, while probability compares the ways it can happen with all the ways.

## Odds in favour

Odds in favour are the ratio of an event's probability to the probability of its complement. A horse with probability \(3/8\) of winning has odds in favour of \(3/5\), usually written 3 to 5, because \((3/8)/(5/8) = 3/5\). Reading 3 to 5 as 3 in 5 is the standard mistake.

## Converting to probability

If the odds in favour are a to b, the probability is \(a/(a+b)\). Odds of 3 to 5 give \(3/8\), the value we started from. Odds of 7 to 2 give \(7/9\), about 0.778. Odds of 2 to 1 give \(2/3\) while odds of 1 to 2 give \(1/3\), the reversal a careless reader misses.

## Odds against

Odds against an event are the probability of failure over the probability of success. Odds of 4 to 1 against mean a probability of \(1/5\) for the event, since it happens once for every four times it does not. A bookmaker quoting 4 to 1 is offering a stake multiplier of 4 on a \(1/5\) chance.

The two languages describe the same uncertainty; the conversion is short enough that there is no reason to guess which one a writer meant.
