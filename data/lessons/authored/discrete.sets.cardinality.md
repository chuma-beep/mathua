# Cardinality of finite sets

The cardinality of a finite set is its number of elements. Equality of cardinalities is witnessed by a bijection, which is the definition that generalises to infinite sets.

## Bijective definition

Two sets have the same cardinality when a bijection exists between them. For finite sets this reduces to counting.

## Sums and products

Cardinality is additive over disjoint unions and multiplicative over Cartesian products. Inclusion-exclusion corrects the overlap for non-disjoint unions.

## Subsets

A set of size \(n\) has \(2^n\) subsets and \(\binom{n}{k}\) subsets of size \(k\). These counts are the bridge to combinatorics.

To check your understanding, exhibit a bijection, add disjoint sizes and count all subsets.

Counting is the finite shadow of bijection. Once cardinality is defined by bijections, the same language extends to infinite sets without a new theory.
