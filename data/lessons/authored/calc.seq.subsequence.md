# Subsequences and the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem

## What a subsequence is

A subsequence keeps the terms of a sequence in order while deleting some of them, so its indices satisfy \(n_1 < n_2 < \cdots\). For \(a_n = (-1)^n\) the even indices give \(1, 1, 1, \ldots\) and the odd indices give \(-1, -1, -1, \ldots\). Both subsequences are constant, yet the original sequence has no limit.

## Bolzano-Weierstrass

Bolzano-Weierstrass says every bounded sequence of real numbers has a convergent subsequence. The sequence \(a_n = (-1)^n (1 + 1/n)\) stays inside the interval from -2 to 2. Its even terms are \(1 + 1/n\), which equal 1.5, 1.25 and 1.125 at \(n = 2, 4, 8\) and tend to 1. Boundedness alone supplies the convergent thread.

## Subsequential limits

A subsequential limit is the limit of some convergent subsequence. For \(a_n = (-1)^n + 1/n\) the even terms tend to 1 and the odd terms tend to -1, so the set of subsequential limits is the pair \(\{1, -1\}\). The largest is the limit superior and the smallest is the limit inferior. Both are attained here.
