# Speed as a unit rate

## Distance From Rate and Time

Speed is a rate that compares distance with time. A car travelling at 60 kilometres per hour for 2.5 hours covers \\(60 \\times 2.5 = 150\\) kilometres. The unit rate carries the units, so hours cancel and kilometres remain.

Keep the units consistent before multiplying. Minutes must become a fraction of an hour, so 30 minutes is 0.5 hours. Multiplying by 30 instead of 0.5 inflates the distance by a factor of sixty.

## Time From Distance and Speed

Time is distance divided by speed. A journey of 210 kilometres at 70 kilometres per hour takes \\(210 \\div 70 = 3\\) hours.

Rearrange the rate relationship rather than memorising three formulas: distance is rate times time, so time is distance divided by rate. The result should look reasonable against experience. A time of 30 hours for 210 kilometres suggests a decimal error.

## Speed From Distance and Time

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. Covering 150 kilometres in 2.5 hours gives \\(150 \\div 2.5 = 60\\) kilometres per hour.

Average speed is not the average of the speeds on each stretch, because time spent at a slow speed weighs more. Use the total distance and the total time whenever a journey has several parts.
