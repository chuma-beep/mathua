# Recognising a fraction in lowest terms

A fraction is in lowest terms when its numerator and denominator share no factor greater than 1. The condition is about the two numbers, not about how large the fraction looks.

## The rule

For positive whole numbers \(a\) and \(b\), the fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\) is in lowest terms exactly when the greatest common factor of \(a\) and \(b\) is 1. Such a pair is called coprime. For example \(\frac{7}{8}\) is in lowest terms, while \(\frac{6}{8}\) is not because both parts are divisible by 2.

## A worked example

Take \(\frac{35}{40}\). Both parts divide by 5, giving \(\frac{7}{8}\) and 7 and 8 share no factor, so the reduced form is in lowest terms. Now take \(\frac{15}{28}\): the numerator has factors 3 and 5, the denominator has factors 2 and 7 and no factor is shared, so the fraction was already in lowest terms. Finally \(\frac{21}{27}\) divides by 3 and becomes \(\frac{7}{9}\), which is reduced.

## Checking the result

To test a pair, try the primes up to the square root of the smaller number. For 15 and 28 the square root of 15 is below 4, so testing 2 and 3 is enough and neither divides both. For a reduced fraction, cross multiplication against the original confirms the value is preserved: \(35 \times 8 = 280\) and \(40 \times 7 = 280\).

Lowest terms is a statement about shared factors and one division by the greatest one settles it.
