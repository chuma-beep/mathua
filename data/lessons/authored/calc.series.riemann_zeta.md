# The Riemann zeta function as a series

## The defining series

The zeta function is the series \(\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{-s}\). At \(s = 2\) the first four terms are 1, 0.25, 0.1111 and 0.0625, whose sum is 1.4236 against the true value 1.6449. The gap is the tail. It shrinks as more terms are added.

## Convergence for s greater than one

For \(s > 1\) the terms \(n^{-s}\) decrease and the integral test bounds the tail by \(\int_N^\infty x^{-s} dx = N^{1-s}/(s-1)\). At \(s = 2\) and \(N = 10\) that bound is 0.1, so the partial sum is within a tenth of the limit. At \(s = 1\) the same integral has no finite value.

## Special values

The even values are known exactly. \(\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6 \approx 1.6449\), \(\zeta(4) = \pi^4/90 \approx 1.0823\) and \(\zeta(6) = \pi^6/945 \approx 1.0173\). Odd values above one have no closed form of this kind. At \(s = 1\) the series is the harmonic series, which diverges.
