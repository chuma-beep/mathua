# Dividing by a decimal

## Scaling the divisor

A decimal divisor can be removed by multiplying both the divisor and the dividend by the same power of ten. The quotient does not change because the division is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by 10/10.

Choose the power of ten that makes the divisor a whole number. For a divisor with one decimal place, multiply both numbers by 10. For two places, multiply by 100.

## Performing the division

After scaling, divide the whole-number dividend by the whole-number divisor. Keep track of the decimal point in the dividend if it has one. The quotient is the same as the original problem.

Estimate before dividing. If the divisor is about 2 and the dividend is about 40, the quotient should be about 20. An answer far from the estimate signals a place-value error.

## Checking the quotient

Multiply the quotient by the original divisor to recover the dividend. This check uses the inverse operation and catches most arithmetic mistakes.

A second check is to compare the size of the quotient with the size of the dividend. Dividing by a number below 1 makes the quotient larger than the dividend.
