# Subtractions that give a negative result

A fraction subtraction is not restricted to nonnegative answers. When the subtrahend is larger, the result lies left of zero on the number line.

## The method

Compare the two fractions first. If the first is smaller, subtract the smaller value from the larger and attach a negative sign. In symbols, \(\frac{a}{b} - \frac{c}{d} = -\left(\frac{c}{d} - \frac{a}{b}\right)\) when the first term is smaller. The magnitude is the positive distance between the two values. The pair \(\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{3}{4}\) gives \(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}\).

## A worked difference

Compute \(\frac{3}{8} - \frac{5}{8}\). The numerators differ by 2 and the larger term is the second, so the result is \(-\frac{2}{8} = -\frac{1}{4}\). A case with unlike denominators is \(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{3}{4}\). Over twelfths this is \(\frac{4}{12} - \frac{9}{12} = -\frac{5}{12}\). The result is negative and its magnitude is less than one half.

## Checking the result

Add the difference back to the subtrahend: \(-\frac{1}{4} + \frac{5}{8} = -\frac{2}{8} + \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{8}\), which is the minuend. For the second case, \(-\frac{5}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}\). A negative result always sits left of zero, so a number line check confirms the sign.

The sign belongs to the fraction as a whole, not to the numerator alone.
