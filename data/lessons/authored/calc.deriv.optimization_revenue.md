# Optimization: revenue

A revenue problem multiplies price by quantity. A linear demand curve makes revenue a quadratic in price.

## Demand and revenue

Demand \(q=a-bp\) falls as price rises. Revenue is \(R=pq=p(a-bp)\).

The revenue function is a downward parabola in \(p\). Its maximum is at the vertex.

The intercept \(a\) is the quantity demanded at price zero, and \(a/b\) is the price at which demand hits zero.

## The revenue-maximizing price

Differentiate \(R=a p-b p^2\) to get \(a-2bp\). Setting it to zero gives \(p=a/(2b)\).

This price is not necessarily the profit-maximizing price. Revenue ignores cost.

The optimal price is halfway between zero and the price that kills demand.

## Quantity and maximum revenue

Substitute the optimal price into demand to get the quantity. Then multiply price by quantity for revenue.

A price above the optimum leaves money on the table. A price below it sells more units at too low a margin.

The maximum revenue is \(a^2/(4b)\), which can be used as a check.

If demand is \(q=100-2p\), revenue is maximized at \(p=25\). At that price the quantity is 50 and revenue is 1250.
