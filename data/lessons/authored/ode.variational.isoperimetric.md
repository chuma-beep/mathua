# The isoperimetric problem

An isoperimetric problem extremises one functional subject to a constraint on another. A Lagrange multiplier turns it into an unconstrained Euler-Lagrange problem for the combined integrand.

## The constrained problem

The admissible curves must satisfy a second integral condition in addition to the boundary values. The constraint defines a subset of curves, and the extremum is sought inside that subset.

## The multiplier

Subtracting a multiple of the constraint integrand from the objective produces a new Lagrangian. Stationarity of the new functional for every admissible variation is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the combination.

## The classical example

Maximising area at fixed perimeter gives a circle. The Euler-Lagrange equation of the combined integrand has constant curvature as its solution, and the multiplier is fixed by the perimeter.

To check your understanding, write the objective, form the combined integrand and set up area and perimeter.

The multiplier is the same device as in constrained optimisation, and the variational version produces a differential equation rather than an algebraic system.
