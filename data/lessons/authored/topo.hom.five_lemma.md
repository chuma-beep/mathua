# The five lemma

The five lemma says that in a commutative diagram with exact rows, four isomorphisms force the fifth. It is the standard shortcut for proving that an induced map is an isomorphism.

## The shape

The diagram has five columns. Two exact rows and five vertical maps form the setting of the lemma.

## The hypothesis

Four of the five vertical maps must be isomorphisms. The two outer ones and the two adjacent to the middle are enough.

## The conclusion

The middle vertical map is then an isomorphism. The lemma is applied most often to a map of long exact sequences induced by a continuous map.

To check your understanding, count the columns, count the assumed isomorphisms and conclude the middle map.

Four known isomorphisms determine the fifth.
