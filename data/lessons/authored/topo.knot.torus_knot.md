# Torus knots

A torus knot winds \(p\) times around one circle of the torus and \(q\) times around the other. Coprime parameters give a knot rather than a link.

## Winding

The torus knot \(T(p,q)\) winds \(p\) times around one circle of the torus and \(q\) times around the other. Coprime parameters give a single component rather than a link.

## Crossing number

The crossing number is \(pq - \max(p,q)\). The cinquefoil \(T(2,5)\) has \(10 - 5 = 5\) crossings.

## Genus

The genus is \((p-1)(q-1)/2\). For \(T(2,5)\) the genus is two.

To check your understanding, read the two winding numbers, multiply the parameters and multiply the shifted parameters.

Two coprime integers encode a torus knot completely.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
