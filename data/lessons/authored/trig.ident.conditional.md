# Conditional trigonometric identities

A conditional identity is an equation in trigonometric ratios that holds only when its angles satisfy a stated relation.

## Identities that need a condition

Without the condition the equation is simply false. The identity \(\sin^{2}A + \sin^{2}B = 1\)
holds when the two angles add to a right angle and nowhere else. At 30 and 60 degrees the squared
sines are 0.25 and 0.75, which add to 1. At 30 and 30 degrees they add to 0.5, so the identity
fails. The condition is part of the statement, so a proof must use it.

## Complementary angles

Complementarity ties each ratio to its cofunction, which is where the cofunction names come from.
If the two angles add to a right angle then \(\tan A\tan B = 1\). At 35 and 55 degrees the
tangents are 0.7002 and 1.4281, whose product is 1.0000 to four places. The same condition makes
the sine of one angle equal the cosine of the other; at 35 and 55 degrees both are 0.5736. A pair
that misses a right angle by a few degrees breaks both statements.

## Angles of a triangle

A triangle supplies the condition that the three angles add to 180 degrees. From it, the three
tangents satisfy \(\tan A + \tan B + \tan C = \tan A\tan B\tan C\). At 45, 60 and 75 degrees the
sum of the tangents is 6.4641. Their product is 6.4641 as well.

Name the condition before using a conditional identity.
