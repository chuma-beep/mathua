# Precision and significant digits

## Significant digits

Significant digits are the digits that carry measured information. Start at the first nonzero digit and count through the last stated digit. Zeros between nonzero digits are significant, and trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant.

Leading zeros before the first nonzero digit are placeholders, not significant digits. The measurement 0.00450 has three significant digits: 4, 5 and the final 0.

## Rounding to a precision

Rounding to a stated precision keeps a fixed number of digits or a fixed decimal place. Look at the next digit to decide whether to round up. The rounded value should not claim more precision than the original measurement.

Write trailing zeros when they are significant. A measurement of 2.0 has two significant digits, while 2 has one. The zero is part of the precision claim.

## Comparing measurements

Measurements can be compared by subtracting them after they are expressed in the same unit. The result should be reported with a precision that matches the least precise input.

Do not add digits that the measurement does not support. A difference cannot be more precise than the measurements used to compute it.
