# Scaling a recipe with ratios

## Scaling Servings Up

A recipe is a ratio of ingredients to servings, so changing the servings changes every ingredient by one factor. A recipe for 4 servings that uses 3 cups of flour scaled to 10 servings needs a factor of \\(10 \\div 4 = 2.5\\).

Multiply each amount by that factor: \\(2.5 \\times 3 = 7.5\\) cups of flour. The factor is greater than one here, so every amount grows. A smaller result signals a factor written upside down.

## Scaling Servings Down

A smaller batch uses a factor below one. Going from 4 servings to 2 gives a factor of \\(2 \\div 4 = 0.5\\). The 3 cups of flour become 1.5 cups. Two teaspoons of salt become 1 teaspoon.

Halving is easy, but other reductions need care with fractions of a teaspoon. Convert to millilitres when a measure becomes too small to read, because a rounded spoon is a larger error at small scale.

## Finding the Scale Factor

The scale factor is always the new quantity divided by the old one. Confirm it before multiplying, since one division controls every line of the recipe. For 10 servings from 4, the factor is 2.5. Another ingredient needing 2 teaspoons then becomes \\(2.5 \\times 2 = 5\\) teaspoons.

Check one converted amount against a rough double. If the new amount is not near twice the old for a factor of 2.5, the multiplication has slipped.
