# Areas of quadrilaterals

Quadrilateral areas follow from slicing the figure into triangles. A diagonal always produces two triangles whose areas add to the whole.

## The rectangle

A rectangle of sides \(a\) and \(b\) has area \(ab\), the simplest quadrilateral formula.

## The trapezoid

A trapezoid with parallel sides \(a\) and \(b\) and height \(h\) has area \(\tfrac12(a+b)h\).

## The diagonal

Any quadrilateral splits along a diagonal into two triangles, so its area is the sum of two triangle areas.

To check your understanding, multiply the sides, average the bases and draw a diagonal.

A quadrilateral is two triangles sharing a diagonal.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
