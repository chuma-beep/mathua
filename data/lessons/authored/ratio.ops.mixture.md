# Mixing paint in a given ratio

## Parts of a Mixture

A paint ratio such as 3 parts blue to 2 parts yellow describes shares of the total, not fixed volumes. The total is 5 parts, so blue is three fifths of the mixture and yellow is two fifths.

With 20 litres of mixed paint, one part is \\(20 \\div 5 = 4\\) litres. Blue is \\(3 \\times 4 = 12\\) litres and yellow is \\(2 \\times 4 = 8\\) litres. The two amounts add back to the total, which is the first check to make.

## One Part at a Time

Thinking in parts keeps the arithmetic simple. Find the size of one part once, then multiply by the number of parts of each colour. A part of 4 litres gives 12 litres of blue for three parts and 8 litres of yellow for two.

The part size is a rate: litres per part. Naming it that way explains why the same multiplication works for every colour and why the units cancel cleanly when the parts are added.

## Total From a Part

A part can also be recovered from one ingredient. If a batch uses 15 litres of blue and blue is 3 parts, then one part is \\(15 \\div 3 = 5\\) litres. The whole mixture is 5 parts, so the total is \\(5 \\times 5 = 25\\) litres.

Then yellow is 10 litres, since it holds two parts. Adding the ingredients recovers 25 litres, which confirms that the part size and the total agree.
