# Adding fractions with the same numerator

Fractions that share a numerator add by a short formula. The shared numerator is factored out and the denominators combine in the remaining factor.

## The method

For a nonzero whole number \(a\) and positive denominators \(b\) and \(c\), \(\frac{a}{b} + \frac{a}{c} = a(\frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}) = \frac{a(b + c)}{bc}\). Add the denominators, multiply by the shared numerator and place the result over the product of the denominators. Reduce at the end. When one denominator is a multiple of the other, the product is larger than necessary but the formula still holds.

## A worked sum

Add \(\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{2}{5}\). The denominator sum is 8 and the product is 15, so the total is \(\frac{2 \times 8}{15} = \frac{16}{15} = 1\frac{1}{15}\). When the denominators already match, the formula collapses to a simple count: \(\frac{3}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}\).

## Checking the result

Convert to fifteenths: \(\frac{2}{3} = \frac{10}{15}\) and \(\frac{2}{5} = \frac{6}{15}\), whose sum is \(\frac{16}{15}\). The check confirms both the total and the regrouping. A frequent error is to add the denominators and keep the numerator, which would give \(\frac{2}{8}\) for the first case, a value far below 1 and clearly wrong since both addends exceed \(\frac{1}{3}\).

The numerator is shared, so it factors out and only the denominators combine.
