# Gaussian primes

A Gaussian prime has no factor except a unit or an associate. An ordinary prime p stays prime when p is 3 mod 4, splits when p is 1 mod 4, and ramifies when p is 2.

## The three cases

If \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) then \(p\) is a Gaussian prime. If \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) then \(p=(a+bi)(a-bi)\) with \(a^{2}+b^{2}=p\). The prime 2 factors as \((1+i)(1-i)\) up to a unit.

## Counting primes

Counting ordinary primes in a residue class is the first step in counting Gaussian primes by norm.

## Norms of factors

A Gaussian prime over \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) has norm \(p\), while the two factors of 2 both have norm 2.
