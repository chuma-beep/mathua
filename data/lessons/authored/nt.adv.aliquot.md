# Aliquot sums

The aliquot sum adds the proper divisors of a number. It separates numbers into perfect, abundant and deficient classes and begins the study of amicable pairs.

## Proper divisors

A proper divisor is a positive divisor smaller than the number. Summing them gives the aliquot sum s of n. The number itself is excluded, which is why a perfect number has s equal to n.

## Perfect, abundant and deficient

A number is perfect when its proper divisors sum to itself. It is abundant when the sum is larger and deficient when the sum is smaller. The comparison is always between s of n and n.

## The full divisor sum

The sum of all divisors is the aliquot sum plus the number. It is written sigma of n. Factoring n turns sigma into a product of geometric series, which is faster than listing every divisor.

The three sections are one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.
