# Area under a parametric curve

## The area integral

The area integral is \(\int y\,dx\), written in the parameter as \(\int y(t)x'(t)\,dt\). With \(x = t\) and \(y = t^2\) on the interval from 0 to 2 the integral is \(\int_0^2 t^2 dt = 8/3\), the area under \(y = x^2\).

## The substitution

The substitution requires \(x(t)\) to be monotone on the interval, so each \(x\) is visited once. The half circle \(x = \cos t\) and \(y = \sin t\) on the interval from 0 to \(\pi\) gives \(\int -\sin^2 t\,dt = -\pi/2\). The sign follows the direction of travel and the magnitude \(\pi/2\) is the area.

## A worked area

A worked area: \(x = t^2\) and \(y = t\) on the interval from 0 to 2 give \(\int_0^2 t \cdot 2t\,dt = 16/3 \approx 5.333\). The curve is \(y = \sqrt{x}\) for \(x\) from 0 to 4. The integral \(\int_0^4 x^{1/2} dx = 16/3\) confirms it.

A complete answer names the relation, substitutes the data and states the result in the language of the problem. When a second method is available, compare the two results before moving on.
