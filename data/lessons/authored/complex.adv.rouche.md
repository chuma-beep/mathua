# Rouché's theorem

Rouché's theorem counts the zeros of a sum by comparing the two terms on a contour.

## Compare two functions on a contour

On the contour, one term must be strictly smaller than the other. Then the sum has the same number of zeros inside as the dominant term. The comparison is an inequality on the boundary, not inside.

## Count zeros

Count the zeros of the dominant term inside the contour. That count is also the count for the sum. Multiplicity is counted with the same multiplicity.

## Apply the conclusion

Rouché's theorem turns a hard zero count into an easier one. It is often used to locate zeros in an annulus or a half-plane. The contour must avoid the zeros of the dominant term.
