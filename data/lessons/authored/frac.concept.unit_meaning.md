# The unit fraction one over n

The unit fraction \(\frac{1}{n}\) is a single part of a whole that has been cut into \(n\) equal parts. Every other fraction is a count of such parts.

## The definition

Split one whole into \(n\) equal parts, where \(n\) is a positive whole number. One part is \(\frac{1}{n}\) and \(a\) parts give \(\frac{a}{n}\). For example \(\frac{5}{8}\) is five copies of \(\frac{1}{8}\). As \(n\) grows the parts shrink, so \(\frac{1}{3}\) is larger than \(\frac{1}{4}\), which is larger than \(\frac{1}{5}\).

## An example

The fraction \(\frac{3}{4}\) equals \(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{7}{10}\) equals seven copies of \(\frac{1}{10}\). On a number line from 0 to 1, marking every tenth places \(\frac{7}{10}\) at the seventh mark. The unit fraction also answers a division question: \(\frac{1}{4}\) is the result of sharing 1 among 4.

## Why it matters

Unit fractions are the building blocks for addition, subtraction and division of fractions. A sum such as \(\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5}\) counts copies of \(\frac{1}{5}\) and a division such as \(3 \div \frac{1}{4}\) asks how many quarters fit in 3. The reciprocal of the whole number \(n\) is exactly \(\frac{1}{n}\).

Counting copies of one unit fraction is the clearest way to see what a fraction measures.
