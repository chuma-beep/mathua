# Related rates: conical tank

Water in a cone relates volume, depth and radius. A fixed shape ratio reduces the volume formula to one variable.

## The shape ratio

If the surface radius is half the depth, then \(r=h/2\) at every water level. Similar triangles keep the ratio constant.

Substitute the ratio into the cone volume before differentiating. This avoids a second unknown rate.

The ratio is a property of the tank, so it does not change as the water rises.

## The volume relation

With \(r=h/2\), \(V=\pi h^3/12\). Differentiating gives \(dV/dt=(\pi h^2/4)\,dh/dt\).

The rate of depth change grows as the cone narrows. The same inflow produces a faster rise at a smaller depth.

The factor \(h^2\) makes the relation nonlinear, so the depth rate is not constant.

## Solving the rate

Substitute the current depth and the known inflow. Solve for \(dh/dt\).

Keep the factor of \(\pi\) consistent. If the inflow is given as a multiple of \(\pi\), the \(\pi\) cancels.

Check the units: volume per time divided by area gives length per time.
