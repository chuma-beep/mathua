# Three-term ratios

## Writing and reducing

A three-term ratio compares three quantities in a fixed order. The ratio 2:3:4 means the first quantity has 2 parts, the second has 3 parts and the third has 4 parts. The order matters: 2:3:4 is not the same as 4:3:2. Count each quantity in the same unit. Divide all three terms by their greatest common factor. If a bag holds 12 red, 18 blue and 24 green counters, the common factor is 6, so the ratio is 2:3:4. A ratio is fully reduced when the only common factor of all three terms is 1.

## Scaling to a total

When a ratio is given with a total, find the value of one part first. If the ratio is 2:3:5 and the total is 50, the ratio has 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts. One part is 50 / 10 = 5, so the quantities are 10, 15 and 25. The largest share is 5 parts, or 25. Keep the order of the ratio when you assign the shares.

## Checking the parts

Add the scaled parts and compare the sum with the stated total. Then divide each part by the same factor and check that the reduced ratio matches the original. A common error is to divide only one term, which changes the comparison rather than simplifying it. Another check is to confirm that the largest share corresponds to the largest term in the ratio.
