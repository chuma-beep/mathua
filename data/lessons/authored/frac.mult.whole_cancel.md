# Multiplying a whole number by a fraction

A whole number multiplies a fraction through its numerator. Cancelling a common factor before multiplying keeps the numbers small and gives the reduced answer directly.

## The method

Write the whole number \(n\) as \(\frac{n}{1}\), then compute \(\frac{n}{1} \times \frac{a}{b} = \frac{na}{b}\). Before multiplying, cancel any common factor shared by \(n\) and \(b\). The cancel divides one numerator and one denominator by the same number, so the value is unchanged. The denominator stays nonzero and the result may be improper.

## A worked product

Compute \(4 \times \frac{3}{8}\). The factor 4 and the denominator 8 share 4, so cancel to leave \(\frac{1}{1} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{2}\), which is \(1\frac{1}{2}\). Without cancelling the product is \(\frac{12}{8}\) and reducing by 4 returns the same value. A second case is \(6 \times \frac{5}{9}\), where 6 and 9 share 3 and the product is \(\frac{10}{3}\) or \(3\frac{1}{3}\).

## Checking the result

Add the fraction four times: \(\frac{3}{8} + \frac{3}{8} + \frac{3}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}\). Divide the product by 4: \(\frac{3}{2} \div 4 = \frac{3}{8}\), which returns the original fraction. Both checks confirm the cancellation was legal.

Cancelling first is the same arithmetic, performed while the numbers are still small.
