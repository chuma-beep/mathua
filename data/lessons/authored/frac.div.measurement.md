# Measurement division with fractions

Measurement division asks how many groups of a stated size fit into a total. The divisor names the size of one group and the quotient counts the groups.

## The method

Divide the total by the group size: a total \(T\) with groups of size \(\frac{c}{d}\) gives \(T \div \frac{c}{d}\) groups. The divisor must be nonzero, so the group size cannot be zero. A remainder smaller than one group may appear when the total is not an exact multiple. Groups of \(\frac{1}{3}\) in 2 give \(2 \div \frac{1}{3} = 6\).

## A worked quotient

How many quarters fit into \(2\frac{1}{2}\)? The total is \(\frac{5}{2}\) and dividing by \(\frac{1}{4}\) gives \(\frac{5}{2} \times 4 = 10\). So ten quarters fill two and a half. A whole number case is \(3 \div \frac{1}{2} = 6\), the count of halves in 3.

## Checking the result

Multiply the count by the group size: \(10 \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{10}{4} = \frac{5}{2}\), which is the original total. The whole number case checks as \(6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3\). A quotient that is not a whole number means the total holds that many full groups plus part of another, which is still a legitimate answer.

Measurement division answers how many groups and multiplication by the group size rebuilds the total.
