# Regrouping an improper sum

Adding mixed numbers can produce a fractional part of 1 or more. Regrouping carries each whole from the fractional part into the whole part, so the answer stays in standard mixed form.

## The method

Add the whole parts and add the fraction parts separately over a common denominator. If the fraction sum is at least 1, write it as a mixed number and add its whole part to the whole total. The fraction that remains is always below 1. The whole parts may be added in any order, because only the fractional part needs a common denominator.

## A worked sum

Add \(2\frac{3}{4}\) and \(1\frac{5}{6}\). The fractions become \(\frac{9}{12}\) and \(\frac{10}{12}\), whose sum is \(\frac{19}{12}\). Regrouping gives \(1\frac{7}{12}\), so the whole total is \(2 + 1 + 1 = 4\) and the answer is \(4\frac{7}{12}\). The fractional part \(\frac{7}{12}\) is below 1, so no further carry is needed.

## Checking the result

Convert both mixed numbers to improper fractions: \(\frac{11}{4}\) and \(\frac{11}{6}\). Over twelfths they are \(\frac{33}{12}\) and \(\frac{22}{12}\), summing to \(\frac{55}{12}\), which is \(4\frac{7}{12}\). The decimal check is \(2.75 + 1.833 = 4.583\) and \(4 + \frac{7}{12}\) is about 4.583.

A carry is the only difference between the mixed method and the improper method, so the two must agree.
