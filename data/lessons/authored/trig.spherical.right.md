# Right spherical triangles

A right spherical triangle has one angle of 90 degrees, so its parts satisfy relations that reduce to the plane ones for small triangles.

## Angles of a right spherical triangle

Write the right angle as \(C\) and the sides opposite the other two angles as \(a\) and \(b\),
with the hypotenuse \(c\). Angles are measured in degrees; sides are measured as angles at the
centre of the sphere. With \(a = 30\) and \(b = 40\) the triangle is fully determined. The plane
triangle with those legs would have \(A = 36.87\) degrees.

## The spherical Pythagorean relation

The relation is \(\cos c = \cos a\cos b\). With \(a = 30\) and \(b = 40\) it gives
\(\cos c = 0.86603(0.76604) \approx 0.66341\), so \(c \approx 48.44\) degrees. The plane
hypotenuse would be 50 degrees, so the spherical side is slightly short. The gap grows with the
size of the triangle and disappears as the triangle shrinks.

## Solving for an angle

Napier's rules give \(\tan A = \tan a/\sin b\). With \(a = 30\) and \(b = 40\),
\(\tan A = 0.57735/0.64279 \approx 0.89820\), so \(A \approx 41.93\) degrees. The check uses
\(\sin A = \sin a/\sin c\), which gives \(0.5/0.74825 \approx 0.66822\) and the same angle.

The two routes differ only in which pair of parts is used, so either can check the other. Every
right spherical triangle is fixed by any two of its five parts, so the other three are never free.
