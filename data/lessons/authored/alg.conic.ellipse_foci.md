# Foci of an ellipse

An ellipse is the set of points whose distances to two fixed foci add to a constant. The major and minor axes determine where the foci lie.

## The defining sum

For an ellipse with semi-major axis \(a\), the sum of distances from any point to the two foci is \(2a\). This is independent of the point.

The major axis is the longer axis. Its half-length is \(a\), and the minor half-length is \(b\).

## Finding c

The foci are at distance \(c\) from the center, where \(c^2=a^2-b^2\). For an ellipse, \(c<a\).

If the larger denominator is under \(y^2\), the major axis is vertical and the foci lie on the \(y\)-axis.

## Checking the numbers

A Pythagorean triple such as \((a,b,c)=(5,3,4)\) makes the arithmetic exact. Then \(c^2=25-9=16\) and \(c=4\).

The foci always lie inside the ellipse because \(c<a\). A value of \(c\) larger than \(a\) signals an arithmetic or axis error.

The focal distance is always smaller than the semi-major axis. Equality would collapse the ellipse onto a line segment.
