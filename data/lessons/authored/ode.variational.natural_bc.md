# Natural boundary conditions

When an endpoint is free the variation does not vanish there, and stationarity adds a boundary condition. The condition is natural because the problem supplies it rather than the statement.

## Free endpoint

The perturbation is no longer required to vanish at a free endpoint. The boundary term from integration by parts survives and must vanish on its own.

## Natural condition

The surviving term is the derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the slope evaluated at the endpoint. Setting it to zero is the natural boundary condition, and it is a condition on the slope rather than on the value.

## Mixed problems

A problem with one fixed and one free endpoint has one essential condition and one natural condition. The essential condition constrains the value, the natural condition constrains the slope, and the two together determine the integration constants.

To check your understanding, relax the endpoint constraint, evaluate the slope derivative and count the constants.

The distinction between essential and natural conditions is the variational origin of the different boundary condition types in a boundary-value problem.
