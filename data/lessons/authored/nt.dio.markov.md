# The Markov equation

The Markov equation x squared plus y squared plus z squared equals three x y z has infinitely many positive integer solutions. Its solutions form a tree.

## The equation

Every solution has positive entries, and the smallest is one, one, one. From any solution, fixing two entries and solving for the third gives a quadratic with two roots.

## Vieta jumping

The two roots sum to three times the product of the fixed entries. One root is the known entry, so the other is an integer, which produces a neighbouring solution.

## The tree

Starting from one, one, one and replacing one coordinate at a time generates every solution exactly once. The Markov numbers are the entries that appear.

To check your understanding, check the base triple, sum the roots and start at one one one.

Markov solutions are connected by Vieta jumping, and the jump is an involution that turns one solution into its neighbour.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
