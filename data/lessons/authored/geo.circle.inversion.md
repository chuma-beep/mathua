# Circle inversion

Inversion in a circle sends a point along the ray from the centre to a point whose distances multiply to the squared radius. Circles and lines transform into each other.

## The defining product

A point at distance d from the centre maps to a point at distance d prime on the same ray, with d times d prime equal to R squared.

## Lines and circles

A line not through the centre inverts to a circle through the centre, and a circle through the centre inverts to a line. A circle not through the centre inverts to another circle.

## Angles and distances

Inversion preserves angles but reverses orientation. Distances are not preserved, which is why the product of the two radial distances is the useful invariant.

To check your understanding, square the radius, invert a line and preserve the angle.

Inversion is governed by the product of radial distances.
