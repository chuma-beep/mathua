# Matrix powers

A matrix power multiplies a matrix by itself repeatedly. Powers reveal nilpotence, idempotence and the long-run behaviour of a linear process.

## Multiplying a matrix by itself

The square of a matrix is the product of the matrix with itself. The entry in row i and column j is the dot product of row i with column j. Matrix multiplication is not commutative, so the order of the factors matters.

## The trace of a power

The trace of a matrix is the sum of its diagonal entries. For a power, compute the power first and then add the diagonal. The trace is invariant under cyclic permutations of a product.

## Higher powers

Repeated multiplication gives A cubed, A to the fourth and so on. A pattern often appears after the second or third power. The pattern is the fastest route for a large exponent.
