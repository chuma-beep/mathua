# Limits of complex fractions

A complex fraction has fractions inside a fraction. Combining them into a single quotient usually reveals a cancelling factor.

## Combining the fractions

Write the numerator as one fraction with a common denominator. Then divide by the outer factor by multiplying by its reciprocal.

The common denominator is the product of the small denominators. Keep track of signs when subtracting.

A complex fraction is not a new operation, only a quotient written in a compressed form.

## Cancelling the zero

After combining, a factor equal to the variable difference appears in both numerator and denominator. Cancel it before substituting.

The cancelled factor is zero at the limit point, which is why direct substitution failed.

Cancellation is valid near the limit point, not necessarily at the point itself.

## The limiting value

For \((1/x-1/a)/(x-a)\), the limit is \(-1/a^2\). The sign comes from the subtraction order.

A quick check with a numerical value of \(a\) catches a sign error. The magnitude scales like \(1/a^2\).

As \(a\) grows, the limit gets closer to zero. The formula shows that trend directly.
