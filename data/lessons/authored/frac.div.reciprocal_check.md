# Checking division with the reciprocal

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. The check reverses the step: the quotient multiplied by the divisor must return the dividend.

## The method

For nonzero fractions, \(\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}\). Invert the divisor by swapping its numerator and denominator, then multiply and reduce. The divisor cannot be zero, so its numerator must be nonzero before inverting. A fraction and its reciprocal multiply to 1, which is why the inversion works.

## A worked quotient

Compute \(\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{5}\). The reciprocal of \(\frac{2}{5}\) is \(\frac{5}{2}\), so the quotient is \(\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{15}{8}\), which is \(1\frac{7}{8}\). A second case is \(\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{10}{9} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{45}{60} = \frac{3}{4}\).

## Checking the result

Multiply the quotient by the divisor: \(\frac{15}{8} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{30}{40} = \frac{3}{4}\), which is the dividend. For the second case, \(\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{10}{9} = \frac{30}{36} = \frac{5}{6}\). The check is the definition of division restated, so a failure points to a wrong reciprocal. A quotient and its divisor always rebuild the dividend exactly. The reciprocal is also called the multiplicative inverse, because it undoes the original factor.

A quotient is correct when the divisor rebuilds the dividend from it.
