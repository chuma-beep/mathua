# Stem-and-leaf plots

A stem-and-leaf plot splits each observation into a stem, usually its leading digits, plus a leaf, usually its last digit, so the shape and the original values stay visible together.

## Stems and leaves

Split each value at a chosen cut point. For 23, 27, 31, 34, 34 and 38 the stem is the tens digit and the leaf is the units digit, so the 2 row reads 3 7 and the 3 row reads 1 4 4 8. Nothing is lost, since six leaves mean the six observations can be recovered exactly.

## Ordering the data

An unordered plot still shows a shape, but an ordered one is faster to read. Sort the leaves within each stem from smallest to largest, so a row 7 3 9 1 becomes 1 3 7 9. A stem of 10 holding 22 leaves then supports counting directly, because the eleventh and twelfth leaves in the ordered listing bracket the median.

## Reading a plot

Rows are intervals and leaves are repeats. A row of twelve leaves at stem 4 says twelve observations fell between 40 and 49; the length of that row against its neighbours locates the mode. If stem 4 holds 12 leaves and stem 5 holds 3, the data are concentrated in the forties and thin out in the fifties.

The plot survives because it keeps every value, so a reader can check a claim against the raw data rather than trusting a summary.
