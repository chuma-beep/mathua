# End behavior

End behavior describes what a function does as its input grows without bound in either direction. For polynomials the leading term decides it.

## Leading term

The term of highest degree dominates for large inputs, so the sign and degree of that term fix both ends of the graph.

## Even and odd degree

An even-degree leading term sends both ends the same way. An odd-degree term sends them in opposite directions.

## Rational functions

A rational function follows the ratio of its leading terms, which produces a horizontal asymptote, a slant asymptote or unbounded growth.

To check your understanding, identify the highest degree, check the parity and compare degrees.

Write end behavior as a limit statement, because the graph never reaches the end.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
