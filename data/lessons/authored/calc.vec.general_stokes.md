# Generalized Stokes' theorem

Generalized Stokes' theorem says the integral of a form over a boundary equals the integral of its exterior derivative over the interior. It contains Green's theorem, the divergence theorem and the classical Stokes theorem as special cases.

## Boundary orientation

An oriented manifold induces an orientation on its boundary. The boundary orientation is chosen so that the interior lies to the left of the boundary direction. Reversing the orientation of the manifold reverses the boundary integral.

## Evaluating on a square

For a one-form on a square, the boundary integral is the sum of the four edge integrals with their induced directions. The exterior derivative is a two-form whose coefficient is a single function. Integrating that coefficient over the square gives the same number.

## Why it generalizes

Green's theorem applies the statement to a planar region. The divergence theorem applies it to a solid and a two-form. The classical Stokes theorem applies it to a surface and a one-form. The generalized form is the single identity behind all three.

The three sections form one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so the three moves stay connected.
