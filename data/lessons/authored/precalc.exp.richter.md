# The Richter scale

The Richter magnitude of an earthquake is a logarithm of the amplitude recorded by a seismograph. One unit of magnitude multiplies the amplitude by ten.

## The definition

Magnitude is the base-ten logarithm of the ratio of amplitude to a reference amplitude, so equal differences in magnitude are equal ratios of amplitude.

## Comparing events

Two events differing by m magnitudes differ in amplitude by a factor of ten to the m.

## Energy

Energy grows faster than amplitude. A modest magnitude difference corresponds to a much larger energy ratio, which is why the scale is compressed.

To check your understanding, take the ratio, subtract magnitudes and compare magnitudes.

A logarithmic scale reports ratios, never absolute sizes.
