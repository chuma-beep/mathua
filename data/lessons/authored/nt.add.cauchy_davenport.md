# The Cauchy-Davenport theorem

The Cauchy-Davenport theorem gives a lower bound for a sumset in a prime field. The bound is the sum of the sizes minus one, capped by the field size.

## The statement

For nonempty subsets of the integers modulo a prime p, the sumset has size at least the minimum of p and the sum of the sizes minus one.

## Sharpness

Arithmetic progressions attain the bound. When the two sets are the same interval, the sumset is the doubled interval and the bound is exact.

## The cap

When the sum of the sizes exceeds the field size, the sumset is the whole field. The cap is why the theorem is stated with a minimum.

To check your understanding, add the sizes, use an interval and exceed the field size.

Cauchy-Davenport is the model result for sumsets in prime fields, and its bound is sharp for intervals.
