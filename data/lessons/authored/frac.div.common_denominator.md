# Dividing fractions with a common denominator

Two fractions with the same denominator divide as their numerators do. The common denominator cancels, leaving a simpler quotient.

## The common denominator cancels

Write the division as a over c divided by b over c. Multiplying by the reciprocal gives a over c times c over b. The c factors cancel, leaving a over b.

## Simplifying the quotient

Reduce a over b by the greatest common divisor of a and b. The simplified numerator is a divided by the gcd and the simplified denominator is b divided by the gcd. Both are integers.

## The missing factor

The quotient asks how many times b over c fits into a over c. Equivalently, it asks what factor multiplies b to give a. The common denominator does not change that factor.

The three sections form one chain. The first names the object, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context.

Practice should be answered without a calculator first. Write the relation, substitute the published values and state the integer result. Then check the result against the defining identity or a second representation before moving to the next question.
