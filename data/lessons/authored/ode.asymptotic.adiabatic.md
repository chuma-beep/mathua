# Adiabatic invariants

An adiabatic invariant is a quantity that stays nearly constant when a parameter of a Hamiltonian system changes slowly. Action variables are the standard example, and their invariance explains the long-term order of slowly forced oscillators.

## Action variable

The action is the area enclosed by an orbit in phase space divided by two pi. For an oscillator it equals the energy divided by the frequency, so it measures amplitude squared times frequency.

## Slow parameter change

When a parameter changes on a time scale much longer than the period, the action changes by an amount of order the small parameter per period. The accumulated change stays bounded instead of growing.

## Consequence

If the frequency rises while the action is fixed, the energy rises in proportion. A swing pumped slowly gains amplitude, and the invariant predicts the gain without solving the full equation.

To check your understanding, write the action integral, compare the two time scales and hold the action fixed.

The invariant is approximate rather than exact, but its error stays bounded for exponentially long times in many systems.
