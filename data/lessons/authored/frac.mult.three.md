# Multiplying three fractions

A product of three fractions multiplies all three numerators and all three denominators. Cancelling across the whole product first keeps the arithmetic small.

## The method

Write the product as one fraction with a numerator product over a denominator product. Any numerator may cancel against any denominator, because multiplication is commutative. Multiply what remains and reduce. Each cancel removes a common factor from one numerator and one denominator, which leaves the value unchanged. The order of the three factors does not change the result and the product is smaller than any factor below 1.

## A worked product

Compute \(\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{9}{10} \times \frac{5}{6}\). The numerator product is 90 and the denominator product is 180, giving \(\frac{1}{2}\). Cancelling first is shorter: 9 against 3 leaves 3, 2 against 10 leaves 5 and 5 against 5 leaves 1, so the product is \(\frac{1}{2}\). Multiplying in pairs gives \(\frac{3}{5}\) and then \(\frac{15}{30} = \frac{1}{2}\).

## Checking the result

Divide the product by the last factor: \(\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{5}{6} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{3}{5}\). Divide again by the middle factor: \(\frac{3}{5} \div \frac{9}{10} = \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{10}{9} = \frac{30}{45} = \frac{2}{3}\), which is the first factor.

Cancelling across a product is one application of the same rule, used several times.
