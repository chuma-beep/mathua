# The friendship theorem

The friendship theorem says that a graph in which every two vertices have exactly one common neighbour is a windmill of triangles sharing one vertex.

## The windmill

The windmill \(F_k\) consists of \(k\) triangles sharing a single common vertex. It has \(2k+1\) vertices, \(3k\) edges and \(k\) triangles.

## The conclusion

If every pair of distinct vertices has exactly one common neighbour, the graph is \(F_k\) for some \(k\). The shared vertex is adjacent to every other vertex, and the remaining edges form a perfect matching.

## Counting the pairs

Each triangle of the windmill supplies the unique common neighbour for its two outer vertices. The common vertex pairs with every other vertex through the triangle that contains it, which is why no other graph survives the condition.

To check your understanding, count the vertices, name the shared vertex and assign a triangle to a pair.

A single common neighbour for every pair forces a very rigid structure, and the windmill is the only one.
