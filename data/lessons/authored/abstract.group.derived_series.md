# The derived series

The derived series iterates the commutator subgroup. A group is solvable exactly when the series reaches the trivial subgroup in finitely many steps.

## Construction

Set \(G^{(0)}=G\) and \(G^{(k+1)}=[G^{(k)},G^{(k)}]\). Each term is normal in the previous one.

## Solvability

The series reaches the trivial subgroup exactly when the group is solvable, and the number of steps is the derived length.

## Examples

Abelian groups have derived length at most one, \(S_3\) has length two, and \(S_n\) for \(n\ge 5\) is not solvable.

To check your understanding, iterate commutators, count the steps and check the abelian case.

The derived series is the standard witness of solvability.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller numbers and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
