# Cantor's diagonal argument

Cantor's diagonal argument shows that the real numbers cannot be listed. Given any listing, a number is constructed to differ from the \(n\)-th entry at the \(n\)-th digit.

## Assuming a listing

Suppose every real in an interval appears in a sequence. Write each expansion as a digit string and arrange them in rows.

## The diagonal

Change the \(n\)-th digit of the \(n\)-th row, choosing a digit different from 9 to avoid the ambiguity of trailing nines. The resulting string is not any row.

## The contradiction

The constructed number differs from every listed number at one digit, so the listing was incomplete. No listing can exist.

To check your understanding, enumerate the rows, change the diagonal digit and compare with every row.

Diagonalisation is a general technique for proving that a set cannot be enumerated. It later reappears in the halting problem and in the time hierarchy theorem.
