# Expanded form of decimals

## Place values

Every digit in a decimal has a place value. To the right of the decimal point the places are tenths, hundredths and thousandths. The digit 4 in 3.47 is in the tenths place and contributes 0.4.

The place value is not the same as the digit. The digit tells how many of that place are present, and the place tells what each one is worth.

## Expanded sums

Expanded form writes a number as the sum of its place-value pieces. The number 3.47 is 3 + 0.4 + 0.07. The pieces may be written with multiplication, as 3 x 1 + 4 x 0.1 + 7 x 0.01.

Add the pieces to recover the standard number. If the sum does not match the original, one place value has been read incorrectly.

## Returning to standard form

To return from expanded form to a decimal, align the place values and add. Keep the decimal point in the same position for every piece. Trailing zeros after the final nonzero digit do not change the value.

A quick check is to count the digits after the decimal point. The number of decimal places in the standard form must match the smallest place value used in the expanded form.
