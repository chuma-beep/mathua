# Exponential functions from two points

An exponential function has the form \(y=ab^x\). Two points determine the initial value \(a\) and the growth factor \(b\).

## The growth factor

Divide the value at \(x=1\) by the value at \(x=0\) to get \(b\). More generally, divide values at consecutive integer inputs.

A growth factor above 1 means growth. A factor between 0 and 1 means decay.

## The initial value

Once \(b\) is known, substitute one point and solve for \(a\). The value at \(x=0\) is \(a\) itself when the point is available.

With points at \(x=1\) and \(x=2\), divide the later value by the earlier one to get \(b\), then divide again to get \(a\).

## Predicting a later value

Substitute the input into \(y=ab^x\). Each step multiplies by \(b\), so a value three steps ahead is \(ab^3\).

Do not add the growth factor repeatedly. Exponential change multiplies, it does not add a constant.

Doubling populations and compound interest both follow this pattern. The growth factor is the multiplier per period, not the amount added.
