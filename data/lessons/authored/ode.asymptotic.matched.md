# Matched asymptotic expansions

Matched asymptotics builds one approximation from an outer solution and an inner solution by requiring their overlapping limits to agree. The matching condition fixes the constants that the two expansions leave free.

## Outer and inner

The outer expansion uses the original variable and is valid away from the layer. The inner expansion uses the stretched variable and is valid inside it, and each carries its own free constants.

## The matching condition

The outer solution expanded near the boundary must equal the inner solution expanded far from it. The equality holds order by order in the parameter and determines the constants.

## Composite expansion

Adding the two expansions and subtracting their common limit produces one expression valid across the whole domain. The subtraction avoids counting the overlap twice.

To check your understanding, write the outer expansion, expand the outer solution near the layer and add the two expansions.

Matching is the step that turns two local descriptions into a single uniformly valid approximation.
