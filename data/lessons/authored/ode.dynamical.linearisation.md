# Linearisation at a fixed point

Near a fixed point the dynamics is approximated by the Jacobian matrix. The trace and determinant of that matrix classify the linear phase portrait without solving the system.

## The Jacobian

The Jacobian collects the first derivatives of the field at the fixed point. A small perturbation then evolves according to the linear system whose matrix is that Jacobian, to first order in the perturbation.

## Trace and determinant

The eigenvalues solve a quadratic whose coefficients are the trace and the determinant. A negative determinant gives a saddle, and a positive determinant splits into nodes and spirals according to the discriminant.

## When the approximation is valid

The linear picture is faithful when no eigenvalue has zero real part. Such a fixed point is called hyperbolic, and the next concept records the theorem that justifies the approximation.

To check your understanding, differentiate both components, compute the trace and compute the eigenvalues.

The trace and determinant are usually easier to compute than the eigenvalues, and they already give the qualitative portrait.
