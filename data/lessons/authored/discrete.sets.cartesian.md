# Cartesian products

The Cartesian product of two sets is the set of ordered pairs with the first coordinate from one set and the second from the other. Its size is the product of the sizes.

## Ordered pairs

\(A\times B=\{(a,b):a\in A,\ b\in B\}\). Order matters, so \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) are different unless the coordinates coincide.

## Size

For finite sets \(|A\times B|=|A|\cdot|B|\). The counting principle for independent choices is exactly this product rule.

## Relations and functions

A relation from \(A\) to \(B\) is a subset of \(A\times B\). A function is a relation with exactly one pair for each first coordinate.

To check your understanding, pair each element, multiply the sizes and take a subset of pairs.

The product rule of counting is the cardinality of a Cartesian product. Every finite counting argument can be phrased as a product of choices.
