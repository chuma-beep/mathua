# Dominant balance

Dominant balance chooses the scaling of a boundary layer or a singular solution by requiring at least two terms of the equation to be the same size. The exponents come from a small algebraic system rather than from guessing.

## The scaling ansatz

The independent and dependent variables are scaled by powers of the parameter. Substituting the ansatz multiplies each term by a different power, and the powers are read off from the exponents.

## Balancing exponents

Setting the exponents of two terms equal gives a linear equation for the scaling exponents. A second balance may be needed when three terms compete, and the consistent solution is the one that leaves no term larger.

## Consistency

After the exponents are chosen, every remaining term must be no larger than the balanced pair. A term that is larger means the balance was wrong and another pair must be selected.

To check your understanding, scale both variables, equate two exponents and compare the remaining terms.

Dominant balance is the general method behind boundary-layer and singular-solution scaling, and it is entirely algebra.
