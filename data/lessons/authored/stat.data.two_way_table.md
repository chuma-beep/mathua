# Two-way tables

A two-way table classifies every case by two categorical variables at once, so each cell counts the cases holding one level of each variable.

## Reading the cells

Each interior cell counts the cases that satisfy one level of each variable. Its position names both levels. A table of 60 commuters by mode and trip length puts 14 people in the bus cell for trips over 30 minutes. Those 14 are bus riders with long trips, not all bus riders. Saying both labels before quoting a cell prevents the error.

## Row and column totals

The margins hold the one-variable counts. A row total adds across a row to give the count for that level of the row variable. A column total does the same down a column. If the bus row holds 14 and 11, the row total is 25 bus riders whatever their trip length. The grand total must equal the sum of every row and column, which is the check that catches a transcription slip.

## A worked table

Take 18 and 7 in the first row, then 12 and 23 in the second. Row totals are 25 and 35, column totals are 30 and 30; the grand total is 60. The cell 18 is therefore 30 percent of the grand total and 72 percent of its row. A percentage within a row answers a different question from a percentage within a column.

The arithmetic is trivial, but the discipline is not, because every count carries two labels.
