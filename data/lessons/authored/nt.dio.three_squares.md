# Sums of three squares

Legendre's three-square theorem decides exactly which integers are sums of three squares. The obstruction is a single residue pattern modulo eight.

## The theorem

An integer is a sum of three squares unless it has the form four to the a times a number congruent to seven modulo eight. The power of four can be divided out.

## The obstruction

Numbers congruent to seven modulo eight cannot be written as three squares. Reducing modulo eight shows that three squares never sum to seven.

## Descent

If a number is a sum of three squares and divisible by four, all three squares are even. Dividing by four preserves representability, which is why the power of four is irrelevant.

To check your understanding, state the excluded form, reduce modulo eight and assume divisibility by four.

Three squares fail on one residue class modulo eight, and the failure survives multiplication by powers of four.
