# Cyclic subspaces

The cyclic subspace generated by a vector is the span of its repeated images under a linear map. Its dimension is the degree of the minimal polynomial of the vector.

## Generation

Starting from a vector v, take v, Tv, T squared v and so on. The span of these images is the cyclic subspace generated by v.

## Dimension

The images stay independent until the first linear relation appears. That first relation has degree equal to the dimension of the cyclic subspace.

## Companion block

On a cyclic subspace the map has a companion matrix. The rational canonical form assembles a map from such blocks.

To check your understanding, iterate the map, find the first relation and write the block.

Cyclic subspaces reduce a linear map to a single vector and its orbit.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
