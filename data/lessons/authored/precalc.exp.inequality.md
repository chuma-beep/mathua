# Exponential inequalities

An exponential inequality compares powers and is solved by matching bases or taking logarithms. The direction of the inequality depends on whether the base is above or below one.

## Matching bases

Rewrite both sides with the same base. If the base exceeds one the inequality keeps its direction; if it lies between zero and one the direction reverses.

## Logarithms

Taking a logarithm of both sides is valid because logarithms are increasing. A base smaller than one needs the reversal rule applied explicitly.

## Integer solutions

When the variable counts discrete steps, solve the real inequality first and then round in the direction the inequality requires.

To check your understanding, rewrite both sides, take the log and solve over the reals.

Check the base before trusting the direction of the inequality sign.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
