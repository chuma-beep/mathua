# The Hartman-Grobman theorem

The Hartman-Grobman theorem says a hyperbolic fixed point and its linearisation have the same phase portrait up to a homeomorphism. The nonlinear terms bend the picture but do not change its topology.

## Hyperbolicity

The theorem needs every eigenvalue of the Jacobian to have a non-zero real part. That condition rules out a centre, where the linear and nonlinear portraits can genuinely differ.

## Topological conjugacy

A homeomorphism defined near the fixed point carries trajectories of the nonlinear flow onto trajectories of the linear flow. It preserves order in time and the topology of the orbits, not their shape.

## What is not preserved

Angles between orbits, distances and periods are not preserved. A linear centre can become a nonlinear spiral with a very different rate, which is why a zero real part needs a separate analysis.

To check your understanding, compute the eigenvalues, state the conjugacy and compare a centre with a spiral.

The theorem is the licence to classify a nonlinear fixed point by its Jacobian, and the licence expires exactly at the imaginary axis.
