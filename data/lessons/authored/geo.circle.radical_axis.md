# The radical axis

## Equal powers

The power of a point with respect to a circle measures how the point sits relative to the circle. Points with equal power with respect to two circles lie on a line called the radical axis.

For two circles, the radical axis is perpendicular to the line joining the centers. When the radii are equal, it is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining the centers.

## The radical axis line

Subtracting the two circle equations eliminates the squared terms and leaves a linear equation. That line is the radical axis. Its position depends on the centers and the radii.

A point on the radical axis has the same power with respect to both circles. Test a point by computing both powers and comparing them.

## Intersections

Two circles meet in two points, one point or no points depending on the distance between their centers compared with the sum and difference of the radii. The radical axis passes through the intersection points when they exist.

The radical axis still exists when the circles do not meet. It is a real line even when the intersection count is zero.
