# Hemispheres

## Volume

A hemisphere is half a sphere. Its volume is half of four thirds pi r cubed, which simplifies to two thirds pi r cubed.

Use the same radius as the full sphere. The flat circular face does not change the radius.

## Curved surface

The curved surface area of a hemisphere is half the surface area of the full sphere, or two pi r squared. This counts only the dome, not the flat circular base.

Read the question carefully. Curved surface area and total surface area differ by the area of the base.

## Total surface

The total surface area adds the flat circular base to the curved surface. The base has area pi r squared, so the total is three pi r squared.

Check that the total is larger than the curved area. The difference must equal the area of the base.

A final check is to restate the answer in the units and context of the original question. If the result is plausible and the units match, the calculation is ready.

A hemisphere is not a sphere with half the surface area of every kind. The dome and the flat base are different surfaces, and a question that asks for surface area must say which one it means. The curved area is two pi r squared, the base is pi r squared and the total is three pi r squared. When you check the answer, compare the total with the curved area and confirm that the difference is exactly the base. This check catches the common error of doubling the curved area or forgetting the flat face.
