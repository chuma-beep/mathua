# The laws of logarithms

Logarithms turn products into sums, quotients into differences and powers into factors. These three rules are the reason logarithms simplify exponential equations.

## Product and quotient

The log of a product is the sum of the logs and the log of a quotient is the difference, both in the same base.

## Power rule

An exponent inside the logarithm moves to the front as a multiplier, which is what allows an unknown exponent to be isolated.

## Validity

Every argument must stay positive. Combining logs can hide a domain restriction that the original expression had.

To check your understanding, split a product, move the exponent and require positive arguments.

Expand before solving and condense only after checking the domains.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
