# Comparing mixed numbers by whole part

A mixed number has a whole part and a fractional part and the whole part dominates the comparison. Two mixed numbers with different whole parts are ordered by those wholes alone.

## The method

Compare the whole parts first. If they differ, the larger whole part gives the larger mixed number, no matter how the fractions compare. If the whole parts are equal, compare the fractional parts by a common denominator or by cross multiplication. A larger whole part wins even when its fraction is very small. Since 3 is less than 4, the number \(3\frac{7}{8}\) is smaller than \(4\frac{1}{8}\).

## A worked example

Compare \(3\frac{2}{5}\) with \(3\frac{5}{8}\). The wholes are equal, so compare the fractions: \(\frac{2}{5} = \frac{16}{40}\) and \(\frac{5}{8} = \frac{25}{40}\), so the second is larger. Now compare \(4\frac{1}{8}\) with \(3\frac{7}{8}\). The whole parts are 4 and 3, so \(4\frac{1}{8}\) is larger even though its fraction is smaller.

## Checking the result

Cross multiplying the fractions confirms the first case, since \(2 \times 8 = 16\) is less than \(5 \times 5 = 25\). In decimals the four values are 3.4, 3.625, 4.125 and 3.875, so the order is \(3\frac{2}{5}\), then \(3\frac{5}{8}\), then \(3\frac{7}{8}\), then \(4\frac{1}{8}\).

The whole part decides first and the fraction decides only a tie.
