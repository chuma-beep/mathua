# Percentage points

## Points versus percent

A percentage point is the arithmetic difference between two percentages. A rate that moves from 20 percent to 25 percent has risen by 5 percentage points. This is different from a 25 percent relative increase.

Keep the two descriptions separate. Points describe the gap on the percentage scale. Percent change describes the size of the gap relative to the starting rate.

## Converting points to percent

To convert a point change into a percent change, divide the point change by the old rate and multiply by 100. A 5 point rise from 20 percent is 5/20 x 100 = 25 percent.

The denominator is always the old rate, not the new rate. Using the new rate gives a different number and answers a different question. Write the old rate first so the denominator is clear.

## Recovering the old rate

If the new rate and the point change are known, subtract the change for a rise or add it for a fall. The result is the old rate. Check by recomputing the point difference from the two rates.

Do not confuse a point change with a percent change when the question uses the word points. The word points is a signal to subtract the percentages directly.
