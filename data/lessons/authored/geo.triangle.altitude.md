# Altitudes of a triangle

## Altitude from area

An altitude is a segment from a vertex perpendicular to the line containing the opposite side. The length of that segment is the height used in the area formula A = bh / 2. Rearrange the formula to h = 2A / b. A triangle with area 36 and base 9 has height 2(36) / 9 = 8. The base and the altitude must be perpendicular.

## Area from an altitude

Multiply the base by the altitude and divide by 2. A triangle with base 14 and altitude 6 has area 14 x 6 / 2 = 42. Any side can be used as the base, but the altitude must be the perpendicular distance to that side. The area is the same whichever base and matching altitude you choose.

## Altitudes outside the triangle

In an obtuse triangle, two altitudes fall outside the triangle. The foot of an altitude can lie on an extension of the opposite side. If the base is 14 and the foot lies 4 units beyond an endpoint, the distance from the foot to the far endpoint is 14 + 4 = 18. A sketch prevents the outside case from being treated as an inside segment.
