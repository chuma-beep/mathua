# Ratios in similar figures

## Corresponding Sides

Similar figures have the same shape, so every side of one matches a side of the other in a fixed ratio. Match sides by position and angle, not by length. Two triangles with corresponding sides 6 and 9 have a ratio of \\(6 \\div 9 = \\frac{2}{3}\\), which means the larger is 1.5 times the smaller.

Write the ratio in one direction and keep that direction for every pair. Mixing the order produces a scale factor that is upside down. Every later answer then inherits the error.

## Finding a Missing Side

A proportion turns one known pair into any missing side. If the smaller triangle has a side of 8 that corresponds to an unknown side, use \\(\\frac{6}{9} = \\frac{8}{x}\\). Cross multiplication gives \\(6x = 72\\), so \\(x = 12\\).

The answer is larger than 8, as it must be, because the other triangle is larger. Check the ratio of the new pair: 8 divided by 12 is also two thirds, which confirms the match.

## Perimeters of Similar Figures

Perimeters add lengths, so they scale by the same factor as the sides. A small figure with perimeter 20 pairs with a large figure of perimeter 30 when the scale factor is 1.5.

Areas do not follow that rule, because area counts square units and both dimensions grow. An area scales by the square of the factor, so a factor of 1.5 multiplies area by 2.25. Confusing the two is the commonest mistake with similar figures.
