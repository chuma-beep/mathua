# Signed angles and clockwise rotation

A signed angle records direction as well as size, with counterclockwise rotation positive and clockwise rotation negative.

## Positive and negative rotation

The sign attaches to the direction of the turn, so the same terminal side can be reached two ways.
An angle of -60 degrees is a clockwise turn of 60 degrees; its terminal side is the same as that
of 300 degrees. The sine follows the sign of the rotation: \(\sin(-60) = -0.8660\) and
\(\sin 300 = -0.8660\), both equal to \(-\sqrt{3}/2\).

## Coterminal angles

Angles that differ by whole turns are coterminal and share every trigonometric value. The family
for 45 degrees is \(45 + 360k\): 45, 405 and 765 going up; -315 and -675 going down. The family
for 300 degrees is 300, 660 and -60. Each member has the same sine, cosine and tangent, which is
why a solution set is usually written as a family rather than a list.

## Reducing a large negative angle

Add whole turns until the angle lands in a convenient range. For -1000 degrees, three turns add
1080, giving 80 degrees, so -1000 and 80 are coterminal and
\(\cos(-1000) = \cos 80 \approx 0.1736\). For -750 degrees, two turns add 720 and leave -30, which
is coterminal with 330. The cosine of that angle is 0.8660.

Reduce first, then evaluate. Keep the sign of the reduced angle throughout.
