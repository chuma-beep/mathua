# Fatou's lemma

Fatou's lemma gives a lower bound for the limit inferior of integrals. It does not require monotonicity, only nonnegativity.

## The lower bound

The limit inferior of the integrals is at least the integral of the pointwise limit inferior. The inequality can be strict. It is the first convergence theorem that does not assume monotonicity.

## A strict example

Tall narrow rectangles can have constant integral while the pointwise limit is zero. The integral of the limit is zero, but the limit of the integrals is positive. This shows why the inequality is not equality.

## Nonnegative sequences

Fatou's lemma applies to nonnegative functions. A constant nonnegative function has integral equal to its value times the measure of the domain. The lower bound is exact in that case.
