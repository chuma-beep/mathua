# Equations of spheres

## Centre and radius

The standard equation of a sphere is (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 + (z - l)^2 = r^2. The center is (h, k, l) and the radius is the square root of the right side.

Read the signs carefully. The equation (x + 3)^2 has center x = -3, not 3. The sign inside the parentheses is the opposite of the center coordinate.

## Building the equation

To build an equation from a center and a radius, square the radius and subtract the center coordinates inside each squared term. Expand only if the question asks for the general form.

A sphere with a diameter has its center at the midpoint of the diameter. The radius is half the diameter length.

## Testing a point

A point lies on the sphere when its distance from the center equals the radius. Compute the squared distance and compare it with r squared. A larger value is outside and a smaller value is inside.

Squared distance avoids square roots and keeps the arithmetic exact. Compare the squared distance directly with the squared radius.

A final check is to restate the answer in the units and context of the original question. If the result is plausible and the units match, the calculation is ready.
