# Signed measures

A signed measure takes values of either sign while staying countably additive. The Jordan decomposition splits it into two ordinary measures.

## Positive and negative parts

Every signed measure is a difference \(\nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-\) of two positive measures, at least one of them finite. For the density \(f(t) = t - 1/2\) on \([0,1]\), the positive part integrates \(f\) where it is positive and the negative part integrates \(-f\) where it is negative.

## Hahn decomposition

The space splits into a positive set \(P\) and a negative set \(N\) with \(\nu \ge 0\) on subsets of \(P\) and \(\nu \le 0\) on subsets of \(N\). For \(f(t) = t - 1/2\) the split is at \(1/2\). Each part has measure \(1/8\), and the total mass of the interval is 0.

## Total variation

The total variation is \(|\nu| = \nu^+ + \nu^-\). Here it is \(1/4\), even though \(\nu([0,1]) = 0\). The variation measures movement rather than net value, which is why a density that goes up and comes back has positive variation.

A signed measure carries a direction, and the Hahn decomposition finds the two directions and the variation totals them.
