# Holder's inequality for integrals

## The inequality

Holder's inequality bounds an integral of a product by two separate integrals: \(\int |fg| \le (\int |f|^p)^{1/p}(\int |g|^q)^{1/q}\). Equality holds when \(|f|^p\) is proportional to \(|g|^q\), which is the case of proportional powers throughout the interval. The discrete and integral forms are proved the same way, from Young's inequality for a product of two numbers.

## Conjugate exponents

The exponents are conjugate, meaning \(1/p + 1/q = 1\). The pairs \(p = 2, q = 2\) and \(p = 3, q = 3/2\) both qualify, since \(1/3 + 2/3 = 1\). Cauchy-Schwarz is the case \(p = q = 2\), the most used member of the family.

## A worked pair

A worked pair: \(f(x) = x\) and \(g(x) = 1\) on the interval from 0 to 1 with \(p = q = 2\). The left side is \(\int_0^1 x\,dx = 1/2\). The right side is \(\sqrt{1/3}\) times \(\sqrt{1}\), about 0.5774. The bound holds. The gap is real rather than a rounding effect.
