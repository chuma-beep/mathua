# Initial and final value theorems

The initial and final value theorems read limiting values of a time function from its Laplace transform.

## Read the initial value

The initial value is the limit of s times the transform as s tends to infinity. The limit must exist for the theorem to apply. A transform with a polynomial numerator of lower degree gives zero.

## Read the final value

The final value is the limit of s times the transform as s tends to zero. All poles of the transform must lie in the open left half-plane. A pole on the imaginary axis or in the right half-plane makes the final value theorem invalid.

## Check the limit conditions

Verify that the time function has a limit and that the poles satisfy the stability condition. The theorems give a quick check but not a substitute for the inverse transform. A failed hypothesis is the usual reason for a wrong answer.

A complete answer states the governing relation, substitutes the given values and reads the result in the language of the problem. Where a second method is available, compare the two results before moving to the next question. The three sections are one chain, and the practice sequence asks one family from each section so the learner keeps the definition, the computation and the interpretation connected.
