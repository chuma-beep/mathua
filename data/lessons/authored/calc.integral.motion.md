# Motion problems with integrals

For a particle with position \(s(t)\), velocity is the derivative and displacement is the integral of velocity.

## Velocity and position

Velocity is \(s'(t)\). Position is an antiderivative of velocity, determined up to a constant.

For \(s(t)=t^2+bt\), velocity is \(2t+b\).

The constant of integration is fixed by an initial position.

## Displacement

Displacement over \([a,c]\) is \(s(c)-s(a)\), which also equals \(\int_a^c v(t)dt\).

Displacement is a signed quantity. It can be zero even when the particle moved.

Distance travelled is the integral of speed, which is different from displacement.

## Average velocity

Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. It is not the average of the endpoint velocities in general.

For a linear velocity, the average over an interval equals the velocity at the midpoint.

Average speed uses distance, while average velocity uses displacement.

If velocity is linear, average velocity equals the velocity at the midpoint of the time interval. That midpoint rule is a useful check.
