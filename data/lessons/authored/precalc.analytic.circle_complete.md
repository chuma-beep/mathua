# Completing the square for circles

A circle written in general form reveals its centre and radius after completing the square in both variables. The process turns a quadratic expression into a squared binomial plus a constant.

## Completing the square

Group the x terms and the y terms, add and subtract the square of half each linear coefficient, then collect the constants.

## Reading the centre

The completed form shows the centre as the numbers subtracted from x and y, with signs reversed from the parentheses.

## The radius

The constant on the right is the square of the radius, so the radius is its positive square root. A negative constant means no real circle.

To check your understanding, group the terms, read the shifts and isolate the constant.

A circle needs equal coefficients on x squared and y squared before completing the square.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
