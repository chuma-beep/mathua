# Associates and factorisation

Associates differ by multiplication by a unit, so every nonzero Gaussian integer has four associates, all with the same norm.

## The associate class

The associates of \(z\) are \(z,-z,iz,-iz\). They occupy four points of the square lattice at the same distance from the origin.

## Norm and associates

Multiplication by a unit does not change the norm, so all four associates share \(N(z)\).

## Choosing a representative

A factorisation is unique only after associates are identified, so a canonical representative is chosen by a rule such as positive real part.

To check your understanding, list the four associates, compare the norms and pick a canonical associate.

Associates are the Gaussian version of a sign, and factorisation is unique only up to them.
