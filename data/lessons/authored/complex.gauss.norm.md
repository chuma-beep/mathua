# Norm in the Gaussian integers

The norm N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2 turns multiplication of Gaussian integers into multiplication of ordinary integers. It measures squared distance from the origin and decides which Gaussian integers are units or primes.

## Definition

The norm of \(z=a+bi\) is \(N(z)=z\bar z=a^{2}+b^{2}\). It is nonnegative and vanishes only at zero.

## Geometry

\(N(z)\) is the squared distance from the origin, so the Gaussian integers of norm one are the four points on the unit circle.

## Arithmetic

The norm is multiplicative, \(N(zw)=N(z)N(w)\), because conjugation reverses the order of a product.

To check your understanding, compute the norm from the parts, read the norm as squared distance and multiply two norms.

The norm is the bridge between Gaussian arithmetic and ordinary integer arithmetic.
