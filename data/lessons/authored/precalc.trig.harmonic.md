# Harmonic motion

Simple harmonic motion is a sinusoidal displacement with a fixed amplitude, period and phase. Springs, pendulums and alternating currents all follow the same model.

## The model

Displacement equals amplitude times cosine of angular frequency times time plus phase, optionally shifted by an equilibrium offset.

## Period and frequency

The period is two pi divided by the angular frequency and the frequency is its reciprocal, so period and frequency carry the same information.

## Reading parameters

The maximum displacement is the amplitude, the equilibrium is the midline and the starting position is the value at time zero.

To check your understanding, write the cosine, divide two pi and read the amplitude.

Harmonic motion is the projection of uniform circular motion onto a line.

Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller coefficients and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
