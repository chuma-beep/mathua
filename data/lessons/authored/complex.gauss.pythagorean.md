# Pythagorean triples from squares

Squaring a+bi gives (a^2-b^2)+2abi, and its modulus gives a Pythagorean triple. Every primitive triple arises this way.

## Squaring

The square of \(a+bi\) has real part \(a^{2}-b^{2}\) and imaginary part \(2ab\), so its norm is \((a^{2}+b^{2})^{2}\).

## The triple

The legs are \(|a^{2}-b^{2}|\) and \(2ab\), and the hypotenuse is \(a^{2}+b^{2}\).

## Perimeter and hypotenuse

The perimeter is the sum of the two legs and the hypotenuse, and the hypotenuse is the norm of \(a+bi\).

To check your understanding, compute the real part, read the legs and sum the three sides.

One complex square produces one Pythagorean triple, with the norm as hypotenuse.
