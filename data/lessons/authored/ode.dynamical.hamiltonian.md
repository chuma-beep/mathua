# Hamiltonian systems

A Hamiltonian system conserves its Hamiltonian and preserves phase-space volume. Its trajectories lie on level sets of the Hamiltonian, so the phase portrait is a contour map.

## Hamilton's equations

The position derivative is the momentum derivative of the Hamiltonian and the momentum derivative is minus the position derivative. The mixed signs are what make the field divergence-free.

## Conservation

Differentiating the Hamiltonian along a trajectory and substituting the equations gives zero. The Hamiltonian is therefore constant on every orbit, and each orbit stays on one level set.

## Volume and spectra

The divergence of a Hamiltonian field is zero, so the flow preserves area in the plane and volume in higher dimensions. A linearised Hamiltonian system has eigenvalues in opposite pairs, which rules out a stable node.

To check your understanding, write both equations, differentiate the Hamiltonian and compute the divergence.

The level sets explain why a Hamiltonian system has no attractor, and the eigenvalue pairing explains why its fixed points are saddles or centres.
