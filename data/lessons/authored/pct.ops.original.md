# Finding the original amount

## Recovering after an increase

A percent change starts from an original amount. The new amount is the original plus or minus a percentage of it. If a price rises by 25%, the new price is 125% of the original. Write 125% as 1.25 and divide the new price by 1.25. A new price of 150 gives an original of 150 / 1.25 = 120. Subtracting 25% of 150 would give 112.50, which is too small because the percentage was taken from the original.

## Recovering after a decrease

If a price falls by 20%, the new price is 80% of the original. Divide by 0.80. A new price of 64 gives an original of 64 / 0.80 = 80. The multiplier is always 1 plus the rate for an increase and 1 minus the rate for a decrease. Keep the multiplier greater than zero, because a decrease of 100% would leave nothing.

## Checking the result

Apply the original percent change to the recovered amount and compare with the stated new amount. For an increase, add the same percentage of the original. For a decrease, subtract it. The check catches the common mistake of applying the percentage to the new amount. A correct recovery always returns the stated new value.
