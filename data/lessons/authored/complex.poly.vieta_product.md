# Products of roots

The product of the roots is (-1)^n times the constant term divided by the leading coefficient.

## Quadratic

For \(az^{2}+bz+c\) the product of the roots is \(c/a\).

## Cubic

For \(az^{3}+bz^{2}+cz+d\) the product is \(-d/a\), because three roots contribute three sign changes.

## Roots of unity

The product of the \(n\)-th roots of unity is \((-1)^{n+1}\), read from the constant term of \(z^{n}-1\).

To check your understanding, read the constant term, apply the cubic sign and use the constant term.

The sign in the product formula alternates with the degree.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
