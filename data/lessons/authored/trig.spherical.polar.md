# Polar spherical triangles

The polar triangle of a spherical triangle has its vertices at the poles of the original sides, so its angles are the supplements of the original sides.

## The polar construction

For a triangle with vertices \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\), take the pole of side \(BC\) that lies on the
same side of that side as \(A\), naming it \(A'\). Do the same for the other two sides. Every side
of the polar triangle is the supplement of an angle of the original, so an angle of 60 degrees
produces a side of 120 degrees.

## Sides from angles

The correspondence runs both ways, so \(a' = 180 - A\) in degrees always. A triangle with angles
70, 80 and 90 has polar sides 110, 100 and 90. The polar triangle of a triangle with a right angle
therefore has a side of 90 degrees, so the polar of a right triangle is again right.

## Summing the polar sides

Adding the three relations gives \(a' + b' + c' = 540 - (A + B + C)\). For the triangle with
angles 70, 80 and 90 the polar sides sum to 300, which is 540 - 240. The polar angle sum runs the
other way: if the original sides are 60, 70 and 80, summing to 210, the polar angles are 120, 110
and 100 and sum to 330, which is 540 - 210.

The two triangles carry exactly the same information with sides and angles exchanged.
