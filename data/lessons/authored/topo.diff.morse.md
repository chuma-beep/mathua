# Morse inequalities

The Morse inequalities compare the counts of critical points with the Betti numbers. They force every Morse function to have at least as many critical points as the total homology.

## The weak inequality

For each index \(k\), the count \(m_k\) is at least the Betti number \(b_k\). A Morse function on the torus needs at least one critical point in indices zero, one and two.

## The alternating sum

The alternating sum of the critical point counts equals the Euler characteristic. On a surface of genus \(g\), the sum \(m_0 - m_1 + m_2\) equals \(2 - 2g\).

## Total critical points

Summing the weak inequalities gives at least \(\sum b_k\) critical points. The two-sphere needs at least two, and the torus at least four.

To check your understanding, compare counts with Betti numbers, compute the alternating sum and add the Betti numbers.

Critical points cannot be fewer than the homology requires.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
