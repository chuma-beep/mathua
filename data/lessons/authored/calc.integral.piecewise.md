# Integrals of piecewise functions

A definite integral over a piecewise function is the sum of the integrals over the pieces.

## Splitting at the break

Locate the point where the formula changes. Split the integral at that point.

Use the branch that is active on each subinterval. Do not apply one formula across the break.

The break belongs to exactly one branch, so the split is clean.

## Constant pieces

On a constant piece, the integral is the value times the length of the interval.

For \(f(x)=a\) on \([0,c]\), the contribution is \(ac\). For \(f(x)=b\) on \([c,d]\), it is \(b(d-c)\).

Constant pieces are rectangles, so no antiderivative is needed.

## Adding the contributions

Add the pieces. The total is independent of the order in which the pieces are integrated.

Check that the intervals meet at the break and do not overlap. A gap or overlap changes the total.

A quick sketch of the two rectangles checks the arithmetic.

For example, \(f(x)=2\) on \([0,3]\) and \(f(x)=5\) on \([3,7]\) integrates to \(2·3+5·4=26\).
