# Interval graphs

Interval graphs belongs to graph_theory.basics. This lesson separates the idea into three question families: interval representation, clique number and recognition. Each section names the object, shows the computation and states how to check the result.

## Interval representation

The first family asks for interval representation. Start by writing the definition, recurrence or condition that connects the given data to the requested integer. Substitute the values and reduce the expression step by step. A useful check is to vary one input and confirm that the answer changes in the direction the definition predicts.

## Clique number

The second family asks for clique number. Use the same object from a different angle: reverse the relation, count the admissible choices or compare two candidate answers. Keep the assumptions visible, because a condition that is easy to overlook is often the whole content of the question. State the resulting integer and explain why the alternative would fail.

## Recognition

The third family asks for recognition. This is the interpretation or synthesis step. Connect the calculation to the original definition, check the range or sign of the result and say what the answer means for the problem. If a second method is available, compare the two results before moving on.

The three sections form one progression. The first fixes the definition, the second performs the computation and the third reads the result in context. Practice asks one family from each section, so a learner who can move through all three has understood interval graphs, not just memorised a formula.
