# The Dirichlet eta function

## Alternating the zeta series

The eta function alternates the zeta terms: \(\eta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n-1} n^{-s}\). At \(s = 1\) the series is \(1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - 1/4 + \cdots\), whose first four terms total 0.5833 and whose limit is \(\ln 2 \approx 0.6931\). Alternation makes the series converge at \(s = 1\), where zeta fails.

## Relation to the zeta function

Splitting the terms into even and odd parts gives \(\eta(s) = (1 - 2^{1-s})\zeta(s)\). At \(s = 2\) the factor is \(1 - 1/2 = 0.5\), so \(\eta(2) = 0.5 \times 1.6449 = 0.8225\). The direct partial sum after four terms is 0.7986, closing on the same value.

## Values at even integers

Combining the two formulas gives exact values at even integers. \(\eta(2) = \pi^2/12 \approx 0.8225\) and \(\eta(4) = 7\pi^4/720 \approx 0.9470\). The factor \(1 - 2^{1-s}\) grows with \(s\), so eta approaches zeta: at \(s = 10\) the factor is \(1 - 2^{-9} \approx 0.998\).
