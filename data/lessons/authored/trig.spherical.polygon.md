# Area of a spherical polygon

On a sphere of unit radius the area of a spherical polygon equals its angle excess, the amount by which its angle sum exceeds the plane value.

## Excess of a polygon

For a polygon with \(n\) sides the plane angle sum is \((n-2)180\) degrees; the excess is the
difference. A quadrilateral on the unit sphere with angles 100, 110, 120 and 130 degrees has sum
460, against a plane sum of 360, so its excess is 100 degrees. In radians that is
\(100\pi/180 \approx 1.745\). The area is 1.745 square units.

## Equal-angle polygons

When every angle is equal the excess is easy to read. A regular pentagon with each angle 120
degrees has sum 600 against a plane sum of 540, so the excess is 60 degrees, which is about 1.047
radians. A regular hexagon would need each angle above 120 degrees to enclose any area, since 120
is the plane value.

## From excess to area

On a sphere of radius \(R\) the area is \(R^{2}\) times the excess in radians. The quadrilateral
above, on a sphere of radius 2, has area \(4(1.745) \approx 6.981\). A lune with angle 60 degrees
has excess 120 degrees, because both of its angles count. Its area on the unit sphere is
\(4\pi(60/360) \approx 2.094\).

Measure the excess in radians before multiplying by the square of the radius.
