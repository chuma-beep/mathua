# The incenter

## Angle bisectors

The angle bisectors of a triangle meet at the incenter. The incenter is equidistant from all three sides, and that common distance is the inradius.

The incenter always lies inside the triangle. Unlike the orthocenter, it does not move outside for an obtuse triangle.

## Inradius from area

The area of a triangle is the inradius times the semiperimeter. Rearranging gives r = A/s. This formula works for every triangle and is often faster than constructing the angle bisectors.

Compute the semiperimeter by adding the three sides and dividing by 2. Keep the area and the semiperimeter in the same square and linear units before dividing.

## Coordinates in a right triangle

For a right triangle with legs on the axes, the incenter is at (r, r). The distance from the incenter to each leg is the inradius. This gives a quick coordinate formula when the legs are known.

Check the coordinates by measuring the distance to the hypotenuse. The perpendicular distance must also equal r.
