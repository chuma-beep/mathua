# Separation in polar coordinates

In a disk the Laplacian separates into a radial equation and an angular eigenvalue problem. The angular problem is periodic, so its eigenvalues are squares of integers.

## The Laplacian in polar form

The radial part carries a first derivative divided by the radius and the angular part carries a second derivative divided by the radius squared. Multiplying by the radius squared clears the denominators.

## The angular problem

Separation produces a second-order equation for the angle with a negative separation constant. Periodicity over a full turn forces the constant to be a square of an integer.

## The radial solution

The radial equation is equidimensional, so its solutions are powers of the radius. Regularity at the centre removes the negative power and leaves the positive one.

To check your understanding, write the polar Laplacian, impose periodicity and write the radial equation.

Separation works because the disk has a symmetry, and the angular eigenvalues are quantised by that symmetry rather than by any boundary condition.
