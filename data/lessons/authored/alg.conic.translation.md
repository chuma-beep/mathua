# Translated conic equations

A conic can be moved without changing its shape. Replacing \(x\) by \(x - h\) and \(y\) by \(y - k\) translates the graph so that its center moves from the origin to \((h, k)\).

## Reading the center

The standard ellipse
\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1
\]
is centered at the origin. The translated ellipse
\[
\frac{(x - h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} = 1
\]
is centered at \((h, k)\). Each squared term vanishes at the corresponding coordinate of the center. For example,
\[
\frac{(x - 3)^2}{25} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{9} = 1
\]
is centered at \((3, -2)\). The plus sign inside the second square means the center has \(y = -2\), not \(y = 2\).

## The standard form

To read a translated conic, compare each numerator with the pattern \((x - h)^2\) and \((y - k)^2\). A term \((x + 4)^2\) is \((x - (-4))^2\), so its center coordinate is \(-4\). The denominators give the squares of the semi-axes, so \(a\) and \(b\) are the positive square roots of those denominators.

## Center coordinates and sums

Once the center \((h, k)\) is known, questions about the center reduce to arithmetic. The sum of the coordinates is \(h + k\), and the midpoint of the major axis is the same point. Translation changes the center but not the lengths \(a\) and \(b\), so the shape and orientation stay fixed.
