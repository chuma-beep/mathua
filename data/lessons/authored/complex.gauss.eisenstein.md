# Eisenstein integers

Eisenstein integers are a+bω with ω^3=1 and 1+ω+ω^2=0. Their norm is a^2-ab+b^2 and there are six units.

## The ring

\(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\) satisfies \(\omega^{2}+\omega+1=0\), so the Eisenstein integers form a triangular lattice.

## The norm

The norm of \(a+b\omega\) is \(a^{2}-ab+b^{2}\), the squared distance to the origin in that lattice.

## Units and primes

The six units are \(\pm1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^{2}\). The ring is Euclidean, so it has unique factorisation up to units.

To check your understanding, use the cube-root relation, compute the norm and count the units.

The Eisenstein integers repeat the Gaussian story on a triangular lattice.
