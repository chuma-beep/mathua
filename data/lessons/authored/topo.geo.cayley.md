# Cayley graphs

The Cayley graph of a finitely generated group records multiplication by each generator. Its geometry reflects the algebra, and its degree is twice the number of generators for a free generating set.

## Degree

For the group \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) with its standard generators, the Cayley graph is the \(n\)-dimensional grid. Each vertex has \(2n\) neighbours, so the degree is \(2n\).

## Free groups

The free group \(F_k\) has a Cayley graph that is a \(2k\)-regular tree. The degree is twice the rank, and the graph is a tree because the group has no relations.

## Generators

The rank of a free abelian group equals the number of standard generators. A Cayley graph of \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) therefore has \(n\) generator directions and \(2n\) edges at each vertex.

To check your understanding, double the dimension, double the rank and count the directions.

The Cayley graph is the group's multiplication table drawn as a metric space.
