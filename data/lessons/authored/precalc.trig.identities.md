# Pythagorean identities

The Pythagorean identities follow from the unit circle. Dividing the defining identity by cosine squared or sine squared produces the tangent and cotangent forms.

## The main identity

Sine squared plus cosine squared equals one for every angle, because the point lies on the unit circle.

## Dividing through

Dividing by cosine squared gives one plus tangent squared equals secant squared. Dividing by sine squared gives one plus cotangent squared equals cosecant squared.

## Using an identity

Replace a squared trigonometric function by one minus its partner, which simplifies expressions and converts equations into a single function.

To check your understanding, square sine and cosine, divide by cosine squared and choose a replacement.

Every form is the same circle equation in different coordinates.
