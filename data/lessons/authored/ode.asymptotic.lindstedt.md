# The Poincare-Lindstedt method

The Poincare-Lindstedt method removes secular terms from a weakly nonlinear oscillator by letting the frequency depend on the amplitude. The expansion stays periodic at every order instead of drifting in phase.

## Strained coordinate

Time is rescaled by a frequency that is itself expanded in the parameter. The unknown frequency corrections are chosen together with the solution, so periodicity becomes a condition on them.

## Choosing the frequency correction

The first correction removes the resonant cosine from the first-order equation. With that choice the correction is periodic, and the frequency shift is proportional to the square of the amplitude for a symmetric nonlinearity.

## The result

The oscillation keeps a fixed period at each order, and the period depends on amplitude. That amplitude dependence is the hallmark of a nonlinear oscillator and agrees with the exact pendulum period at small amplitude.

To check your understanding, rescale time by the frequency, find the resonant term and write the period.

Straining the time replaces the growing secular term with a physically meaningful frequency shift.
