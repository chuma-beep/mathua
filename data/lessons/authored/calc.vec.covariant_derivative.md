# Covariant derivatives

A covariant derivative differentiates a vector field along a direction while respecting the geometry.

## Differentiating along a curve

Along a curve the covariant derivative adds a correction to each component, built from the Christoffel symbol, the velocity and the field.

The extra term compensates for the turning of the basis. A field that looks constant in components may still be changing.

In polar coordinates the field \(\partial_\phi\) differentiated along itself gives \(-r\,\partial_r\), so at \(r=3\) the correction is -3.

## The covariant derivative in coordinates

For each direction the derivative of a component gains a term with the symbols and the components.

On a vector field the symbols act on one index. On a one-form they enter with the opposite sign. The result is a tensor.

On the unit sphere the correction to \(\partial_\phi\) in the \(\phi\) direction has radial part \(-\sin\theta\cos\theta\), which at \(\theta=\pi/4\) is -0.5.

## Parallel transport

A field is parallel transported when its covariant derivative along the curve vanishes.

On a plane this reproduces the constant fields. On the unit sphere transport around a latitude circle at colatitude \(\theta\) rotates the field by \(2\pi(1-\cos\theta)\).

At \(\theta=\pi/3\) the rotation is \(\pi\), a half turn. That rotation is the holonomy, which vanishes on a flat space.
