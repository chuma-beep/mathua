# Estimating a difference of fractions

An estimate for a difference rounds both fractions to nearby benchmarks before subtracting. It tells whether the answer is near zero, near one half or near a whole.

## The method

Round the minuend and the subtrahend separately to 0, \(\frac{1}{2}\) or 1. Subtract the rounded values. When both round in the same direction the error partly cancels and when they round in opposite directions the estimate moves by the sum of the two errors. A benchmark within one eighth of a fraction is usually close enough for a mental estimate.

## A worked difference

Estimate \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{5}{12}\). The first fraction rounds to 1 and the second to \(\frac{1}{2}\), so the estimate is \(\frac{1}{2}\). The exact difference over twenty fourths is \(\frac{21}{24} - \frac{10}{24} = \frac{11}{24}\), which is about 0.458. The estimate is high by \(\frac{1}{24}\), a small amount, because the two roundings moved in opposite directions.

## Checking the result

Bracket the answer. Replacing the subtrahend by \(\frac{1}{2}\) gives the lower bound \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{8}\) and replacing it by \(\frac{1}{3}\) gives an upper bound near \(\frac{13}{24}\). The exact value \(\frac{11}{24}\) lies between \(\frac{9}{24}\) and \(\frac{13}{24}\), so the estimate is credible.

A difference estimate is useful when it comes with a bracket.
