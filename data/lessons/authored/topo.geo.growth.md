# Growth rates of groups

The growth function counts elements inside a ball of radius \(n\) in the Cayley graph. Abelian groups grow polynomially, free groups grow exponentially, and the degree is a quasi-isometry invariant.

## Abelian groups

The group \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) has polynomial growth of degree \(n\). The ball of radius \(r\) has roughly \(r^n\) elements.

## Products

Polynomial growth degrees add under direct product. The group \(\mathbb{Z}^n \times \mathbb{Z}^m\) has growth degree \(n + m\).

## Free groups

A free group of rank at least two has exponential growth. The number of reduced words of length \(n\) grows exponentially, because every generator may be followed by any generator except its inverse.

To check your understanding, read the dimension, add the degrees and recognise exponential growth.

Growth degree is the dimension of an abelian group seen from infinity.
