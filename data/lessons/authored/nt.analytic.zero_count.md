# Counting zeta zeros

The number of nontrivial zeros up to a height grows like the height times its logarithm. This density is what makes the zeros a statistical object.

## The counting function

The Riemann-von Mangoldt formula counts zeros with imaginary part between zero and T. Its leading term is T over two pi times the logarithm of T over two pi.

## A tractable height

At a height that is a multiple of two pi the leading count simplifies. The linear factor becomes the multiplier itself.

## Trivial zeros

Trivial zeros sit at negative even integers. Their count up to a magnitude is half that magnitude, so they are far sparser than the nontrivial ones.

To check your understanding, state the leading term, choose a multiple of two pi and locate the sequence.

Zero counting turns an analytic question into an arithmetic one, and the leading constant is the density of zeros per unit of height.
