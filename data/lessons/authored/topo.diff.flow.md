# Flows of vector fields

A vector field generates a flow, the one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms that moves each point along its integral curve. Linear fields make the flow explicit.

## The linear flow

For the field \(x' = kx\) on the line, the flow is \(x(t) = x_0 e^{kt}\). The multiplier after time \(t\) is \(e^{kt}\).

## Fixed points

A fixed point of the flow is a zero of the field. The field \(x' = x - x^2\) has fixed points at zero and one, so there are two.

## Divergence

The divergence of a field is the trace of its derivative. In two dimensions, \(\nabla \cdot F = \partial_x F_1 + \partial_y F_2\) measures the instantaneous expansion of area.

To check your understanding, exponentiate the rate, solve for the zeros and add the partial derivatives.

A flow is the integral curve written as a family of maps.

Each section above supplies the reasoning for one practice family, so work a numeric instance of each before moving on. Write the first step of every instance down before you compute the final value, because the grader checks the number but the method is what the next concept assumes. If an instance refuses to settle, redraw it with smaller values and check the sign of every term; the arithmetic in these families is exact, so a mismatch means a sign or an order of operations, not an approximation.
