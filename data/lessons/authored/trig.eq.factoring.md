# Solving trigonometric equations by factoring

Factoring turns a trigonometric equation into a product of ratios whose zeros are read off one factor at a time.

## The zero-product step

A product is zero only when one of its factors is zero. Write \(2\sin x\cos x - \sin x = 0\) as
\(\sin x(2\cos x - 1) = 0\). Then either \(\sin x = 0\) or \(\cos x = 1/2\). The first gives 0 and
180 degrees, the second gives 60 and 300 degrees. It has four solutions in one turn.

## Removing common factors

Dividing through by a common factor destroys the solutions that factor carries. The equation
\(\sin x\tan x = \sin x\) loses both of its roots at 0 and 180 degrees if both sides are divided
by \(\sin x\). Collecting instead gives \(\sin x(\tan x - 1) = 0\). Now \(\sin x = 0\) at 0 and
180 degrees, while \(\tan x = 1\) at 45 and 225 degrees. The answer is four angles rather than
two.

## Checking every branch

Each factor carries its own count. One branch can contribute nothing at all. In
\(\cos x(2\sin x + 3) = 0\) the second factor would need \(\sin x = -3/2\), which no angle
satisfies, because the sine of an angle never leaves the interval from -1 to 1. Only
\(\cos x = 0\) survives, at 90 and 270 degrees. A product that looks like two branches yields two
solutions.

A branch with an impossible ratio is rejected before any angle is written down.
