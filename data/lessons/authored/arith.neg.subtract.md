# Subtracting negative numbers

## Subtracting a Negative

Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its opposite. The expression \\(7 - (-3)\\) becomes \\(7 + 3\\), which is 10. Two minus signs in a row turn into a plus.

A number line explains why. Subtracting moves left, but the amount being subtracted is itself negative, so the move reverses and goes right. Every case follows from that single reversal. No separate rule is needed.

## Two Negative Numbers

Both numbers may be negative. Take \\(-4 - (-9)\\). The subtraction of -9 becomes addition of 9, so the expression is \\(-4 + 9 = 5\\).

The sign of the first number does not change. Only the operation and the sign of the second number are rewritten, so -4 stays negative until the addition is performed. Changing both signs is the usual mistake. It answers a different question.

## Order With Negatives

Work left to right when several terms appear. For \\(3 - (-4) + (-6)\\), the first subtraction becomes addition, giving \\(3 + 4 - 6\\). That is \\(7 - 6 = 1\\).

Rewriting every subtraction as addition first makes the order safe. A second example is \\(-2 - 5 = -7\\), where the subtraction keeps its ordinary meaning because 5 is positive. Mixed signs reward a careful rewrite before any arithmetic.
