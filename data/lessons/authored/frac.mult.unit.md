# Multiplying by a unit fraction

Multiplying by \(\frac{1}{n}\) takes one part in \(n\) of the other factor, so it divides that factor by \(n\). The numerator is unchanged and the denominator grows.

## The method

For a fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\) and a positive whole number \(n\), the product is \(\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{1}{n} = \frac{a}{bn}\). Multiply the denominators, keep the numerator and reduce if the numerator and the new denominator share a factor. The result is smaller than the original factor whenever the factor is positive. The new denominator records the total number of equal parts after the subdivision.

## A worked product

Compute \(\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{5}\). Multiplying the denominators gives \(\frac{3}{20}\), which is 0.15 and smaller than 0.75. A second case is \(\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{12}\), which reduces to \(\frac{1}{6}\). The numerator 2 and the denominator 12 share 2, so the reduction is required for the simplest form.

## Checking the result

Multiply the product by 5: \(\frac{3}{20} \times 5 = \frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}\), which recovers the original fraction. For the second case, \(\frac{1}{6} \times 4 = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}\). Division by \(n\) and multiplication by \(\frac{1}{n}\) are the same operation, so the check must return the start.

A unit fraction multiplies by dividing and the denominator records the division.
