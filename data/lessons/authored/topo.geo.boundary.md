# Group boundaries

The boundary of a hyperbolic group is the set of directions at infinity. A line has two boundary points, a lattice in \(n\) dimensions has a sphere, and a free group has a Cantor set.

## The line

The boundary of \(\mathbb{Z}\) consists of two points, one at each end. It is the zero-dimensional sphere \(S^0\).

## Lattices

The boundary of \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) for \(n \ge 2\) is a sphere of dimension \(n - 1\). The plane gives a circle, and three-space gives a two-sphere.

## Free groups

The boundary of a free group of rank at least two is a Cantor set. It has topological dimension zero, unlike the spherical boundaries of lattices.

To check your understanding, count the boundary points, subtract one from the dimension and recognise the Cantor set.

The boundary records the directions in which a group can escape.
