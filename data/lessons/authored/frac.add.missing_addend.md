# Finding a missing addend

A missing addend is the number that completes a given total. In a fraction equation the unknown is isolated by subtracting the known addend from both sides.

## The method

From \(x + \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}\), subtract \(\frac{a}{b}\) to obtain \(x = \frac{c}{d} - \frac{a}{b}\). Write both fractions over a common denominator, subtract the numerators and reduce. The total must be at least the known addend when both are positive, otherwise the unknown is negative. For \(x + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}\) the answer is \(\frac{1}{2}\). A negative answer is a legal result and signals that the stated total is too small.

## A worked sum

Solve \(x + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{7}{8}\). Subtracting gives \(x = \frac{7}{8} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\). A case with unlike denominators is \(x + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{11}{15}\). The known addend becomes \(\frac{6}{15}\), so \(x = \frac{11}{15} - \frac{6}{15} = \frac{5}{15} = \frac{1}{3}\).

## Checking the result

Add the found addend back to the known one. For the first case, \(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{7}{8}\), which is the stated total. For the second, \(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{5}{15} + \frac{6}{15} = \frac{11}{15}\). Writing the check over a different common denominator gives the same value and catches a reduction error.

The unknown addend is the gap between the known part and the total.
