# Christoffel symbols

Christoffel symbols are the coordinate expressions for the derivative of basis vectors, measuring how the coordinate frame turns.

## The metric connection

The Levi-Civita connection is the unique connection that is torsion free and compatible with the metric. Compatibility says the inner product of transported vectors does not change.

Torsion freedom says the connection is symmetric in its two lower indices. Those conditions fix the symbols from the metric. In two dimensions 3 distinct symbols remain.

## The formula in coordinates

The symbols are half the inverse metric contracted with derivatives of the metric: one derivative of each of two entries minus one derivative of the third. The index pattern is what makes the connection metric compatible.

In polar coordinates the metric has entries 1 and \(r^{2}\). The nonvanishing symbols are \(-r\) for two \(\phi\) indices and \(1/r\) for one \(r\) and one \(\phi\). At \(r=2\) they are -2 and 0.5.

## A worked surface

On the unit sphere the metric is \(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}\). The symbol with two \(\phi\) indices is \(-\sin\theta\cos\theta\). The mixed one is \(\cot\theta\).

At \(\theta=\pi/4\) the two values are -0.5 and 1. The first is minus the product of sine and cosine. The second is their ratio.

The symbols are not components of a tensor. They change with the coordinates through an extra term with a second derivative.
