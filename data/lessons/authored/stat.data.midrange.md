# The midrange

The midrange is the average of the smallest and largest observations, so it is the midpoint of the interval that the data span.

## The midpoint of the extremes

The midrange uses two numbers and ignores the rest. For minimum 12 and maximum 48 the midrange is \((12 + 48)/2 = 30\). Geometrically it is the centre of the range interval, so it is the value a uniform distribution would be centred on. It differs from the median unless the data are symmetric.

## Computing it

Add the minimum and the maximum and halve the sum. For the sample 3, 7, 8, 10 and 22 the extremes are 3 and 22, so the midrange is 12.5, while the mean is 10 and the median is 8. The gap is a warning that the upper extreme is pulling the midrange away from the bulk.

## Its limits

The midrange has a breakdown point of zero. Replacing one observation changes it arbitrarily, because the largest value is one of its two inputs, so its variance is poor and it is rarely used as a robust estimate of centre. It has one honest use, as a quick midpoint for a symmetric process, such as a tolerance band from 9.8 to 10.2 with midrange 10.0.

The statistic is a two-point summary; it should never be presented as though it used the whole sample.
