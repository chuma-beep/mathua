# Mordell equations

A Mordell equation writes y squared as x cubed plus a constant. It defines an elliptic curve, and its integer points are finite by Siegel's theorem.

## The curve

The equation y squared equals x cubed plus k is an elliptic curve when the right side has distinct roots. The constant k controls the shape and the discriminant.

## The discriminant

The discriminant of x cubed plus k is minus twenty-seven k squared. The discriminant of the elliptic curve multiplies that by minus sixteen.

## Integral points

Siegel proved that only finitely many integer points lie on such a curve. Finding them all still requires a separate computation for each k.

To check your understanding, evaluate the right side, cube the constant and state finiteness.

Mordell equations are the simplest elliptic curves, and their integral points are finite but not uniformly bounded.
