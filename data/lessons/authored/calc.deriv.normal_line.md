# Slopes of normal lines

A normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line at a point. Its slope is the negative reciprocal of the tangent slope.

## The tangent slope

The derivative gives the tangent slope. For \(f(x)=x^2\), the slope at \(x=a\) is \(2a\).

For \(f(x)=x^3\), the slope at \(x=a\) is \(3a^2\).

Evaluate the derivative at the point of tangency, not at a nearby point.

## The negative reciprocal

Perpendicular slopes multiply to -1. If the tangent slope is \(m\), the normal slope is \(-1/m\).

A horizontal tangent has a vertical normal, and a vertical tangent has a horizontal normal.

The negative reciprocal is undefined when the tangent slope is zero, which is exactly the vertical-normal case.

## Writing the normal line

Use point-slope form with the point of tangency and the normal slope. The normal passes through the same point as the tangent.

The normal is not the tangent. Confusing the two is the most common error in this topic.
