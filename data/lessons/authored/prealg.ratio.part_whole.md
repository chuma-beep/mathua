# Part-to-whole ratios

## From counts to a ratio

A part-to-whole ratio compares one group with the entire set. If a class has 8 boys and 12 girls, the part-to-part ratio of boys to girls is 8:12, or 2:3. The part-to-whole ratio of boys to all students is 8:20, or 2:5. Add the parts to get the whole, then divide the part and the whole by their common factor. The order is part first and whole second.

## Finding the whole

When a part and its part-to-whole ratio are known, use the scale factor. If the ratio is 2:5 and the part is 14, one ratio part is 14 / 2 = 7. The whole is 5 parts, or 35. Check that the other part is 35 - 14 = 21 and that 14:35 reduces to 2:5. The scale factor is the same for the part and the whole.

## Comparing part to part

A part-to-part ratio and a part-to-whole ratio answer different questions. The ratio 2:3 compares boys with girls, while 2:5 compares boys with all students. To convert, add the two parts to form the whole. Do not use the other part as the denominator unless the question asks for a part-to-part comparison. Read the wording carefully before writing the ratio.
