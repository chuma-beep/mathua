# Powers of ten and their exponents

## Ten to a Whole Power

A power of ten counts how many tens are multiplied together. The exponent records that count. \\(10^3\\) means \\(10 \\times 10 \\times 10\\), which is 1000. The exponent 3 matches the three zeros in the answer.

The pattern continues upward without any new rule. \\(10^4 = 10000\\) has four zeros. The power \\(10^5\\) has five. Recognising the pattern lets you write large numbers without counting digits by hand.

## The Zero and First Powers

The first power of ten is ten itself, because \\(10^1\\) means one factor of ten. The zero power looks strange but follows from division: dividing \\(10^2\\) by \\(10^2\\) gives 1, while subtracting exponents gives \\(10^0\\). So \\(10^0 = 1\\).

Any nonzero number raised to the zero power is 1 for the same reason. Zero is the exception, since \\(0^0\\) is not defined in this course.

## Multiplying Powers of Ten

Multiplying powers of ten adds their exponents. \\(10^2 \\times 10^3\\) contains five factors of ten, so the product is \\(10^5\\), which is 100000.

Coefficients multiply separately from the powers. Take \\((3 \\times 10^2)(4 \\times 10^3)\\). The coefficients give 12. The exponents add to 5, so the result is 1,200,000. Convert to standard form at the end, because a coefficient above ten is usually rewritten.
