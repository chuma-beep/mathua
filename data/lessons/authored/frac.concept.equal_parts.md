# Equal parts of a whole

A fraction counts equal parts of one whole. The parts must have equal size, not merely equal number or the count does not describe the whole.

## The definition

To write \(\frac{a}{b}\), split the whole into \(b\) parts of equal measure. Each part is the unit fraction \(\frac{1}{b}\) and \(a\) copies of it give \(\frac{a}{b}\). The whole itself is \(\frac{b}{b}\), which equals 1.

## An example

Take a rectangle 12 cm wide and 5 cm high, with area 60 cm squared. Four vertical strips of width 3 cm each have area 15 cm squared and three of them cover 45 cm squared. Since \(45/60 = \frac{3}{4}\), the shaded region is three quarters of the rectangle. Cutting the same rectangle into pieces 1 cm, 2 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm wide gives four pieces of unequal area and no piece is a quarter, even though there are four of them.

## Why it matters

Area models, number line intervals and fair sharing all depend on equal parts. On a number line the interval from 0 to 1 must be divided into equal lengths or the tick marks do not represent unit fractions. In sharing, each recipient must receive the same amount, which is the same requirement stated with objects.

Equal parts are what make a denominator a count of equal shares.
