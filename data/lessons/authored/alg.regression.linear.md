# Least-squares regression line

A least-squares line summarizes a set of points by minimizing the sum of squared vertical distances from the points to the line.

## The least-squares line

The line has the form \(\hat y=mx+b\). The slope measures the predicted change in \(y\) for a one-unit increase in \(x\).

The least-squares slope is not simply the slope between the first and last points. Every point contributes through the residuals.

## The intercept and the mean point

The least-squares line always passes through the mean point \((\bar x,\bar y)\). Once the slope is known, the intercept follows from \(b=\bar y-m\bar x\).

With symmetric \(x\)-values and residuals that cancel, the slope and intercept are the underlying values used to build the data.

## Using the line

Substitute an \(x\)-value into \(\hat y=mx+b\) to predict \(y\). Extrapolation far outside the data is less reliable than interpolation.

A prediction is a summary of the pattern, not a guarantee. Check whether the point lies inside the range of the observed data.
