# Oriented surface flux

Flux measures how much of a vector field crosses a surface in a chosen direction. An oriented surface supplies the normal vector that makes the sign meaningful.

## The flux integral

The flux of F across an oriented surface is the integral of F dot n over the surface, where n is the chosen unit normal. For a graph, the unnormalised normal is enough when the area element is included. The answer is a scalar and its sign depends on the orientation.

## Upward and downward graphs

For z equal to f(x,y), the upward normal is (-f_x, -f_y, 1). The downward normal is its negative. Dotting the vector field with these normals gives expressions that differ only by an overall sign. A flux can therefore be positive, negative or zero.

## A vertical square

The surface r(u,v) equal to (u,0,v) is a vertical square in the plane y equal to zero. Its normal has no x or z component, so only the y component of the field contributes to the flux. This makes the vertical example a clean orientation test.
