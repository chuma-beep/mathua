# The harmonic mean value property

A harmonic function equals its average over every sphere centred inside its domain. The mean value property is equivalent to harmonicity and gives the maximum principle without any differentiability argument.

## Average over a circle

For a point inside the domain, the average of the function over a circle centred at that point equals the value at the centre. The radius must be small enough that the whole circle stays in the domain.

## Consequence for maxima

An average cannot exceed the largest value on the circle. If a harmonic function attained a strict interior maximum, a small circle around it would average to something smaller, which contradicts the property.

## The converse

If the mean value property holds for every sufficiently small sphere then the function is harmonic. Expanding the average in powers of the radius isolates the Laplacian as the first non-vanishing coefficient.

To check your understanding, write the circular average, bound the average by the maximum and expand the average in the radius.

The property converts a differential equation into an averaging rule, and the averaging rule is what makes harmonic functions so rigid.
