# Comparing fractions with the same numerator

When two fractions share a numerator, the denominators decide which is larger. A smaller denominator means each part is larger, so the fraction is larger.

## The same-numerator rule

For positive numerators, a over b is larger than a over c exactly when b is smaller than c. The denominator counts the size of each part. Fewer, larger parts make the fraction larger.

## The larger denominator

The larger fraction has the smaller denominator. Reading the denominator is enough; no common denominator is needed. This is the quickest comparison when the numerators match.

## Comparing with one

A fraction a over b is less than one exactly when a is less than b. Equality gives one and a larger numerator gives a value above one. This check places the fraction relative to a whole.
