# The second variation

The second variation decides whether a stationary curve is a minimum, a maximum or a saddle. Its sign is governed by a Sturm-Liouville problem, and a conjugate point marks where the extremal stops being minimal.

## The quadratic form

Differentiating the functional twice gives a quadratic form in the perturbation. The coefficient of the squared slope is the second derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the slope.

## Legendre condition

A minimum requires that coefficient to be positive at every point. A negative value means a perturbation that lowers the functional, so the stationary curve cannot be a minimum.

## Conjugate points

The sign of the second variation is decided by a Jacobi equation. If a non-trivial solution vanishes at an interior point before the endpoint, that point is conjugate and the extremal is not a local minimum.

To check your understanding, differentiate the functional twice, evaluate the slope coefficient and write the Jacobi equation.

Stationarity is necessary but not sufficient, and the second variation is the test that separates a minimum from a saddle.
