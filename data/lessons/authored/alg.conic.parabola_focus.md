# Focus and directrix of a parabola

A parabola is the set of points equidistant from a focus and a directrix. Its standard form makes both easy to read.

## The standard form

The upward parabola \(y=x^2/(4p)\) has vertex at the origin. The number \(p\) is the distance from the vertex to the focus.

A negative \(p\) opens the parabola downward. The absolute value of \(p\) is still the focal distance.

## Focus and directrix

The focus is \((0,p)\) and the directrix is the line \(y=-p\). The vertex is halfway between them.

Every point on the parabola is the same distance from the focus and the directrix. That equality is the definition, not a coincidence.

## The latus rectum

The latus rectum is the chord through the focus perpendicular to the axis of symmetry. Its length is \(4p\).

For \(p=2\), the latus rectum has length 8. It passes through \((0,2)\) and spans \(x=-4\) to \(x=4\).

A parabola with a small \(p\) is tightly curved near the vertex, while a large \(p\) is flatter. The focus moves with that curvature.
