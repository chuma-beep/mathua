# Singular perturbation

A singular perturbation multiplies the highest derivative, so the limit equation has fewer boundary conditions and the expansion fails in a thin layer. The parameter changes the order of the problem, not just its coefficients.

## Loss of order

Setting the parameter to zero removes the highest derivative. A second-order equation becomes first order, and one boundary condition can no longer be satisfied by the reduced equation.

## The outer solution

The outer solution solves the reduced equation and satisfies the boundary conditions it can reach. It is accurate away from the layer where the removed derivative matters.

## The inner layer

Rescaling the independent variable by the parameter restores the second derivative. The scaled equation balances the two highest derivatives, and its solution supplies the boundary condition the outer solution lost.

To check your understanding, set the parameter to zero, solve the reduced equation and rescale the variable.

A singular perturbation is recognised by the order drop, and the missing boundary condition is always paid back inside a layer.
