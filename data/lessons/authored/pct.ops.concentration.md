# Percentage concentration of a mixture

## Percent by Volume

Concentration compares the amount of solute with the amount of the whole mixture. For a liquid mixture the comparison is usually by volume. If 40 millilitres of acid are dissolved in enough water to make 500 millilitres of solution, the concentration is \\(40 \\div 500 = 0.08\\), which is 8 percent.

The denominator is the finished volume, not the volume of water added. That distinction matters, because the acid itself takes up space and the total is what a label describes.

## Finding the Amount of Solute

To find the solute, multiply the concentration by the total volume. A 12 percent solution with a total volume of 750 millilitres contains \\(0.12 \\times 750 = 90\\) millilitres of solute.

Check the answer by dividing it back by the total, which must recover the percent. A result larger than the total volume is impossible, so an answer such as 900 millilitres signals a misplaced decimal point.

## Diluting a Solution

Dilution adds solvent while the amount of solute stays fixed. Start with 90 millilitres of acid and want a 6 percent solution. The total volume must satisfy \\(90 \\div V = 0.06\\), so \\(V = 1500\\) millilitres.

The original 750 millilitres already includes the acid, so the water to add is \\(1500 - 750 = 750\\) millilitres. Adding the full 1,500 millilitres of water would double the total and halve the concentration again.
