# The limit inferior of a sequence

The limit inferior is the smallest value that a subsequence can approach. It is the mirror image of the limit superior.

## Subsequential limits

The limit inferior is the infimum of all subsequential limits. Every convergent subsequence contributes one candidate. The smallest candidate is the limit inferior. For a convergent sequence it agrees with the ordinary limit. Equivalently, it is the limit of the tail infima: \(\liminf a_n = \sup_{n\ge1}\inf_{k\ge n} a_k\). Each tail infimum can only increase, and its limit is the smallest value the sequence approaches infinitely often.

## The smallest cluster point

For a sequence with even constant A and odd constant B, the limit inferior is the smaller of A and B. A shift by C adds C to that value. The comparison between the two subsequences is unchanged.

## Convergent sequences and shifts

A convergent sequence has equal limit superior and limit inferior. If the sequence is shifted, both limits shift by the same amount. The difference between the two is a measure of oscillation, and a shift does not change that difference.
