# Even and Odd Trigonometric Identities

## Even Functions

Cosine and secant are even: cos(-x) = cos(x) and sec(-x) = sec(x). Their graphs are symmetric about the vertical axis.

An even function satisfies f(-x) = f(x) for every value in its domain.

The even property lets a negative angle be replaced by its positive counterpart without changing the value.

## Odd Functions

Sine, tangent, cosecant and cotangent are odd: sin(-x) = -sin(x), and the same sign change applies to the other three.

An odd function satisfies f(-x) = -f(x). Its graph has half-turn symmetry about the origin.

The odd property is useful when simplifying expressions with negative angles or when integrating over symmetric intervals.

## Using the Identities

Apply the even or odd property before combining terms. A sign error at this stage propagates through the whole simplification.

Sine squared is even because the two sign changes cancel: sin(-x) squared equals sin(x) squared.

Classify each function in an expression first, then rewrite the expression and check one numerical value.
