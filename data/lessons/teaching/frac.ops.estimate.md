# Estimating with Fractions

## Benchmark Values

An estimate replaces each fraction with the nearest benchmark: zero, one half or one. A fraction with a small numerator is near zero, one close to half is near one half, and one close to the denominator is near one.

Three eighths is near one half because it is slightly below four eighths. Seven eighths is near one. One ninth is near zero. Choosing the nearest benchmark keeps the error small.

## Estimating a Sum

Replace each fraction by its benchmark, then add the benchmarks. The estimate is usually a whole number or a half. It tells whether the exact sum is near zero, near one or near two.

For five sixths plus seven eighths, both fractions are near one, so the sum is near two. The exact sum is one and seventeen twenty-fourths, which is indeed close to two.

## Using the Estimate

An estimate catches unreasonable answers. A sum of two positive fractions near one cannot be less than one. A difference of two fractions near one half should be small.

Estimates also guide decisions in context. If a recipe needs about two cups and the bowl holds three, the cook knows the bowl is large enough without computing exact amounts first.
