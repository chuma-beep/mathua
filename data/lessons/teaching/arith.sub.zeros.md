# Subtracting Across Zeros

## Why Zeros Need Care

A zero in the minuend cannot lend a unit because it has none. The borrow must come from the next place that has a value. That place gives one unit to the zero, which then becomes ten and can lend onward.

The chain can cross several zeros. In 400 minus 178, the hundreds place lends to the tens, the tens becomes ten and immediately lends one to the ones, leaving nine tens. The ones then becomes ten and the subtraction proceeds.

## Working the Chain

Rewrite the top number after the borrows before subtracting. In 400 minus 178 the top becomes 3 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 ones. Subtract ones from ones, tens from tens and hundreds from hundreds.

Writing the regrouped values above the digits keeps the chain visible. It also shows why the hundreds digit drops by one even though the original hundreds digit was borrowed only indirectly.

## Checking the Difference

Add the difference to the subtrahend. The sum must equal the original minuend. This check is especially useful across zeros because a skipped borrow changes the answer by a whole place value.

Estimate as a second check. 400 minus 178 is close to 400 minus 180, which is 220. An answer near 22 or 2,200 is off by a factor of ten.
