# Ordering Rational Numbers

## A Common Form

Rational numbers can be written as fractions, decimals or integers. Convert them to one form before comparing.

Decimals compare place by place. Fractions with the same denominator compare by numerator; otherwise use a common denominator.

Negative numbers are ordered in reverse: -5 is less than -2 even though 5 is greater than 2.

## Comparing Pairs

On a number line, the number farther left is smaller. This rule holds for every rational number.

For -3/4 and -0.7, write both as decimals: -0.75 < -0.7, so -3/4 is smaller.

A quick benchmark such as zero, one half or one can often settle a comparison without exact conversion.

## Ordering Lists

Sort a list by repeatedly comparing neighbours, or place every number on a sketch of the number line.

Check the final order by reading from left to right. Each number must be no greater than the one after it.

Ordering is the first step in finding a median, a range or a solution interval.


A common mistake is to treat a longer decimal as a larger number. Place value decides, so 0.45 is greater than 0.405 even though it has fewer digits. Writing both numbers with the same number of decimal places removes that trap before the comparison begins.
