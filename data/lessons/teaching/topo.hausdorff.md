## Definition

A topological space $X$ is Hausdorff (or $T_2$) if for any two distinct points $x \neq y$ in $X$, there exist disjoint open neighborhoods $U$ of $x$ and $V$ of $y$.

### Example

$\mathbb{R}$ with its usual metric is Hausdorff: take $\epsilon = d(x,y)/2$.

## Examples and non-examples

Metric spaces are always Hausdorff. The indiscrete topology on $\{a,b\}$ is not Hausdorff because the only open sets are $\emptyset$ and the whole space.

### Example

The discrete topology on any set is Hausdorff, and $\mathbb{R}^2$ with the Euclidean metric is Hausdorff.

## Metric spaces are Hausdorff

Every metric space is Hausdorff: distinct points $x,y$ are separated by balls of radius $d(x,y)/2$.
