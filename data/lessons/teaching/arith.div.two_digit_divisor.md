# Dividing by a Two-Digit Divisor

## Estimating the Quotient

Long division with a two-digit divisor starts with an estimate. Round the divisor to a friendly number and ask how many copies fit into the leading part of the dividend. The estimate is rarely exact, but it narrows the digit to try.

The estimate must be adjusted by multiplication. Multiply the divisor by the trial digit and compare with the current partial dividend. If the product is too large, lower the digit by one and try again.

## Recording the Steps

Write the trial digit above the digit of the dividend that ends the partial product. Multiply, subtract and bring down the next digit. Each round produces one digit of the quotient and a remainder smaller than the divisor.

The remainder is the check on the work. A remainder that is equal to or larger than the divisor means the quotient digit is too small. A negative subtraction means it is too large.

## Checking With Multiplication

Division and multiplication undo each other. Multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. The result must equal the original dividend.

This check catches most errors in one line. It also prepares for decimal division, where the same relationship holds once the remainder is written as a fraction or continued as a decimal.
