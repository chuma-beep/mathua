# Gaussian Integers

## The Gaussian Integers

The Gaussian integers are the complex numbers a plus b i with integer a and b. They form a ring under ordinary addition and multiplication, and they behave in many ways like the ordinary integers. The four units are one, minus one, i and minus i, and every nonzero Gaussian integer has a factorisation into Gaussian primes.

## Norm and Units

The norm of a plus b i is a squared plus b squared, an ordinary nonnegative integer. The norm is multiplicative, so the norm of a product is the product of the norms. That identity converts questions about Gaussian factors into questions about sums of two squares in the ordinary integers.

## Arithmetic in the Plane

Arithmetic in the plane is therefore both algebraic and geometric. Multiplying by i rotates a point by ninety degrees, and multiplying by a general Gaussian integer scales and rotates the plane. The norm measures squared distance from the origin, which is why the ring connects factorization with lattice geometry and with the two-squares theorem.
