## Definition of order

The order of an element $g$ in a group $G$ is the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $g^n = e$ (additively, $n \cdot g = 0$). If no such $n$ exists, the order is infinite.

### Example

In $Z_6$, the order of $2$ is $3$ because $2+2+2 = 0$ and no smaller positive multiple works.

## Order of a group

The order of a group $G$, denoted $|G|$, is the number of its elements. For a finite cyclic group $\langle g \rangle$, the order of $g$ equals $|G|$ when $g$ is a generator.

### Example

$|S_3| = 6$, and the 3-cycle $(1 2 3)$ has order $3$.

## Computing orders

Use repeated operation until the identity appears. For addition mod $n$, multiples of the element are tested.
