# Normal spaces

## Separating Closed Sets

A space is normal when any two disjoint closed sets can be placed in disjoint open neighbourhoods. Equivalently, for every closed set A contained in an open set U, there is an open set V with A contained in V and the closure of V contained in U.

Normality is a separation axiom, stronger than Hausdorff in the presence of suitable hypotheses but not comparable in general. Metric spaces are normal, compact Hausdorff spaces are normal, and every closed subspace of a normal space is normal.

The definition is about closed sets rather than points. That is why normality interacts so well with Urysohn functions and with partitions of unity. It is also why normality can fail in products and quotients even when the factors are well behaved.

## Normality and Other Separation Axioms

A normal T1 space is called T4. Normality alone does not imply Hausdorff, so the T1 hypothesis is needed for the familiar separation consequences. With T1, normal spaces separate points from closed sets and admit Urysohn functions.

The implications form a small hierarchy. Metric implies normal, compact Hausdorff implies normal, and normal plus second countable implies metrizable under the Urysohn metrization theorem. None of these implications reverses in general.

Counterexamples are important. The Sorgenfrey plane is a product of two normal spaces and is not normal. The Niemytzki plane is another standard example. These show that normality is not productive and is not inherited by arbitrary subspaces.

## Examples and Counterexamples

The real line, every metric space and every compact Hausdorff space are normal. A discrete space is normal. A finite T1 space is discrete and therefore normal. These examples cover most of the spaces encountered in a first course.

Failures of normality usually involve a large product or a topology with too many open sets. The Sorgenfrey plane is the standard product counterexample. The space of countable ordinals is normal but its product with itself is not.

When a proof needs normality, check the hypothesis explicitly. Compact Hausdorff is enough, metric is enough, and a closed subspace of a normal space is enough. Do not assume that an open subspace or an arbitrary quotient preserves the property.
