# Solving Multiple-Angle Equations

## The Double-Angle Form

An equation such as sin(2x) = 0 is solved by first treating 2x as a single angle. The general solution for that angle is then divided by two.

Every solution of the inner equation contributes a solution of the original equation, and the division may bring new solutions into the requested interval.

Keep the period of the inner angle in mind: sin(2x) repeats twice as fast as sin(x).

## Working Within an Interval

When the answer must lie between 0 and 360 degrees, list the inner-angle solutions in the wider range and then halve each one.

For sin(2x) = 0, the inner solutions are 0, 180, 360 and 540 degrees, giving 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees after halving.

Discard any value outside the interval and order the rest from smallest to largest.

## Checking Solutions

Substitute each candidate into the original equation. A solution of the doubled equation is valid only if the original also holds.

Graph both sides on the same axes to see the intersections; the number of intersections in one period is a quick check.

If a solution is lost, check whether division by two missed a family of inner solutions.
