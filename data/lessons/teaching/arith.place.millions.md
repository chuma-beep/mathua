# Place Value to Millions

## The Periods of a Large Number

Large numbers are grouped in periods of three digits. From the right, the periods are ones, thousands and millions. Each period repeats the same ones, tens and hundreds pattern.

In 4,207,315 the millions period holds 4, the thousands period holds 207 and the ones period holds 315. Reading the periods in order gives four million, two hundred seven thousand, three hundred fifteen.

## The Value of a Digit

A digit's value is the digit times the value of its place. The 2 in 4,207,315 sits in the hundred-thousands place, so its value is 200,000 even though the digit itself is 2.

Moving one place left multiplies the value by ten. Moving one place right divides it by ten. This pattern is what makes expanded form and standard form interchangeable.

## Writing and Comparing

Expanded form writes a number as a sum of place values. 4,207,315 becomes 4,000,000 plus 200,000 plus 7,000 plus 300 plus 10 plus 5. Adding the parts returns the standard form.

To compare two large numbers, compare the number of digits first. If the counts match, compare the leading digit and move right until the digits differ. The first difference decides the ordering.
