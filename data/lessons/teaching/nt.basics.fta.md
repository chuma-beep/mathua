# Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

## Prime Factorisation

The fundamental theorem of arithmetic says that every integer greater than one factors into primes, and the factorisation is unique apart from the order of the factors. This turns the primes into the multiplicative building blocks of the integers. A proof uses the Euclidean algorithm and the fact that a prime dividing a product must divide one of its factors.

## Uniqueness

The uniqueness half is the part that needs work. If a number had two prime factorisations, cancel common primes and use Euclid's lemma to derive a contradiction. The same argument shows that the exponent of each prime in a factorisation is determined by the number, so expressions such as two cubed times three squared describe exactly one integer.

## Counting Factors

Once the prime factorisation is known, every multiplicative question about the number can be read from its exponents. A divisor chooses an exponent between zero and the exponent in the factorisation for each prime. That is why the number of divisors is a product of one plus each exponent, and why the sum of divisors is a product of geometric series.
