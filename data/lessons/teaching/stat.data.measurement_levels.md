# Levels of measurement

A variable's level of measurement says which comparisons and summaries are meaningful. The four levels are nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio.

## Nominal and ordinal variables

A nominal variable only names a category. Eye colour, country of birth and favourite sport are nominal. The values can be counted, but they cannot be ordered.

An ordinal variable has a meaningful order. A satisfaction rating from poor to excellent is ordinal. The order matters, but the distance between neighbouring categories need not be equal.

## Interval and ratio variables

An interval variable has equal distances between values but no true zero. Temperature in degrees Celsius is interval: the difference between 10 and 20 equals the difference between 20 and 30, but 20 degrees is not twice as warm as 10 degrees.

A ratio variable has equal distances and a true zero. Height, mass and income are ratio variables. A mass of 20 kilograms is twice a mass of 10 kilograms because zero kilograms means no mass.

## Choosing an appropriate summary

Counts and modes suit nominal data. Medians suit ordinal data. Means and standard deviations suit interval or ratio data when the distribution is not badly skewed.

Ratios of values are meaningful only at the ratio level. Saying one Celsius temperature is twice another is not meaningful, even though both numbers are valid temperatures.
