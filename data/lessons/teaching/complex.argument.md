# Argument of a Complex Number

**Argument:** For $z\neq0$, $\arg(z)$ is the angle from the positive real axis to $z$, measured counter-clockwise. The principal value lies in $(-\pi,\pi]$.

## Angle in the Plane

### Standard Positions
$\arg(1)=0^{\circ}$, $\arg(i)=90^{\circ}$, $\arg(-1)=180^{\circ}$, $\arg(-i)=270^{\circ}$ ($-90^{\circ}$ principal). For $z$ in a quadrant, $\arg(z)=\operatorname{atan2}(b,a)$.

### Addition Under Multiplication
$\arg(z_{1}z_{2})=\arg(z_{1})+\arg(z_{2})\pmod{360^{\circ}}$. This mirrors $r$ multiplying and angles adding in polar form.

## Example

$z=1+i$ lies at $45^{\circ}$, so $\arg(z)=45^{\circ}$. Check: $|z|=\sqrt{2}$, $z=\sqrt{2}(\cos45^{\circ}+i\sin45^{\circ})$.
