# Decimals and Powers of Ten

## Multiplying

Multiplying by ten moves the decimal point one place to the right. Multiplying by one hundred moves it two places.

The digits keep their order; only the position of the decimal point changes. Add zeros when the number runs out of digits.

Multiplying by a power of ten makes a number larger, so the point must move right.

## Dividing

Dividing by ten moves the decimal point one place to the left. Dividing by one thousand moves it three places.

Division by a power of ten makes a number smaller, so the point moves left. Leading zeros may be needed.

The exponent of ten counts the places moved. A positive exponent multiplies; a negative exponent divides.

## Checking the Direction

Estimate first: 4.5 times 100 should be near 450, not 45. The estimate catches a point moved the wrong way.

The number of places moved equals the exponent when multiplying. Keep that count separate from the digits.

Powers of ten are the reason metric conversions are simple: each prefix step is a factor of ten.
