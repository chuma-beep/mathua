# Similar Matrices

## The similarity relation

Two square matrices A and B are similar when B = P inverse A P for some invertible matrix P. The matrix P represents a change of basis, so similar matrices describe the same linear transformation in different coordinate systems.

This section develops the idea and connects it to the surrounding linear algebra.

- Check that similarity uses an invertible change of basis
- Recognise similarity as an equivalence relation

## Invariants under similarity

Similar matrices share eigenvalues, trace, determinant, and characteristic polynomial. These quantities are invariants of the underlying transformation, which is why they can be computed from whichever representative is easiest to work with.

## Diagonalisation as similarity

Diagonalisation is the special case where the similar matrix is diagonal. A matrix is diagonalisable exactly when it has enough independent eigenvectors to form a basis, and the diagonal entries of the resulting matrix are its eigenvalues.
