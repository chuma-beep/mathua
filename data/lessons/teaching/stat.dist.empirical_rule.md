# The empirical rule

For a roughly normal distribution, the empirical rule gives the share of observations within one, two and three standard deviations of the mean.

## The 68-95-99.7 rule

About 68 percent of observations lie within one standard deviation of the mean. About 95 percent lie within two standard deviations. About 99.7 percent lie within three.

The rule is a shorthand for the normal curve. It is not a theorem about every data set, and it works poorly for strongly skewed distributions.

## Intervals from the mean

If the mean is $\mu$ and the standard deviation is $\sigma$, the two-standard-deviation interval is

$$[\mu - 2\sigma,\ \mu + 2\sigma].$$

A value outside that interval is unusual for a normal distribution, though not impossible. The interval describes the middle of the distribution, not a guarantee about every observation.

## Applying the rule

In a normal sample of 1000 observations, about 680 should lie within one standard deviation and about 950 within two. These are expected counts, so the observed counts will vary.

Check the shape before using the rule. A histogram with a long tail or two peaks is not well described by a single normal curve.
