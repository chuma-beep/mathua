# Equations of a Plane

## A normal vector

A plane in three-dimensional space is determined by a point on it and a nonzero normal vector perpendicular to it. Every direction vector inside the plane has zero dot product with the normal, which is the geometric source of the plane equation.

This section develops the idea and connects it to the surrounding linear algebra.

- Check that a direction lies in the plane
- Use the dot product test

## Point-normal form

The point-normal form writes the equation as n dot (r - r0) = 0. Expanding the dot product gives an equation of the form ax + by + cz = d, where the coefficients a, b, c are the components of the normal and d is their dot product with the known point.

- Test whether a point satisfies the equation

## Distance from a point

The constant term controls how far the plane sits from the origin. The distance from the origin to the plane is the absolute value of d divided by the length of the normal, and intercepts can be found by setting the other coordinates to zero.
