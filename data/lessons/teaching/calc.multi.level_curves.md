# Level Curves and Contour Diagrams

A level curve collects the points where a function of two variables has a fixed value. A contour diagram draws several level curves together.

## Level curves of a function

The level curve for value \(k\) is the set of points satisfying \(f(x,y)=k\). For \(f(x,y)=x^2+y^2\), the level curve \(k=25\) is a circle of radius 5. The level curve \(k=0\) is the single point at the origin.

A level curve can be empty. For \(f(x,y)=x^2+y^2\), there is no level curve for \(k=-1\) because the output is never negative. The range determines which levels are possible.

## Circles, lines and hyperbolas

Different functions produce different level-curve families. \(x^2+y^2=k\) gives circles for \(k>0\). \(x+y=k\) gives parallel lines. \(x^2-y^2=k\) gives hyperbolas, and \(x^2-y^2=0\) gives the two lines \(y=\pm x\).

For \(x^2-y^2=16\) and \(x=5\), the equation gives \(25-y^2=16\), so \(y^2=9\). The two points have \(y=3\) and \(y=-3\). Both lie on the same level curve.

## Reading a contour diagram

A contour diagram labels level curves by their values. Moving from one curve to the next changes the output by the contour interval. The gradient is perpendicular to the level curve and points toward increasing values.

If contours are close together, the surface is steep. If they are far apart, it is gentle. A closed contour with no other contours inside it often signals a local maximum or minimum, but the labels must be checked before deciding which.
