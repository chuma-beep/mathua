# Finite Fields

**Finite field:** A field with finitely many elements. Finite fields exist exactly for prime-power orders $p^n$, one (up to isomorphism) per prime power — written $\mathbb F_{p^n}$ or $GF(p^n)$.

## Worked: F_4 exists, order-6 fields do not

Test small orders:
1. 4 = $2^2$ is a prime power: $\mathbb F_4 = \mathbb F_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)$ exists.
2. 6 is not a prime power: no field has 6 elements (additive group would be $Z_6$, whose non-units kill the field axioms).
3. So existence is purely arithmetic on the order: prime power or nothing.

So the classification starts as a filter: check the order first.

## Worked: F_4^× has order 3

Count the multiplicative group:
1. $\mathbb F_4$ has 4 elements; removing 0 leaves 3.
2. The nonzero elements form a cyclic group: some element cubes to 1 first.
3. So $|\mathbb F_4^\times| = 3$ — and in general $|\mathbb F_{p^n}^\times| = p^n - 1$, always cyclic.

So finite-field unit groups are as simple as possible: cyclic, order one less.

## Frobenius

$x \mapsto x^p$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb F_{p^n}$: the Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)$ is cyclic generated by Frobenius, of order $n$.
