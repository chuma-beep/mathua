# Equations with Fractional Coefficients

## The Fractional Coefficient

A coefficient such as one third means the variable is divided by three. Multiplying both sides by three undoes it.

The reciprocal of a fraction is its flip. Multiplying a fraction by its reciprocal gives one, which isolates the variable.

Keep the equation balanced: whatever operation clears the fraction on one side must be applied to the other side.

## Clearing Denominators

When several fractions appear, multiply every term by the least common denominator. This turns the equation into one with whole-number coefficients.

For x/2 + x/3 = 5, multiply by 6 to get 3x + 2x = 30, so x = 6. The result can be checked in the original equation.

Clearing denominators is a convenience, not a requirement. The same solution comes from working with the fractions directly.

## Checking the Solution

Substitute the answer into the original equation and simplify both sides independently. The two sides must agree exactly.

A fractional answer is acceptable. Reduce it and, when a decimal is requested, give enough places to show the value.

If the check fails, look for a sign error or a term that was not multiplied by the common denominator.
