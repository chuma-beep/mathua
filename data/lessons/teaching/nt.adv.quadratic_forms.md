# Binary Quadratic Forms

## The Discriminant

A binary quadratic form is an expression a x squared plus b x y plus c y squared with integer coefficients. Its discriminant is b squared minus four a c. The discriminant is unchanged by a change of variables with determinant one, so it classifies forms into equivalence classes.

## Definite and Indefinite Forms

The sign of the discriminant and the sign of the leading coefficient determine the shape of the form. A negative discriminant with positive leading coefficient gives a positive definite form, whose values are always nonnegative and vanish only at the origin. A positive discriminant gives an indefinite form, whose values take both signs.

## Evaluating a Form

Evaluating a form at an integer pair is the concrete operation behind representation problems. Asking whether an integer is represented by a form is asking whether a certain quadratic equation has integer solutions. The discriminant controls which primes can appear, which is the bridge from forms to quadratic reciprocity.
