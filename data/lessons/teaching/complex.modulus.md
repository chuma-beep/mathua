# Modulus of a Complex Number

**Modulus:** For $z=a+bi$, the modulus (absolute value) is

$$|z|=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$$

It is the distance from $z$ to the origin in the complex plane and satisfies $|z|^{2}=z\bar{z}$.

## Geometric Meaning

### Distance to the Origin
$|z|$ is the Euclidean distance $\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$. For $z=3+4i$, $|z|=\sqrt{9+16}=5$.

### Product with Conjugate
$z\bar{z}=(a+bi)(a-bi)=a^{2}+b^{2}=|z|^{2}$, so $|z|=\sqrt{z\bar{z}}$.

## Example

Find $|3+4i|$: $\sqrt{3^{2}+4^{2}}=\sqrt{25}=5$. Find $|5-12i|$: $\sqrt{25+144}=13$.
