# Trigonometric limits

This lesson develops trigonometric limits through three connected ideas. Work through the sections in order and check each example before moving on.

## The fundamental sine limit

The limit of sine of x divided by x as x approaches zero is one. The angle must be measured in radians for this limit to hold.

The result follows from comparing the areas of a triangle, a sector and a larger triangle. It is the foundation for the derivatives of the trigonometric functions.

A common error is to use degrees. In degrees the limit is pi divided by one hundred eighty, so always convert before applying the rule.

## Scaling the argument

If the argument is k times x, the limit of sine of k x divided by x is k. The factor appears because the argument and the denominator must be matched.

For a ratio of two scaled sines, the limit is the ratio of the two scale factors. The constants must be nonzero for the division to make sense.

Multiply and divide by the missing factor rather than guessing. The algebraic step makes the cancellation visible.

## Related trigonometric limits

The limit of one minus cosine of x divided by x squared is one half. The limit of tangent of x divided by x is one.

These follow from the fundamental sine limit and the Pythagorean identity. They are used when differentiating sine, cosine and tangent from the definition.

Combine the limits with the limit laws. A product or quotient of trigonometric expressions can often be reduced to known pieces.
