# Vectors in Three Dimensions

## Components and length

A three-dimensional vector has three components.
Its length uses the sum of three squares.
The length formula extends the two-dimensional distance formula.

A point in space needs three coordinates, so a displacement also needs three components. The distance formula extends by adding a third squared difference under the square root.

## Operations

Addition and scalar multiplication work component by component.
The dot product multiplies matching components and adds the results.
A zero dot product means the vectors are perpendicular.

The dot product in three dimensions follows the same pattern as in two dimensions. It measures how much of one vector lies along another and produces a single number. Perpendicular vectors have a dot product of zero just as they do in the plane.

## Geometry in space

A vector gives a direction and a length in space.
The difference of two position vectors gives a displacement.
Lengths and angles follow the same rules as in the plane.

Three-dimensional vectors describe velocity, force and displacement in physical space. The same algebra used for plane vectors therefore carries over without new rules. Coordinates, lengths and angles remain the basic tools for analysing motion in space.
