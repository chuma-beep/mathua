## Definition of ideals

An ideal $I$ of a ring $R$ is a non-empty subset such that (i) $I$ is closed under addition, and (ii) for every $r \in R$ and $x \in I$, both $r x$ and $x r$ are in $I$ (absorption).

### Example

$2Z$ is an ideal of $Z$: even plus even is even, and any integer times an even is even.

## Examples and non-examples

Multiples $nZ$ are ideals of $Z$. Odd integers are not ideals because odd plus odd is even, leaving the set.

### Example

$\{0,2,4\}$ is an ideal of $Z_6$: closed under addition mod $6$ and absorbs multiplication.

## Principal ideals

The set $(a) = \{ r a : r \in R \}$ is the principal ideal generated by $a$.
