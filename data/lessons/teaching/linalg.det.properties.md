# Properties of Determinants

## Scaling and products

Determinants respond predictably to row operations. Adding a multiple of one row to another leaves the determinant unchanged, swapping two rows changes its sign, and scaling a single row by k multiplies the determinant by k. Scaling the whole n by n matrix by k multiplies the determinant by k raised to n.

## Determinants of inverses

The determinant is multiplicative, so det(AB) = det(A)det(B) for square matrices of the same size. This rule extends to products of several matrices and explains why an inverse has determinant equal to the reciprocal of the original determinant.

## Singular matrices

A square matrix has determinant zero exactly when its rows or columns are linearly dependent. In that case the matrix is singular and cannot be inverted. A repeated row, a zero row, or a column that is a multiple of another all force the determinant to vanish.
