# Areas of Circular Segments

## Sector and Triangle

A circular segment is the region between a chord and its arc. It is the sector minus the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.

The sector area is the fraction of the circle given by the central angle, and the triangle area uses half the product of the radii and the sine of the angle.

For a right central angle, the triangle is right isosceles and its area is half the square of the radius.

## Computing a Segment

Subtract the triangle area from the sector area, keeping the same units throughout.

For a 90 degree angle, the sector is a quarter of the circle and the triangle is half the square of the radius.

An exact answer may contain pi. A decimal answer should state the number of places used.

## Recovering a Radius

If the segment area and angle are known, work backward through the sector and triangle formulas.

For a quarter circle, the sector area coefficient is pi times the radius squared divided by four, which makes the radius easy to isolate.

Check that the recovered radius is positive and that the segment area is smaller than the sector area.
