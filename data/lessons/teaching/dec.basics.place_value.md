# Decimal Place Value

## Places to the Right

Each place to the right of the decimal point is one tenth of the place before it. The names are tenths, hundredths, thousandths and so on.

In 4.276, the 2 is in the tenths place, the 7 in the hundredths place and the 6 in the thousandths place.

A zero in a place still matters for the value of the digits around it, even though it adds nothing to the digit sum.

## Value of a Digit

The value of a digit is the digit multiplied by the value of its place. In 4.276 the 7 is worth 7 hundredths, or 0.07.

The same digit has different values in different positions. A 3 in the tenths place is ten times a 3 in the hundredths place.

Expanded form writes a number as the sum of these place values.

## Reading and Writing

Read the whole part, then the decimal part as a single number named by its last place. 4.276 is four and two hundred seventy-six thousandths.

When writing from words, count the places named by the final word and fill any gaps with zeros.

Aligning decimal points makes addition, subtraction and comparison reliable.
