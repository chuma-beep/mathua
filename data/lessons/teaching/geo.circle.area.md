# Area of a Circle

The area enclosed by a circle depends on just one measurement — the radius:

\[A = \pi r^2\]

where $r$ is the radius and $\pi \approx 3.14159$. Note the difference from circumference: area involves $r^2$ and comes out in square units, while circumference is linear.

## Where the Formula Comes From

Slice a circle into many thin pie wedges, then rearrange them alternating up and down. The wedges fit into an almost-rectangle whose height is the radius $r$ and whose width is half the circumference, $\pi r$. Rectangle area gives:

\[A = (\pi r) \cdot r = \pi r^2\]

The more slices, the closer the shape gets to a true rectangle.

## Example

**Example**

Find the area of a circle with radius $5$ m. Give an exact answer and one rounded to the nearest tenth.

*Solution*

Exact: $A = \pi(5)^2 = 25\pi\text{ m}^2$

Rounded:

\[25\pi \approx 25(3.14159) = 78.5398 \approx 78.5\]

**Example**

A circle has diameter $12$ cm. Find its area.

*Solution*

First find the radius: $r = \frac{12}{2} = 6$ cm.

\[A = \pi(6)^2 = 36\pi \approx 113.1\text{ cm}^2\]

Watch for this two-step pattern: when a problem gives a diameter, halve it before squaring.

## Area vs. Circumference

| | Circumference | Area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formula | $C = 2\pi r$ | $A = \pi r^2$ |
| Units | plain length | square units |

## Summary

- Circle area: $A = \pi r^2$.
- Convert diameter to radius ($r = \frac{d}{2}$) before using the formula.
- Area answers come in square units.
