# Multiplying Two Two-Digit Numbers

## Splitting Both Factors

A two-digit number is a sum of tens and ones. When both factors have two digits, split each one before multiplying. The product then becomes four smaller products that are added together.

For 34 times 26, write 34 as 30 + 4 and 26 as 20 + 6. The four pieces are 30 times 20, 30 times 6, 4 times 20 and 4 times 6. Add the pieces at the end rather than in the middle so a lost term is easy to spot.

## The Standard Algorithm

The written method records the same four products in two rows. Multiply by the ones digit first, then multiply by the tens digit and shift the result one place left. Adding the rows gives the product.

Keep the columns aligned by place value. A common error is to forget the zero that holds the empty ones place in the second row. Writing the zero explicitly makes the alignment visible and prevents a product that is ten times too small.

## Checking the Result

Estimate before multiplying. Round each factor to the nearest ten and multiply the rounded values. The exact product should be close to that estimate, so a result far away signals a place-value mistake rather than a small slip.

A second check uses the ones digits. The ones digit of the product must match the ones digit of the product of the two ones digits. If 34 times 26 ends in 4, the final digit should be 4 because 4 times 6 is 24.
