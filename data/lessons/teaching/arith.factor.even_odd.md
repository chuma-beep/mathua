# Even and Odd Numbers

## The Definition

An even number can be split into two equal whole-number groups. An odd number leaves one over. The last digit decides the parity because every place above the ones is a multiple of ten and ten is even.

A number ending in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 is even. A number ending in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9 is odd. The rule applies to numbers of any size.

## Parity of Sums and Differences

Adding two even numbers gives an even number. Adding two odd numbers also gives an even number because the two leftovers pair up. Adding an even number and an odd number gives an odd number.

Subtraction follows the same pattern because subtraction is addition of the opposite. The parity of a difference depends only on whether the two starting numbers have the same parity.

## Parity of Products

A product is even if either factor is even. An even factor contributes a factor of two, so the product is divisible by two. A product is odd only when both factors are odd.

This rule explains why a multiplication table has even answers in whole rows and columns. It also gives a fast check on a computed product: an even times an odd cannot produce an odd result.
