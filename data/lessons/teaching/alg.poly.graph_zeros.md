# Zeros and the Graph of a Polynomial

## What a Zero Is

A zero of a polynomial P is a number r with P(r) = 0. On the graph, zeros are exactly the x-intercepts, the points where the curve meets the horizontal axis. Factoring is the usual way to find them: a product is zero exactly when one of its factors is zero, so each linear factor x - r contributes the zero r. A polynomial of degree n has at most n real zeros, and counting complex zeros with multiplicity it has exactly n.

## Multiplicity and Crossing

The exponent on a factor controls how the graph behaves at that zero. A factor with exponent one gives a simple zero and the graph crosses the axis. An even exponent makes the graph touch the axis and turn back, because the sign of the factor does not change across the zero. An odd exponent greater than one gives a flattened crossing. Reading these multiplicities turns a factored expression into a reliable sketch without plotting many points.

## Sketching from Factors

The leading term determines what happens far to the left and far to the right. An even degree sends both ends the same way, up for a positive leading coefficient and down for a negative one. An odd degree sends the ends opposite ways. Combining end behaviour with the zeros and their multiplicities gives the shape of the graph, and checking one test point fixes whether the curve lies above or below the axis between consecutive zeros.
