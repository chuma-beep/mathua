# Average and instantaneous rates of change

This lesson develops average and instantaneous rates of change through three connected ideas. Work through the sections in order and check each example before moving on.

## Average rate over an interval

The average rate of change over an interval is the change in output divided by the change in input. It is the slope of the secant line through the two endpoints.

The average rate ignores what happens between the endpoints. Two functions can share an average rate over an interval while behaving very differently inside it.

Units matter. If the output is a distance and the input is a time, the average rate is a speed. Label the units in the final answer.

## Instantaneous rate at a point

The instantaneous rate is the limit of average rates as the interval shrinks toward the point. It is the derivative at that point.

Geometrically it is the slope of the tangent line. Physically it is the speed at an instant, or the marginal cost at a production level.

A positive instantaneous rate means the output is increasing at that moment. A negative rate means it is decreasing. A zero rate means the tangent is horizontal.

## Comparing the two rates

The average rate and the instantaneous rate agree for a linear function. For a nonlinear function they generally differ, and the difference carries information about curvature.

The mean value theorem guarantees that some interior point has an instantaneous rate equal to the average rate over the interval, under the usual continuity and differentiability hypotheses.

In applications, report both rates when the distinction matters. A driver may have an average speed of sixty but an instantaneous speed above the limit.
