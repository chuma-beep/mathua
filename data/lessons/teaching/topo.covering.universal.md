# Universal covering spaces

## Lifting Paths

A covering map is a local homeomorphism with evenly covered fibres. Paths and homotopies lift uniquely once a starting point is chosen. This lifting property is the technical heart of covering space theory and the reason covering spaces compute fundamental groups.

The circle is the model case. The map from the real line to the circle sending t to the point at angle 2 pi t is a covering. A path in the circle lifts to a path in the line, and the endpoint records the winding number.

Lifting is unique for a fixed starting point. That uniqueness turns the set of lifts into a discrete fibre and gives a bijection between the fibre and the fundamental group under suitable hypotheses.

## The Universal Cover

A universal cover is a simply connected covering space. It exists whenever the space is connected, locally path connected and semilocally simply connected. The universal cover of the circle is the real line, and the universal cover of the torus is the plane.

The universal cover is unique up to homeomorphism over the base. Its group of deck transformations is isomorphic to the fundamental group of the base. The cover is regular, and every other connected cover is a quotient of it by a subgroup.

The sphere covers the real projective plane with two sheets. The plane covers the torus with infinitely many sheets. These examples show how the fibre size records the index of the corresponding subgroup.

## Deck Transformations

A deck transformation is a homeomorphism of the cover that commutes with the covering map. Deck transformations form a group under composition, and for the universal cover that group is the fundamental group of the base.

The action of the deck group is free and properly discontinuous when the cover is universal. The quotient by the action recovers the base. This gives a dictionary between connected covers, subgroups of the fundamental group and actions of the group.

Computing the deck group is often the quickest route to the fundamental group. For the circle the deck group is the integers, acting by translation. For the torus it is the integer lattice acting by translation on the plane.
