# Reading derivatives from a graph

This lesson develops reading derivatives from a graph through three connected ideas. Work through the sections in order and check each example before moving on.

## Slope and sign from a graph

The derivative at a point is the slope of the tangent line there. A graph that rises from left to right has a positive derivative on that interval.

A graph that falls has a negative derivative. A horizontal tangent gives a zero derivative. Estimate the slope from a grid by choosing two points on the tangent line.

The sign of the derivative is easier to read than its exact value. Use the sign first to locate increasing and decreasing intervals.

## Turning points and extrema

A local maximum occurs where the derivative changes from positive to negative. A local minimum occurs where it changes from negative to positive.

A zero derivative alone does not guarantee an extremum. The sign change is the decisive evidence. A flat inflection point has zero derivative without a sign change.

Count the sign changes on a derivative graph to count the extrema. This is often faster than computing and testing every critical point.

## Relating the graphs of f and f prime

Where the graph of f is steep, the graph of its derivative is far from the horizontal axis. Where f is flat, the derivative is close to zero.

A local extremum of f corresponds to a zero of the derivative. An inflection point of f corresponds to an extremum of the derivative, not necessarily a zero.

Sketch the derivative beneath the original graph and align the features vertically. The exercise builds the habit of reading one graph through another.
