# Separable extensions

## Repeated Roots

A polynomial is separable when it has no repeated root in an algebraic closure.

Equivalently, it is coprime with its formal derivative.

A field extension is separable when every element has a separable minimal polynomial.

## Characteristic Zero and Characteristic p

Over a field of characteristic zero every irreducible polynomial is separable.

In characteristic p the derivative of x to the p minus a is zero.

That vanishing derivative produces a purely inseparable extension.

## Finite Fields

Every finite extension of a finite field is separable because the Frobenius map is bijective.

The derivative test remains the quickest computational check.

Typical questions ask for a derivative degree or for the number of distinct roots.

The derivative is the fastest separability test. If the derivative shares a nonconstant factor with the polynomial, a repeated root exists. In characteristic zero this cannot happen for an irreducible polynomial, so the interesting cases are all in positive characteristic.
