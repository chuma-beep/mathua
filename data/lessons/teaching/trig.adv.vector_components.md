# Vector Components

## Breaking a Vector Apart

A vector with magnitude r and direction theta has horizontal component r cos(theta) and vertical component r sin(theta).

The components form a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the magnitude. The angle is measured from the positive horizontal axis.

A negative component means the vector points left or down, so keep the sign rather than taking an absolute value.

## From Components to Magnitude

The magnitude is the square root of the sum of the squares of the components. This is the Pythagorean theorem.

The direction is found from the tangent of the angle, with the quadrant adjusted for the signs of the components.

A 3-4-5 component pair has magnitude 5, which makes many textbook examples exact.

## Using Components

Components add separately: add the horizontal parts and add the vertical parts. The resultant is then rebuilt from the sums.

This separation is why vector problems are easier in components than with magnitudes and angles alone.

Check the resultant by estimating its direction from a sketch before computing the exact angle.
