# Parts of a Circle: Radius, Diameter, Chord

A **circle** is the set of all points exactly the same distance from a fixed point called the **center**. That fixed distance gives the circle its size.

## The Vocabulary

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Center | the point every edge point is equidistant from |
| Radius ($r$) | segment from the center to any point on the circle |
| Diameter ($d$) | segment through the center connecting two points on the circle |
| Chord | segment joining any two points on the circle |
| Arc | part of the circle between two points on it |

Two facts connect these:

\[d = 2r \qquad r = \frac{d}{2}\]

Every diameter is a chord — the longest possible one — but not every chord passes through the center.

## Example

**Example**

A circle has a radius of $6$ cm. What is its diameter?

*Solution*

\[d = 2r = 2(6) = 12\]

The diameter is $12$ cm.

**Example**

A chord of a circle measures $9$ inches. Could the radius be $4$ inches?

*Solution*

No. The longest chord is the diameter, so every chord must be no longer than the diameter. A $9$-inch chord requires a diameter of at least $9$ inches, i.e. a radius of at least $4.5$ inches.

## Naming Arcs

An arc is named by its endpoints. A **semicircle** is an arc of exactly half the circle ($180^\circ$). An arc smaller than a semicircle is a **minor arc**; larger is a **major arc**.

## Summary

- Radius runs center → circle; diameter runs across through the center; $d = 2r$.
- A chord connects any two points on the circle; the diameter is the longest chord.
- Arcs are pieces of the circle itself.
