# Point-Slope Form of a Line

## The Point-Slope Form

The point-slope form of a line is y - y1 = m(x - x1), where m is the slope and (x1, y1) is any point on the line. It follows directly from the slope formula: the slope between (x1, y1) and any other point (x, y) is (y - y1)/(x - x1), and setting that equal to m gives the form after multiplying both sides. Because any point on the line works, the same line has many correct point-slope equations.

## Building an Equation

To build the equation from two points, first compute the slope as the change in y divided by the change in x, then substitute either point into the form. The arithmetic is usually easiest with the point that has smaller or positive coordinates, but the resulting equation is equivalent. A horizontal line has slope zero and equation y = y1; a vertical line has undefined slope and equation x = x1, which cannot be written in point-slope form.

## Converting Forms

Converting to slope-intercept form means distributing m and adding y1 to both sides, which exposes the y-intercept. Converting to standard form means clearing fractions and moving both variables to one side. Each form displays different information: point-slope shows a point and a direction, slope-intercept shows the intercept and slope, and standard form is convenient for systems. Being able to move between them is more useful than memorising one as the only correct answer.
