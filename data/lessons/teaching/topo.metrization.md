# Urysohn Metrization Theorem

**Urysohn metrization:** Every second-countable regular Hausdorff space is metrizable. In particular, compact Hausdorff second-countable spaces are metrizable.

## When Topology Comes from a Metric

### The Hypotheses
Regular + Hausdorff + second-countable (countable base) suffices. Regularity gives Urysohn functions; countability builds an embedding into $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$ (Hilbert cube), which is metric.

### Consequences
Metrizable spaces are Hausdorff, normal, and paracompact. Non-metrizable examples like the Sorgenfrey line fail the hypotheses (not second-countable in the metric sense).

## Example

$[0,1]$ is compact Hausdorff and second-countable, hence metrizable (the usual metric). $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$ with product topology is second-countable regular Hausdorff, hence metrizable via $d(x,y)=\sum2^{-n}\min(1,|x_n-y_n|)$.
