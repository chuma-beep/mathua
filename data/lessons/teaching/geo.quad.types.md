# Types of Quadrilaterals

A **quadrilateral** is a closed figure with four straight sides. The angles of any quadrilateral total $360^\circ$. Special quadrilaterals are named by their parallel sides, equal sides, and right angles.

## The Family of Quadrilaterals

| Name | Properties |
| --- | --- |
| Trapezoid | exactly one pair of parallel sides |
| Parallelogram | both pairs of opposite sides parallel |
| Rectangle | parallelogram with four right angles |
| Rhombus | parallelogram with four equal sides |
| Square | rectangle that is also a rhombus: four equal sides and four right angles |
| Kite | two pairs of adjacent sides equal |

## The Hierarchy

These names nest inside each other:

- Every rectangle is a parallelogram.
- Every rhombus is a parallelogram.
- Every square is a rectangle **and** a rhombus (and therefore also a parallelogram).

So a square earns all five labels at once, while a trapezoid earns only its own.

Opposite sides of a parallelogram are not only parallel but also equal in length, and opposite angles are equal.

## Example

**Example**

A quadrilateral has four equal sides and no right angles. What is it?

*Solution*

Four equal sides make it a rhombus. Without right angles it is not a square or a rectangle. Classification: rhombus (which is also a parallelogram).

**Example**

A quadrilateral has one pair of parallel sides of lengths $9$ and $5$. Can it be a parallelogram?

*Solution*

No. A parallelogram needs *both* pairs of opposite sides parallel — which forces opposite sides to be equal in length. Since $9 \neq 5$, this shape is a trapezoid.

## Summary

- Quadrilateral: four sides; interior angles total $360^\circ$.
- Trapezoid → parallelogram → rectangle/rhombus → square is the nesting order.
- A square is simultaneously a rectangle, a rhombus, and a parallelogram.
