# Finitely generated modules

## Finite Generation

A module is finitely generated when finitely many elements produce every element by linear combinations.

Over a principal ideal domain the structure theorem separates a free part from a torsion part.

The theorem generalizes the classification of finite abelian groups.

## Free Rank and Torsion

The rank of the free part is the number of independent generators.

The torsion submodule contains the elements annihilated by a nonzero scalar.

For a product of cyclic groups the torsion order is the product of the cyclic orders.

## Fields versus the Integers

Over a field every finitely generated module is a finite-dimensional vector space, so the torsion part vanishes.

Over the integers the torsion part can be nontrivial.

The contrast explains why the same theorem looks different in linear algebra and in abelian group theory.

To read a finitely generated module, separate its free rank from its torsion subgroup. The rank is the number of copies of the base ring. The torsion order is the product of the cyclic orders that remain after the free part is removed.
