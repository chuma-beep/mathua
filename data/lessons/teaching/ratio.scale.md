# Geometry Applications

## Solving Equations for Geometry Problems

With geometry problems in algebra,
      it is really helpful to draw a picture to understand the scenario better.
      After drawing the shape and labeling the given information, we will choose the formula to use from the list in Subsection .

**Example**
An Olympic-size swimming pool is rectangular and 50 in length.
          We don't know its width, but we do know that it required 150 of painter's tape to outline the edge of the pool during recent renovations.
          Use this information to set up an equation and find the width of the pool.

*Solution*
Since it required 150 of painter's tape to outline the pool,
          we know the perimeter of the pool is 150 .
          This suggests using the perimeter formula for a rectangle: $P=2(\ell+w)$ . (This formula was discussed in Subsection ).
With this formula,
          we can substitute $150$ in for $P$ and $50$ in for $\ell$ : \[
            150=2(50+w).
          \] Now we can solve the equation for the width of the pool.
We found that the width of the pool is $25$ meters.


Let's look at another example. In this one we need to use an algebraic expression for one of the sides of a rectangle.

**Example**
Azul is designing a rectangular garden and they have $40$ meters of wood planking for the border.
          Their garden's length is $4$ meters less than three times the width,
          and the perimeter must be $40$ meters.
          Find the garden's length and width.

*Solution*
Let Azul's garden width be $w$ meters.
          We can then represent the length as $3w-4$ meters since we are told that it is $4$ meters less than three times the width.
          It's given that the perimeter is $40$ meters.
          Substituting those values into the formula, we have: P\amp=2(\ell+w) 40\amp=2(3w-4+w) 40\amp=2(4w-4)\amp\text{Like terms were combined.}
To determine the length,
          recall that this was represented by $3w-4$ , which is: 3w-4\amp=3(\substitute{6})-4 \amp=14 .
          Thus, the width of Azul's garden is $6$ meters and the length is $14$ meters.


For triangle problems, we may need to use the Pythagorean Theorem that we learned in Subsection . If we know the lengths of two sides of a right triangle then we can find the length of the third side.

**Example**
Tan owns a road sign manufacturing company and he is producing triangular yield signs for the State of Oregon. The signs are equilateral triangles measuring $36$ inches on each side
          as shown in Figure . Find the area of one sign in square feet to help Tan estimate the amount of material he needs to produce the signs.

*Solution*
We will start by converting $36$ inches to $3$ feet, because the area needs to be in square feet.
          The area of a triangle is found using $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$ ,
          where $A$ is the area, $b$ is the width of the base, and $h$ is the height. In this case the base is at the top of the triangle.
We know the width of the triangle is $3$ feet, but we don't know the height. By drawing in the height we form two right triangles so we can use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the height. Half of the width is $1.5$ feet, so we will substitute for $b$ and $c$ in the pythagorean theorem.
Now we can calculate the area of one sign. A\amp=\frac{1}{2}bh \amp\approx\frac{1}{2}(3)(\substitute{2.598}) \amp=3.897 The area of one sign is approximately 3.897 .

Now we will look at an example that involves a circle. It can be difficult to measure the radius of a circle or cylinder. But if we can measure the circumference, then we can find the radius.

**Example**
Batula wants to order a custom replacement column for the front of her house and she needs to know the radius. She takes a string and wraps it around the old column. She measures the string and finds the circumference is $3$ feet, $2.5$ inches. What is the radius of the column?

*Solution*
The formula for the circumference of a circle is $C=2\pi r$ ,
          where $C$ stands for the circumference and $r$ stands for the radius.
We will let the radius of Batula's column be $r$ inches.
          It's given that the circumference is $3$ feet, $2.5$ inches, so let's convert $3$ feet into inches. 3\,\text{ft}\amp=\frac{3\,\text{ft}}{1}\cdot\frac{12\,\text{in}}{1\,\text{ft}} \amp=\frac{3\,\cancel{\text{ft}}}{1}\cdot\frac{12\,\text{in}}{1\,\cancel{\text{ft}}} \amp=3\cdot12\,\text{in} \amp=36\,\text{in} Since $3$ feet is $36$ inches, we can add the $2.5$ inches for a total of $38.5$ inches. Substituting the circumference into the formula, we have: C\amp=2\pi r 38.5\amp=2\pi r
Therefore, Batula should order a column with a radius of $6.127$ inches.
          A specific measurement like that may not be possible,
          but Batula could round to something like $6\,\frac{1}{8}$ inches,
          which is very close.
          If the manufacturer wanted the diameter instead,
          we would multiply that by $2$ to get $12.25$ or $6\,\frac{1}{4}$ inches.

Here is an example using volume.

**Example**
Mark is designing a cylindrical container for his ice cream business. He wants each container to be $15$ centimeters tall and hold $1$ gallon of ice cream. What dimension should Mark use for the radius of the container?

*Solution*
The formula for the volume of a cylinder is $V=\pi r^2h$ ,
          where $V$ stands for the volume, $r$ stands for the radius and $h$ is the height.
Since the volume is in gallons and the dimensions are in centimeters, we need to convert $1$ gallon to cubic centimeters. 1\,\text{gal}\amp=\frac{1\,\text{gal}}{1}\cdot\frac{231\,\text{in}^3}{1\,\text{gal}}\cdot\frac{2.54^3\,\text{cm}^3}{1\,\text{in}^3} \amp=\frac{1\,\cancel{\text{gal}}}{1}\cdot\frac{231\,\cancel{\text{in}^3}}{1\,\cancel{\text{gal}}}\cdot\frac{2.54^3\,\text{cm}^3}{1\,\cancel{\text{in}^3}} \amp=1\cdot231\cdot2.54^3\,\text{cm}^3 \amp=3785.41\,\text{cm}^3 Now we can substitute the volume and height into the formula: V\amp=\pi r^2h 3785.41\amp=\pi r^2(15)
Note that our check is approximate because we rounded our answer.
          Mark will want to make the radius of his container at least $8.963$ centimeters.
          He should make it a little larger to have space at the top of the container.
