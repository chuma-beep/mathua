# Circumference of a Circle

The **circumference** is the perimeter of a circle — the distance around it. What makes circles special is that the distance around is always the same multiple of the distance across, for every circle, no matter its size.

## The Number Pi

Measure any circle's circumference and divide by its diameter: you get approximately $3.14159\ldots$ — the famous constant $\pi$ (pi). Since $\pi = \frac{C}{d}$ for every circle:

\[C = \pi d\]

and since $d = 2r$, equivalently:

\[C = 2\pi r\]

For estimating, use $\pi \approx 3.14$ or the fraction $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$; keep answers exact with the $\pi$ symbol unless asked to round.

## Example

**Example**

A circle has diameter $10$ inches. Find its circumference exactly, then to the nearest hundredth.

*Solution*

Exact: $C = \pi d = 10\pi$ inches.

Using $\pi \approx 3.14159$:

\[C \approx 10(3.14159) = 31.4159 \approx 31.42\]

**Example**

Find the circumference of a circle with radius $7$ cm.

*Solution*

\[C = 2\pi r = 2\pi(7) = 14\pi \approx 43.98\text{ cm}\]

## Unrolling a Circle

Imagine wrapping a string around the circle once, then unrolling it flat: the string's length is $d$ lengths of about $3.14$ laid end to end. That is why $C = \pi d$.

## Summary

- Circumference: the distance around a circle.
- $C = \pi d = 2\pi r$, with $\pi \approx 3.14159$.
- The ratio $\frac{C}{d} = \pi$ is identical for all circles.
