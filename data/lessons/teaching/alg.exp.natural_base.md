# The Natural Base e

## Where e Comes From

The number e is approximately 2.71828 and appears whenever growth is compounded continuously. It is the limit of (1 + 1/n)^n as n grows without bound, which is why it describes a quantity that grows at a rate proportional to its current size. Unlike a rounded base such as 2.7, e has the property that the function e^x has slope equal to its value at every point, and that is what makes it the natural base for calculus and for continuous models.

## The Exponential Function

The function f(x) = e^x is always positive, passes through the point (0, 1), and grows quickly to the right while decaying toward zero to the left. Its inverse is the natural logarithm ln x, defined only for positive x. The two statements y = e^x and x = ln y say the same thing, so converting between them is the main skill. For example, ln 1 = 0 because e^0 = 1, and ln e = 1 because e^1 = e.

## Natural Logarithms

To solve an equation such as e^(2x) = 7, take the natural logarithm of both sides and use ln(e^u) = u to obtain 2x = ln 7, so x = (ln 7)/2. Keeping the answer exact is usually better than rounding early. The same idea converts a continuous growth model A = P e^(rt) into a statement about doubling time or about the continuous rate r, and it connects exponential growth to the logarithm rules used elsewhere in algebra.
