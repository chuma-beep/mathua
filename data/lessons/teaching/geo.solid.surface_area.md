# Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism

The **surface area** of a solid is the total area of all its faces — the amount of wrapping paper needed to cover it exactly. For a rectangular prism, add the areas of the six rectangles.

## The Formula

Each face appears twice (top/bottom, front/back, left/right):

| Pair | Area |
| --- | --- |
| top and bottom | $2lw$ |
| front and back | $2lh$ |
| left and right | $2wh$ |

Adding them:

\[SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)\]

Surface area answers come in **square units** — each face contributes an area, not a volume.

## Example

**Example**

Find the surface area of a prism measuring $5$ cm by $4$ cm by $3$ cm.

*Solution*

\[SA = 2(5 \cdot 4) + 2(5 \cdot 3) + 2(4 \cdot 3)\]
\[SA = 40 + 30 + 24 = 94\]

The surface area is $94\text{ cm}^2$.

**Example**

A cube has edges of $2$ m. Find its surface area.

*Solution*

All six faces are $2 \times 2$ squares:

\[SA = 6 \cdot (2 \cdot 2) = 6 \cdot 4 = 24\]

The surface area is $24\text{ m}^2$.

## Volume vs. Surface Area

| | Volume | Surface area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measures | space inside | covering outside |
| Formula ($l,w,h$) | $V = lwh$ | $SA = 2(lw+lh+wh)$ |
| Units | cubic | square |

## Summary

- Surface area = total area of every face.
- Rectangular prism: $SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)$; cube: $SA = 6s^2$.
- Answers come in square units.
