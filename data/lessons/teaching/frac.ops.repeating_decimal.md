# Repeating Decimals

## Terminating and Repeating

A fraction in lowest terms produces a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. Any other prime factor produces a repeating block.

One eighth terminates because 8 is 2 cubed. One sixth repeats because 6 contains a factor of 3. Simplifying first matters, since a denominator of 6 may reduce to 3 or 2.

## The Length of the Block

The repeating block length is the smallest positive k for which ten to the k leaves remainder one when divided by the denominator, once the factors 2 and 5 are removed.

For one seventh the remainders cycle through six values, so the block has six digits. For one eleventh the block has two digits. A longer block is not a different kind of decimal, only a longer cycle.

## Writing the Decimal

Long division reveals the block. Continue dividing, record each remainder and stop when a remainder repeats. The digits produced between the two appearances of that remainder form the repeating block.

A repeating decimal is written with a bar over the block. Zero point three repeating equals one third, and zero point one six repeating equals one sixth. The bar is shorthand for the infinite repetition.
