# Symmetry and definite integrals

This lesson develops symmetry and definite integrals through three connected ideas. Work through the sections in order and check each example before moving on.

## Even functions

An even function is symmetric about the vertical axis. The area on the left of the axis equals the area on the right, so the integral over a symmetric interval is twice the integral over half of it.

The rule requires the interval to be symmetric about zero. If the interval is shifted, translate it first or use a substitution.

An even integrand can still have a large value. Symmetry halves the work, not the answer.

## Odd functions

An odd function has half-turn symmetry about the origin. The area above the axis on one side cancels the signed area below the axis on the other side.

The integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval is therefore zero, provided the integral exists. This is a cancellation of signed areas, not a claim that the function is zero.

Improper integrals need extra care. The symmetric limit may exist even when the two one-sided improper integrals diverge separately.

## Combining even and odd parts

Any function can be split into an even part and an odd part. On a symmetric interval the odd part integrates to zero and only the even part contributes.

This decomposition explains why many Fourier coefficients vanish. It also gives a quick check on a complicated integral before any antiderivative is attempted.

Test the integrand for symmetry first, then compute only the surviving part. State the symmetry you used in the explanation.
