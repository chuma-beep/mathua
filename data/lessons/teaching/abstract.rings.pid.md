# Principal ideal domains

## Principal Ideals

An ideal in a ring is principal when one element generates it. A principal ideal domain is an integral domain in which every ideal has this form.

The integers and the polynomial ring over a field are the two examples that carry most of the intuition.

When every ideal is principal, divisibility and ideal containment become the same relation.

## Gcd and Lcm

The ideal generated by two integers is generated by their greatest common divisor.

The intersection of two principal ideals is generated by their least common multiple.

These identities let you answer ideal questions with ordinary arithmetic.

## The Domain Chain

Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain, and every principal ideal domain is a unique factorization domain.

The reverse implications fail, so the chain is a one-way supply of theorems.

Remembering the direction prevents you from assuming that every unique factorization domain has a division algorithm.

When an ideal is presented by several generators, compute the gcd of the generators to replace them with one. For intersections compute the lcm. Both operations keep the answer inside the ring and avoid a search through all elements.
