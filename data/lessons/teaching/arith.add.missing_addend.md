# Finding a Missing Addend

## The Addition Relationship

An equation such as 7 plus what equals 12 hides one addend. The total and the other addend are known. Subtraction recovers the missing part because addition and subtraction are inverse operations.

The missing addend is the total minus the known addend. In the example, 12 minus 7 equals 5. Substituting 5 back into the original sentence gives 7 plus 5 equals 12, which confirms the answer.

## Larger Numbers

The same method works for multi-digit numbers. If 248 plus what equals 615, subtract 248 from 615. The missing addend is 367.

Column subtraction with borrowing may be needed. Line up the place values, subtract from the ones, and regroup when a digit is too small. The check is always to add the two addends and compare with the total.

## Words That Hide an Addend

Word problems rarely say plus what. Phrases such as how many more are needed or how many are still missing describe the same unknown addend.

Identify the total and the known part before computing. If a shelf holds 40 books and 17 are on it, the number still to be placed is 40 minus 17, which is 23.
