# Proof by Cases

## Splitting into Cases

A proof by cases divides the situation into possibilities that cover every outcome, proves the claim in each case and concludes that the claim always holds.

The cases must be exhaustive, and they may overlap as long as every object falls into at least one of them. A missing case leaves a gap in the proof.

A good division makes each case simpler than the original problem, often by fixing a parity, a sign or an ordering.

## Choosing the Cases

Parity gives two cases, even and odd. Residues modulo m give m cases. Sign analysis of a product of k factors gives 2 to the power k sign patterns.

The number of cases should match the structure of the claim. Too few cases hide work, and too many repeat the same argument.

State the cases explicitly before proving them so a reader can check that the list is exhaustive.

## Writing the Proof

Begin by announcing the case split, then prove each case under its own heading. Use the case assumption in the argument and avoid relying on facts that hold only in another case.

At the end, state that the cases exhaust the possibilities and therefore the claim is proved. That sentence is part of the proof, not decoration.

Proof by cases is often combined with contradiction or with a direct algebraic argument inside each case.
