# Numbers in Standard Form

## Powers of Ten

A power of ten is written with an exponent that counts the zeros. Ten to the third power is 1,000, which has three zeros. Ten to the sixth power is 1,000,000, which has six.

Standard form writes a number as a value between one and ten multiplied by a power of ten. 4,300 becomes 4.3 times ten to the third power. The exponent records how far the decimal point moved.

## Moving the Decimal Point

For numbers larger than one, the exponent is positive and equals the number of places the point moves left. For 52,000 the point moves four places, so the exponent is four and the coefficient is 5.2.

For numbers between zero and one, the exponent is negative and equals the number of places the point moves right. 0.007 becomes 7 times ten to the negative third power because the point moves three places.

## Converting Back

To return to ordinary form, move the decimal point by the exponent. A positive exponent moves the point right and a negative exponent moves it left. Add zeros when the digits run out.

Check the direction against the size of the result. A positive exponent should produce a number larger than the coefficient, while a negative exponent should produce a smaller one. A reversed direction is the most common error.
