# Fact Families

## One Set of Numbers

A fact family is a group of related equations that use the same three numbers. For 3, 4 and 12 the family contains two multiplication facts and two division facts.

The multiplication facts are 3 times 4 equals 12 and 4 times 3 equals 12. The division facts are 12 divided by 3 equals 4 and 12 divided by 4 equals 3. Each fact uses all three numbers once.

## Using a Family to Divide

Knowing a multiplication fact gives two division facts for free. If 6 times 7 equals 42, then 42 divided by 6 equals 7 and 42 divided by 7 equals 6. The unknown factor is the quotient.

This relationship is the reason division facts are taught beside multiplication tables. A learner who hesitates on 42 divided by 6 can recover the answer by asking which number times 6 gives 42.

## Missing Numbers

A missing-number problem names the family without saying so. In 8 times what equals 56, the three family members are 8, 7 and 56. The missing factor is 7 because the product and the other factor are known.

The same reasoning handles a missing divisor. In 56 divided by what equals 8, the missing number is 7. Rewriting the division as a multiplication makes the family visible and removes the guesswork.
