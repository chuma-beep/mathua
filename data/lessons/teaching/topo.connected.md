## Definition

A topological space $X$ is connected if it cannot be written as the disjoint union of two nonempty open sets. Equivalently, the only subsets that are both open and closed are $\emptyset$ and $X$.

### Example

The interval $(0,1)$ is connected, while $(0,1) \cup (2,3)$ is not.

## Examples and characterizations

Intervals in $\mathbb{R}$ are connected. The set $\mathbb{Q}$ of rationals is totally disconnected: between any two rationals lies an irrational that separates them.

### Example

$\mathbb{R}$ is connected. So is any closed interval $[a,b]$.

## Separation by open sets

To show a space is not connected, it suffices to find two disjoint nonempty open sets whose union is the space.
