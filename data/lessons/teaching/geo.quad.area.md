# Area of Rectangles and Parallelograms

Both the rectangle and the parallelogram measure area as "base times height" — a fact that comes from counting unit squares.

## Rectangle

A rectangle with length $l$ and width $w$ is covered by $l$ rows of $w$ squares, so:

\[A = l \cdot w\]

For a square (where $l = w = s$), this becomes $A = s^2$.

## Parallelogram

A parallelogram has a base $b$ and a height $h$ — the perpendicular distance between the base and its opposite side. Slice off the triangular piece at one end and slide it to the other: the parallelogram becomes a rectangle with the same base and height.

\[A = b \cdot h\]

The height must be measured perpendicular to the base — not along the slanted side.

## Example

**Example**

Find the area of a rectangle measuring $8$ m by $5$ m.

*Solution*

\[A = 8 \cdot 5 = 40\]

The area is $40\text{ m}^2$.

**Example**

A parallelogram has base $7$ cm, slanted side $5$ cm, and height $4$ cm. Find its area.

*Solution*

Use the perpendicular height, not the slanted side:

\[A = 7 \cdot 4 = 28\]

The area is $28\text{ cm}^2$. (The side length $5$ is not needed.)

## Units

Lengths multiply to make square units: two lengths in inches give an area in $\text{in}^2$.

## Summary

- Rectangle: $A = lw$; square: $A = s^2$.
- Parallelogram: $A = bh$, with $h$ perpendicular to the base.
- Area is always expressed in square units.
