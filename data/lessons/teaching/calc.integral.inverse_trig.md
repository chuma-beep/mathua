# Integrals yielding inverse trigonometric functions

## The arctangent form



An integrand of the form one over a squared plus x squared produces an arctangent.

The coefficient is one over a.

Complete the square when the denominator is quadratic.

The pattern is easy to recognise once the denominator is a sum of squares. The constant a controls both the scale of the answer and the coefficient in front of the arctangent.

## Completing the square



Rewrite the quadratic as a shift squared plus a constant.

Substitute to reach the standard arctangent form.

Return to the original variable in the final answer.

Completing the square turns any quadratic denominator into the standard form. The shift becomes the inner variable of the arctangent and the remaining constant becomes a.

## Choosing the substitution



Match the integrand to the arcsine or arctangent pattern.

A square root of a squared minus x squared points to arcsine.

Check the sign and the constant before integrating.

Compare the integrand with the derivative formulas for arcsine and arctangent before choosing a method. A square root usually points to arcsine, while a quadratic denominator without a root points to arctangent.
