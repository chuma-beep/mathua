# Angles of Elevation and Depression

## Looking Up and Looking Down

An angle of elevation is measured upward from the horizontal. An angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal.

Both angles are measured from a horizontal line at the observer, not from the vertical. A common error is to use the wrong reference line.

The angle of elevation from A to B equals the angle of depression from B to A when both are measured from horizontal lines.

## Choosing a Ratio

Sketch the right triangle and label the side opposite the angle, the side adjacent to it and the hypotenuse.

If the opposite side and hypotenuse are involved, use sine. If the adjacent side and hypotenuse are involved, use cosine. If the two legs are involved, use tangent.

The unknown is often a height or a horizontal distance, so choose the ratio that contains that side.

## Solving a Height or Distance

Write the ratio with the known values and solve for the unknown. Round only at the end and keep the units.

For a 45 degree elevation, the height equals the horizontal distance because tangent of 45 degrees is one.

For a 60 degree elevation, the height is the distance times the square root of three, so check whether an exact or decimal answer is requested.
