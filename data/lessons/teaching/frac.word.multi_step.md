# Multi-Step Fraction Problems

## Reading the Steps

A multi-step problem describes more than one change. Read once for the situation and a second time for the quantities. Name what is used, what remains and what is asked before computing.

The order of the steps follows the story. A tank filled and then drained uses addition followed by subtraction. A share of a remaining amount uses subtraction followed by multiplication.

## Working With the Remainder

When a problem gives a fraction of what is left, compute the remaining fraction first. Two fifths used leaves three fifths. A later share applies to that three fifths, not to the original whole.

This distinction is the most common source of error. Multiplying the second share by the original amount instead of the remainder overstates the result and breaks the check that all parts add to the whole.

## Checking the Story

Add the parts and compare with the whole. If one half is used, one third of the remainder is used and the rest remains, the three amounts must account for the whole with no gap.

A useful check converts the answer back into the story. If the answer says three eighths remain, then five eighths were used across the steps. Both statements must be consistent.
