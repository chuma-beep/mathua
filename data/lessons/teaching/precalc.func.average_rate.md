# Average Rate of Change

The average rate of change measures how quickly one quantity changes relative to another over an interval. It is the slope of the secant line through two points on a graph.

## Secant Lines and Average Rate

For a function $f$, the average rate of change from $x=a$ to $x=b$ is the slope of the line through $(a,f(a))$ and $(b,f(b))$.

\[
\text{average rate of change}=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}
\]

The line through the two points is a secant line. Unlike a tangent line, it touches the graph twice.

## Computing an Average Rate

For $f(x)=x^2$ on $[1,4]$, the values are $f(1)=1$ and $f(4)=16$. The average rate of change is

\[
\frac{16-1}{4-1}=\frac{15}{3}=5.
\]

The sign matters. A negative result means the function is lower at the right endpoint on average.

## Average Rate as a Bridge to the Derivative

If the interval shrinks toward a single point, the secant line approaches the tangent line. The average rate of change then approaches the instantaneous rate of change, which is the derivative.

Average rates also appear in applications: average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time, and average cost is total cost divided by the number of items.
