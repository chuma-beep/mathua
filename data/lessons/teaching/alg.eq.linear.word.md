# Linear Equation Word Problems

## Translating Words

A linear equation word problem starts with a relationship that stays the same while quantities change. Common examples are two plans with a fixed fee and a per-unit charge, a total cost made of a base price plus a rate, or two quantities that add to a fixed total. The work is to translate the words into expressions and then into one equation that sets two descriptions of the same quantity equal.

## Building the Equation

Name the unknown explicitly, for example let x be the number of months. Write every other quantity in terms of x, including the ones that only appear in the question. A table with a row for each plan or each quantity often makes the relationship visible. The equation should have a clear meaning on both sides: cost equals cost, distance equals distance, or total equals total. If the units on the two sides differ, the setup is wrong.

## Solving and Checking

Solve the equation with the usual steps, then answer the question that was asked rather than the equation that was written. A problem may ask for a total cost, a break-even time or a number of items, and those are different substitutions. Check the answer in the original story, not only in the equation, and include units. If the answer is a fraction of an item or a negative time, reconsider whether the model applies to that range.
