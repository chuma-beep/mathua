# Study design

Study design determines what a data set can support. A well-designed experiment can support a causal claim. An observational study usually cannot.

## Experimental units and groups

The experimental units are the people, plants or objects that receive a treatment. A treatment group receives one condition. A control group receives a baseline or placebo condition.

If an experiment has $g$ groups with $n$ units in each, the total number of units is $gn$. Random assignment decides which unit enters which group.

Randomisation protects against systematic differences between the groups. Without it, a difference in the response may reflect a difference that existed before the treatment.

## Randomized blocks

A randomized block design first divides units into blocks that are similar. Each block then receives every treatment in random order.

With $t$ treatments and $b$ blocks, there are $tb$ treatment-block cells. Blocking reduces variability caused by the blocking variable, so the treatment comparison becomes more precise.

A matched-pairs design is the special case with two treatments and one pair per block.

## Proportional allocation

In a stratified sample, the population is divided into strata. Proportional allocation gives each stratum a share of the sample equal to its share of the population.

If one stratum is one quarter of the population and the sample has 80 units, that stratum receives $80/4=20$ units.

Report the design, the randomisation and the sample size. A number without its design is hard to interpret.
