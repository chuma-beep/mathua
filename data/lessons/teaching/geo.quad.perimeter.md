# Perimeter of Polygons

The **perimeter** of a polygon is the distance around its outside edge. "Perimeter" literally means "measure around." To find it, add the lengths of all the sides.

\[P = a + b + c + \cdots\]

## Perimeter Formulas

For special polygons the addition can be shortened with multiplication:

- Triangle: $P = a + b + c$
- Rectangle: $P = 2l + 2w$ (two lengths and two widths)
- Square: $P = 4s$
- Regular polygon ($n$ equal sides): $P = n \cdot s$

## Example

**Example**

Find the perimeter of a rectangle with length $6$ cm and width $4$ cm.

*Solution*

\[P = 2(6) + 2(4) = 12 + 8 = 20\]

The perimeter is $20$ cm.

**Example**

A regular pentagon has sides of length $7$ m. Find its perimeter.

*Solution*

A pentagon has $5$ equal sides:

\[P = 5(7) = 35\]

The perimeter is $35$ m.

## Units

Perimeter is a length, so it is measured in plain units: cm, m, inches — never square units.

## Summary

- Perimeter = add up all the side lengths.
- Shortcuts: rectangle $2l + 2w$, square $4s$, regular polygon $n \cdot s$.
- Perimeter units are the same units as the sides (not squared).
