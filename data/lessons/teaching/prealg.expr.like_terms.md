# Combining Like Terms

Algebraic expressions can be large and complicated, so anything we can do to rewrite an expression in a simpler way is helpful. One of the fundamental skills we have for simplifying expressions is *combining like terms*.

## Identifying Terms

> **Definition**
> In an algebraic expression, the *terms* are quantities being added together.

Note that terms are not the same thing as *factors*, which are quantities being multiplied together. Factors are studied more later in this book.

**Example**
List the terms in the expression $2\ell+2w$.

*Solution*
The expression has two terms that are being added, $2\ell$ and $2w$.

When an expression has subtraction, we can rewrite the it using addition of a negative to make it easier to see exactly what the terms are.

**Example**
List the terms in the expression $-3x^2+5x-4$.

*Solution*
We can rewrite this expression as $-3x^2+5x+(-4)$ to see that the terms are $-3x^2$, $5x$, and $-4$. Note that the third term is $-4$, not just $4$.

**Example**
List the terms in the expression $3\,\text{cm}+2\,\text{cm}-3\,\text{cm}+2\,\text{cm}$.

*Solution*
This expression has four terms: $3\,\text{cm}$, $2\,\text{cm}$, $-3\,\text{cm}$, and $2\,\text{cm}$.

## Combining Like Terms

If you have $3\,\text{cm}+2\,\text{cm}$, it is natural to add those together to get $5\,\text{cm}$. That works because their units ( $\text{cm}$) are the same. The same idea applies to other terms, even ones that don't have units. For example, with $2x+3x$, we have $2$ *things* and then $3$ more of those *things*. All together, we have $5$ of those things. So $2x+3x$ is the same as $5x$.

Terms in an algebraic expression that can be combined by adding them together into one new term are called *like terms*. 
1. Sometimes like terms use a common variable, like how $2x+3x$ has terms that each use $x$. This simplifies to $5x$.
2. Sometimes like terms use the same units, like how $3\,\text{cm}+2\,\text{cm}$ has terms that each use $\text{cm}$. This simplifies to $5\,\text{cm}$.
3. Sometimes like terms have something else in common, like how $3\sqrt{7}+2\sqrt{7}$ has terms that each use $\sqrt{7}$. This simplifies to $5\sqrt{7}$.

**Example**
In the expressions below, look for like terms and then simplify where possible by adding or subtracting. 
1. $5\,\text{in}+20\,\text{in}$
2. $16\,\text{ft}^2+4\,\text{ft}$
3. $2\,\apple+5\,\apple$
4. $5\,\text{min}+12\,\text{ft}$
5. $5\,\dog-2\,\cat$
6. $20\,\text{m}-6\,\text{m}$

*Solution*
We can combine terms with the same units, but we cannot combine terms with distinct units such as minutes and feet. 
1. $5\,\text{in}+20\,\text{in}=25\,\text{in}$
2. $16\,\text{ft}^2+4\,\text{ft}$ cannot be combined
3. $2\,\apple+5\,\apple=7\,\apple$
4. $5\,\text{min}+12\,\text{ft}$ cannot be combined
5. $5\,\dog-2\,\cat$ cannot be combined
6. $20\,\text{m}-6\,\text{m}=14\,\text{m}$

One of the examples from was $16\,\text{ft}^2+4\,\text{ft}$. The units on these two terms look similar, but they are different. $16\,\text{ft}^{2}$ is a measurement of how much area something has. $4\,\text{ft}$ is a measurement of how long something is. illustrates this.

**Example**
Simplify each expression by combining like terms (if possible). 
1. $\frac{5}{7}x-\frac{2}{7}x+\frac{3}{7}y$
2. $\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{2}x^2$
3. $-3.01r+1.2s-5t$
4. $\frac{2}{3}y+\frac{5}{6}y$
5. $\frac{10}{3}t-\frac{1}{2}x-t$
6. $x-0.15x$

*Solution*
1. This expression has two like terms, $\frac{5}{7}x$ and $-\frac{2}{7}x$, which we can combine. We have to subtract $\frac{5}{7}-\frac{2}{7}$, which is straightforward since they have the same denominator. $\highlight{\frac{5}{7}x-\frac{2}{7}x}+\frac{3}{7}y =\highlight{\frac{3}{7}x}+\frac{3}{7}y$ We don't combine $\frac{3}{7}x$ and $\frac{3}{7}y$ because $x$ and $y$ are different variables.
2. This expression cannot be simplified because the variable parts are not the same. We cannot add $x$-terms with $x^2$-terms just like we cannot add feet (a measure of length) with square feet (a measure of area).
3. There aren't any like terms.
4. The two terms are like terms. To combine them, we need to add $\frac{2}{3}+\frac{5}{6}$. For a review of adding fractions with different denominators, see. In this case, $\begin{aligned} \frac{2}{3}+\frac{5}{6}&=\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{2}+\frac{5}{6} \\ &=\frac{4}{6}+\frac{5}{6} \\ &=\frac{9}{6}=\frac{3}{2} \end{aligned}$ So $\frac{2}{3}y+\frac{5}{6}y=\frac{3}{2}y$.
5. There are two like terms: $\frac{10}{3}t$ and $-t$. A lonely $t$ is usually just written as $t$, but we can *think* of it as $1t$. So we are combining $\frac{10}{3}t$ and $-t$ and we need to subtract $\frac{10}{3}-1$. $\begin{aligned} \frac{10}{3}-1&=\frac{10}{3}-\frac{3}{3} \\ &=\frac{7}{3} \end{aligned}$ Our two terms combine to make $\frac{7}{3}t$. There was another term when this started and the final simplified expression is $\frac{7}{3}t-\frac{1}{2}x$.
6. This expression can be thought of as $1.00x-0.15x$. Subtracting decimals $1.00-0.15$, the result is $0.85$. So we have $0.85x$.

$x+0.25x$

*Solution*
Think of this expression as $1.00x+0.25x$ and simplify to get $1.25x$.

$\frac{4}{9}x-\frac{7}{10}y+\frac{2}{3}x$

*Solution*
This expression has two like terms that can be combined: $\frac{4}{9}x$ and $\frac{2}{3}x$. To combine them, we need to add the fractions $\frac{4}{9}+\frac{2}{3}$. $\begin{aligned} \frac{4}{9}+\frac{2}{3}&=\frac{4}{9}+\frac{6}{9} \\ &=\frac{10}{9} \end{aligned}$ So $\frac{4}{9}x+\frac{2}{3}x=\frac{10}{9}x$. And together with the third term, the answer is $\frac{10}{9}x-\frac{7}{10}y$.

$\frac{5}{6}y-\frac{8}{15}y+\frac{2}{3}y^2$

*Solution*
In this expression we can combine the $y$-terms. We need to subtract the fractions $\frac{5}{6}-\frac{8}{15}$. $\begin{aligned} \frac{5}{6}-\frac{8}{15}&=\frac{25}{30}-\frac{16}{30} \\ &=\frac{9}{30} \\ &=\frac{3}{10} \end{aligned}$ So $\frac{5}{6}y-\frac{8}{15}y=\frac{3}{10}y$. And together with the third term, the answer is $\frac{3}{10}y+\frac{2}{3}y^2$.

$4x+1.5y-9z$

*Solution*
This expression cannot be simplified further because there are not any like terms.

## Applications

The *perimeter* of a shape is the length of a strip of tape that could be taped tightly around the shape. When a shape has straight side edges, the perimeter comes from adding all of the side lengths together. This can lead to like terms that can be combined.

**Example**
Find the perimeter of this shape, which is not drawn to scale. Simplify the perimeter expression as much as possible. a five-sided shape whose sides are labeled 2x, 3y, 1.5x, 5y, and x \begin{tikzpicture} \draw[fill=wood] (0,0) -- node[pos=0.5,below]{$2x$} (3,0) -- node[pos=0.5,below right]{$3y$} (4,2) -- node[pos=0.5,above right]{$1.5x$} (2,3) -- node[pos=0.5,above left]{$5y$} (-1,1) -- node[pos=0.5,below left]{$x$} cycle; \end{tikzpicture}

*Solution*
The perimeter is the result from adding the five sides together: $2x+3y+1.5x+5y+x$. There are three $x$-terms that sum to $4.5x$, and two $y$-terms that sum to $8y$. So the perimeter is $4.5x+8y$.

Sometimes it makes sense to add two algebra expressions together, and that may give an opportunity to combine like terms.

**Example**
A chemist has a bottle with $1.2\,\text{L}$ of water mixed with $0.3\,\text{L}$ of gasoline. At this time, they have forgotten the density of water (in $\text{g}/$) and the density of gasoline (also in $\text{g}/$) so they use $w$ and $g$ as variables for these densities. This means $1.2w+0.3g$ is the total mass of this mixture, in grams. There is a second bottle, with $0.9\,\text{L}$ of water mixed with $0.5\,\text{L}$ of gasoline. The chemist pours it all together. What is the mass of the combined mixture?

*Solution*
The first mass is $1.2w+0.3g$ grams, and the second mass is $0.9w+0.5g$ grams. So together, the mass in grams is: 
\[ \begin{aligned} (1.2w+0.3g)+(0.9w+0.5g)&=\firsthighlight{1.2w}+\secondhighlight{0.3g}+\firsthighlight{0.9w}+\secondhighlight{0.5g} \\ &=2.1w+0.8g \end{aligned} \]
