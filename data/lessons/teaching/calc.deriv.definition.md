# The derivative as a limit

This lesson develops the derivative as a limit through three connected ideas. Work through the sections in order and check each example before moving on.

## The limit of a difference quotient

The derivative at a point is the limit of the difference quotient as the step size shrinks. The quotient is the slope of a secant line, and the limit is the slope of the tangent line.

Write the definition with the point fixed and the step size as the variable. The numerator is a change in output and the denominator is a change in input. The limit must exist and be finite.

The definition is not only a formula to memorise. It explains why differentiability is a local property and why a sharp corner has no derivative.

## Evaluating the limit algebraically

For a polynomial, expand the numerator and cancel the step size. The cancelled factor is the source of the zero in the denominator before the limit is taken.

After cancellation, substitute zero for the step size. The remaining expression is the derivative. Checking the result against a derivative rule is a useful verification.

For a rational function, combine fractions before cancelling. For a radical, multiply by the conjugate. The algebra is different but the limiting step is the same.

## Interpreting the derivative

The derivative measures the instantaneous rate of change of the output with respect to the input. Its units are output units per input unit.

At a point where the derivative is positive the function is increasing. Where it is negative the function is decreasing. Where it is zero the tangent is horizontal and the point is a candidate extremum.

The definition also gives a linear approximation. Near the point, the function is close to its tangent line, with an error that shrinks faster than the step size.
