# Van Kampen's Theorem

The fundamental group of a union can be assembled from the pieces, provided the
overlap is nice. Van Kampen's theorem says: if \(X = U \cup V\) with \(U, V\)
open and path-connected and \(U \cap V\) path-connected, then \(\pi_1(X)\) is
generated by \(\pi_1(U)\) and \(\pi_1(V)\) together, with one relation forcing
each loop of the intersection to mean the same thing on both sides.

## Statement and Setup

Fix a basepoint \(x_0 \in U \cap V\). The inclusions induce homomorphisms
\(\pi_1(U) \to \pi_1(X)\) and \(\pi_1(V) \to \pi_1(X)\). Van Kampen: every loop
in \(X\) factors (up to homotopy) into pieces alternating between \(U\) and
\(V\), so these two maps jointly surject onto \(\pi_1(X)\). The kernel is
generated by elements \(i_U(g) \, i_V(g)^{-1}\) for
\(g \in \pi_1(U \cap V)\): a loop living in the overlap must read the same
whether viewed from \(U\) or from \(V\).

Path-connectedness of the intersection is load-bearing. Without it the pieces
can join in ways no relation controls, and the conclusion fails.

## Wedge of Circles

Let \(X = S^1 \vee S^1\) be the figure eight: two circles joined at a point.
Thicken each circle slightly to open sets \(U, V\) so that each deformation
retracts onto one circle and \(U \cap V\) is contractible. Then
\(\pi_1(U) \cong \mathbb{Z} = \langle a \rangle\),
\(\pi_1(V) \cong \mathbb{Z} = \langle b \rangle\), and the intersection
contributes no relations. Van Kampen gives
\(\pi_1(X) \cong \langle a, b \rangle\), the **free group on two generators**:
words in \(a^{\pm 1}, b^{\pm 1}\) with no relations, where \(ab \neq ba\).

## Computing Fundamental Groups

The torus \(T = S^1 \times S^1\) is a square with opposite edges glued. Removing
one interior point leaves a wedge of two circles, with \(\pi_1\) free on the
edge-loops \(a, b\). Filling the point back in kills exactly the commutator
loop \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\) that runs around the missing point. Van Kampen yields
\(\pi_1(T) \cong \langle a, b \mid aba^{-1}b^{-1} = 1 \rangle \cong
\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}\).

The pattern generalizes: a cell complex with one vertex has \(\pi_1\) free on
its edges modulo one relation per attached 2-cell. This is how presentations
of fundamental groups are read off a space.
