# Eccentricity of a Conic

## A measure of shape

Eccentricity describes how far a conic departs from a circle.
A circle has eccentricity zero.
The value is the same for every point on the curve.

Eccentricity compares the distance to the focus with the distance to the directrix. A small value keeps the curve close to circular, while a value near one stretches it into a long ellipse.

## Ellipses and hyperbolas

For an ellipse e = c / a with c^2 = a^2 - b^2.
For a hyperbola e = c / a with c^2 = a^2 + b^2.
An ellipse has e between zero and one, and a hyperbola has e above one.

For an ellipse the foci lie inside the curve, so c is smaller than a. For a hyperbola the foci lie outside the branches, so c is larger than a. The two formulas for c differ by a single sign.

## The parabola case

A parabola has eccentricity exactly one.
Every point on a parabola is equally far from the focus and the directrix.
The eccentricity therefore distinguishes all three conic types.

The parabola is the boundary case because its focus and directrix are equally distant at every point. That equality is exactly the statement e = 1. Classifying a conic by eccentricity therefore rests on one definition rather than on three separate rules.
