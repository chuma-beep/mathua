# Sketching Polynomial Graphs

## End behavior

The degree controls what happens far to the left and far to the right.
An even degree sends both ends the same way.
An odd degree sends the two ends in opposite directions.

The leading term dominates once |x| is large, so the other terms cannot change the eventual direction. That is why a sketch starts from the two ends and then fills in the middle.

## Zeros and multiplicity

A zero of odd multiplicity crosses the axis.
A zero of even multiplicity touches the axis and turns back.
The multiplicity also controls how flat the graph looks at that zero.

At a zero of multiplicity one the graph passes straight through. At multiplicity two it turns, and at multiplicity three it passes through while flattening against the axis. These local shapes are the second thing to mark.

## Turning points

A polynomial of degree n has at most n - 1 turning points.
The graph can have fewer turning points than the maximum.
Combine end behavior, zeros and turning points into one sketch.

A degree n polynomial has at most n real zeros counted with multiplicity and at most n - 1 turning points. A sketch that respects those limits is consistent even when the exact heights are unknown.
