# Mixed Numbers in Word Problems

Mixed-number word problems ask about whole-plus-fraction quantities: "how
much fabric for 3 curtains needing \(2\frac{1}{2}\) yards each?" The reading
is always the same — a mixed number is a whole plus a fraction — so the real
work is converting: improper fractions multiply and divide, mixed numbers
add and subtract, and the final answer usually goes back to mixed form.

## Word Problems

Three curtains need \(2\frac{1}{2}\) yards each. Convert first:
\(2\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}\), then \(3 \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{15}{2} =
7\frac{1}{2}\) yards. Converting before multiplying keeps the whole part
from being multiplied twice.

A \(9\frac{3}{4}\) liter tank drains in equal \(\frac{1}{2}\) liter portions:
\(9\frac{3}{4} = \frac{39}{4}\), then \(\frac{39}{4} \div \frac{1}{2} =
\frac{39}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{1} = \frac{78}{4} = 19\frac{1}{2}\) portions.
Division runs on the improper form; the mixed answer reads naturally.

## Strategy

Read the problem twice: first for the story (whole quantities with fractional
parts), second for the operation. For \(\times\) and \(\div\), convert every
mixed number to an improper fraction first. For \(+\) and \(-\), work with
mixed form directly. Always convert the final answer back to the form the
problem asked in. Finish by asking whether the answer makes sense: mixed
problems should land near a whole-number estimate (about 3 × \(2\frac{1}{2}\)
≈ 8 yards, and \(7\frac{1}{2}\) is close).

## Practice

Paint covers \(10\frac{1}{2}\) square meters per liter and you have
\(2\frac{1}{4}\) liters: \(10\frac{1}{2} = \frac{21}{2}\) and
\(2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}\), so \(\frac{21}{2} \cdot \frac{9}{4} =
\frac{189}{8} = 23\frac{5}{8}\) square meters. Check: roughly \(10 \times 2 =
20\), and \(23\frac{5}{8}\) is close — the multiplication checks out.
