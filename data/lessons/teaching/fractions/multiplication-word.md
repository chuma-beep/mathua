# Multiplying Fractions in Word Problems

Multiplication word problems with fractions almost always hide the word *of*:
"\(\frac{1}{2}\) of the garden", "a recipe scaled by \(\frac{3}{4}\)". When a
problem asks for a fraction *of* a quantity, multiply. The arithmetic is
always the same — multiply numerators, multiply denominators — so the real
work is spotting the word *of* and deciding what it applies to.

## Word Problems

A garden is \(\frac{3}{4}\) of an acre and \(\frac{2}{3}\) of it is planted
with tomatoes. Planted area: \(\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4} =
\frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}\) acre. The word *of* signals multiplication, and
the answer simplifies by dividing top and bottom by 6.

A recipe calls for \(\frac{1}{2}\) cup of flour and you triple it:
\(3 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1\frac{1}{2}\)
cups. A whole number is a fraction over 1, so it multiplies the same way.

## Strategy

Read the problem twice: first for the story (what fraction of what), second
for the numbers. Translate *of* into \(\cdot\), multiply straight across, and
simplify before or after. With mixed numbers, convert to improper fractions
first — never multiply the whole parts and fraction parts separately. Finish
by asking whether the answer makes sense: a fraction *of* something proper
must be smaller than what you started with.

## Practice

A tank holds \(\frac{5}{6}\) of its capacity and \(\frac{4}{5}\) of that water
is fresh: \(\frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{6} = \frac{20}{30} = \frac{2}{3}\) of
capacity is fresh water. Check: \(\frac{2}{3}\) is less than
\(\frac{5}{6}\), and each factor is proper, so the product had to shrink —
the multiplication checks out.
