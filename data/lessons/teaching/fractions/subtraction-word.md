# Subtracting Fractions in Word Problems

Subtraction word problems with fractions ask how much is left, how much more
one quantity has, or what the difference is. The arithmetic is always the
same — common denominator, subtract numerators — so the real work is reading:
identify the whole, the part removed, and whether the fractions share a
denominator already.

## Word Problems

Maya has \(\frac{7}{8}\) of a liter of paint and uses \(\frac{3}{8}\) for one
wall. Remaining: \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\) liter.
Same denominators subtract directly; the answer simplifies by dividing top and
bottom by 4.

With different denominators, convert first. Leo runs \(\frac{5}{6}\) km and
walks \(\frac{1}{4}\) km back; the net distance is
\(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{10}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}\) km.
The common denominator 12 is the least common multiple of 6 and 4.

## Strategy

Read the problem twice: first for the story (who loses what), second for the
numbers and their denominators. If denominators match, subtract numerators
and simplify. If not, raise both fractions to the least common denominator
before subtracting — never subtract denominators. Finish by asking whether
the answer makes sense: a remainder must be smaller than what you started
with, and a difference must fit between the two quantities.

## Practice

A recipe needs \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup of sugar and you have already added
\(\frac{1}{3}\) cup: \(\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{9}{12} - \frac{4}{12} = \frac{5}{12}\)
cup still to add. Check: \(\frac{5}{12}\) is less than
\(\frac{3}{4}\), and \(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{5}{12} = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}\)
recovers the recipe, so the subtraction checks out.
