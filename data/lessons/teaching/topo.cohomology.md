# Cohomology

**Cohomology:** The dual theory to homology: $H^k(X)$ counts $k$-dimensional holes with a ring structure from the cup product. De Rham cohomology computes it by differential forms on smooth manifolds; $H^0$ counts connected components.

## Worked: the torus

Compute the integral cohomology of $T^2$:

1. $H^0=\mathbb{Z}$: the torus is connected, one component.
2. $H^1=\mathbb{Z}^2$: two independent 1-cycles, one per circle factor.
3. $H^2=\mathbb{Z}$: one fundamental class, generated by the cup product $a\smile b$ of the two $H^1$ generators.

So the ring structure matters: $H^*(T^2)$ is generated by $a,b$ in degree 1 with $a\smile b$ generating $H^2$.

## Worked: de Rham on the circle

Compute $H^1_{dR}(S^1)$ with forms:

1. Closed 1-forms on $S^1$ are constant multiples $c\,dt$ of the angle form.
2. Exact forms integrate to 0 around the circle, so $c\,dt$ is exact only for $c=0$.
3. Hence each real $c$ gives a class: $H^1_{dR}(S^1)$ is one-dimensional over $\mathbb{R}$.

So closed modulo exact leaves one class per hole: integration pairs forms with cycles.

## Worked: homotopy invariance

Compare cohomology of $\mathbb{R}^2$ minus the origin with $S^1$:

1. The punctured plane deformation-retracts onto the unit circle.
2. Homotopic maps induce equal maps on cohomology, so the groups agree.
3. Thus $H^1$ of the punctured plane is $\mathbb{Z}$, read off from the circle.

So singular cohomology is a homotopy invariant, and Poincare duality $H^k\cong H_{n-k}$ extends the picture to compact orientable $n$-manifolds.
