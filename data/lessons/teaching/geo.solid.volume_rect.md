# Volume of a Rectangular Prism

A rectangular prism is a box shape: six rectangles meeting at right angles. Its **volume** — the number of unit cubes that fill it — comes from multiplying its three edge lengths:

\[V = l \cdot w \cdot h\]

where $l$, $w$, and $h$ are the length, width, and height.

## Why Multiply?

A box $5$ inches long, $4$ inches wide, and $3$ inches high holds a bottom layer of $5 \cdot 4 = 20$ one-inch cubes. The box is $3$ layers high, so:

\[V = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 60 \text{ in}^3\]

Volume answers are always in **cubic units**, because the unit is a cube: $\text{cm}^3$, $\text{in}^3$, $\text{ft}^3$, and so on.

## The Base-Times-Height View

The same formula reads as "area of the base times the height":

\[V = B h\]

where $B = lw$ is the base area. This viewpoint carries over to cylinders, which have circular bases.

## Example

**Example**

Find the volume of a rectangular prism measuring $5$ cm by $4$ cm by $3$ cm.

*Solution*

\[V = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 60\]

The volume is $60\text{ cm}^3$.

**Example**

A storage box has a square base with sides of $2$ ft and stands $5$ ft tall. How many cubic feet does it hold?

*Solution*

Base area: $B = 2 \cdot 2 = 4\text{ ft}^2$.

\[V = Bh = 4 \cdot 5 = 20\]

The volume is $20\text{ ft}^3$.

## Summary

- Rectangular prism volume: $V = lwh = Bh$.
- Think "squares in the floor, stacked so many layers high."
- Volume units are cubic units.
