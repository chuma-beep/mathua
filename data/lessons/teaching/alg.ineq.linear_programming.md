# Linear Programming in Two Variables

## Feasible Regions

A linear programming problem asks for the largest or smallest value of a linear objective subject to linear inequalities. Each inequality describes a half-plane, and the solution set of the whole system is their intersection, called the feasible region. Drawing the boundary lines and testing one point per region is enough to shade the correct side. The feasible region is usually a polygon, and it may be unbounded, which matters when the objective has no maximum.

## Corner Points

The objective function is a linear expression such as P = 3x + 2y. Its level curves are parallel lines, and moving them in the direction of increase or decrease until they leave the feasible region shows where the optimum occurs. Algebraically, the optimum of a linear objective over a polygon occurs at a corner point, so the reliable method is to list every corner and evaluate the objective there. A corner is found by solving the two boundary equations that meet at it.

## Optimising

After evaluating, compare the values and state the answer with the point that produces it. If two corners tie, every point on the edge between them gives the same optimum. If the region is unbounded, check whether the objective grows without limit in the open direction. Writing the constraints, the corners and the objective values in a table makes the comparison auditable and catches arithmetic errors before the final answer is given.
