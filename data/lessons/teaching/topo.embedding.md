# Embedding Theorems

**Embedding:** An injective immersion that is a homeomorphism onto its image. Whitney embeds every $n$-manifold in $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$; Nash embeds Riemannian manifolds isometrically in some $\mathbb{R}^N$.

## Worked: Whitney's target dimension

Place a closed $n$-manifold by Whitney's theorem:

1. Map the manifold generically into $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$; self-intersections are isolated double points.
2. Push apart the finitely many double points using the extra room (dimension count $2n$ beats $n+n$).
3. The result is injective with matching subspace topology: an embedding.

So $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ always has room: Whitney's number $2n$ resolves all generic intersections.

## Worked: the circle in the plane

Embed $S^1$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ as the unit circle:

1. Send angle $t$ to $(\cos t, \sin t)$; distinct angles give distinct points.
2. The map is an immersion with nonvanishing derivative everywhere.
3. The circle topology matches the subspace topology from the plane.

So $S^1$ embeds in $\mathbb{R}^2$, and likewise $S^2$ embeds in $\mathbb{R}^3$ as the unit sphere.

## Worked: wild versus tame

Contrast two embeddings of $S^2$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$:

1. The standard sphere is tame: both complementary regions are balls (Schoenflies).
2. The Alexander horned sphere interlocks its horns so one complementary region is not simply connected.
3. Hence the horned sphere is wild: same abstract sphere, pathologically embedded.

So embeddings remember placement, not just the source: tame versus wild is about the complement.
