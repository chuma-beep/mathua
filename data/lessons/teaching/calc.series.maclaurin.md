# Maclaurin series

## Taylor series centred at zero



A Maclaurin series is a Taylor series with centre zero.

Write the coefficients as derivatives at zero divided by factorials.

The radius of convergence is inherited from the Taylor expansion.

A Maclaurin series is useful because every coefficient is read from the behaviour of the function at a single point. When the derivatives at zero are simple, the expansion can be written down directly.

## Standard Maclaurin series



The exponential function expands with reciprocal factorial coefficients.

Sine and cosine alternate signs and use odd or even powers.

The geometric series is the simplest Maclaurin series.

These standard expansions are worth memorising because they are the building blocks for more complicated functions. Substitution and algebraic manipulation extend them without recomputing derivatives.

## Building new series



Substitute a multiple of x into a known series.

Integrate or differentiate a series term by term inside its radius.

Check the interval of convergence after the operation.

After changing a series, state the new radius and check the endpoints separately. A series that is valid inside an interval may fail at one or both ends.
