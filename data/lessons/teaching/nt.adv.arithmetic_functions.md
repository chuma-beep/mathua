# Arithmetic Functions and Dirichlet Convolution

## Divisor Functions

An arithmetic function assigns a number to each positive integer. The divisor function d(n) counts divisors, the sum-of-divisors function sigma(n) adds them, and Euler's totient phi(n) counts the integers up to n that are coprime to n. These functions are the vocabulary in which much of elementary number theory is written.

## Dirichlet Convolution

The Dirichlet convolution of two arithmetic functions is defined by summing over pairs whose product is n. Convolution turns the pointwise product of Dirichlet series into a new function and makes the divisor sum the convolution of the constant function one with the identity. The Mobius function is the inverse of the constant function under this operation.

## Multiplicative Functions

Many arithmetic functions are multiplicative, meaning that their value on a product of coprime arguments is the product of their values. A multiplicative function is therefore determined by its values on prime powers. This reduces a computation on a large integer to a small table indexed by the primes that divide it.
