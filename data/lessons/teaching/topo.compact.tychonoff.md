# Tychonoff's theorem

## Arbitrary Products

Tychonoff's theorem states that an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact in the product topology. The product may be infinite, even uncountable. This is one of the most used theorems in general topology.

The finite case follows from the tube lemma and is elementary. The infinite case needs a choice principle, and in fact the theorem is equivalent to the axiom of choice over the usual axioms of set theory.

The product topology is essential. The box topology is finer and the theorem fails for it. An infinite product of compact spaces can fail to be compact in the box topology, so the choice of topology is part of the statement.

## The Finite Intersection Property

A space is compact exactly when every family of closed sets with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection. Tychonoff's theorem is usually proved by applying this criterion to a family of closed sets in the product.

The proof extends the family to an ultrafilter or a maximal family with the finite intersection property. Each coordinate projection then has a cluster point by compactness of the factor, and the tuple of cluster points lies in every set of the family.

The finite intersection property formulation is often the most convenient one in applications. It turns compactness into a statement about consistency of finite subsystems, which is why compactness appears in model theory and in the theory of profinite groups.

## Consequences

Tychonoff's theorem implies that the Hilbert cube is compact and that arbitrary products of closed intervals are compact. It gives compactness of profinite groups and of Stone spaces, which are central in algebra and logic.

It also implies the Banach-Alaoglu theorem in functional analysis, after translating weak-star compactness into compactness of a product of disks. That application shows how a point-set theorem can carry real analytic weight.

The theorem is not constructive. It gives existence of cluster points without producing them. In concrete problems one usually combines it with a description of the compact set as a product of simpler compact sets.
