## Definition of cyclic groups

A group $G$ is cyclic if there exists an element $g \in G$ such that every element of $G$ can be written as a power of $g$ (written additively as multiples of $g$). The element $g$ is called a generator.

### Example

$Z_n$ under addition is cyclic, generated by $1$. The Klein four-group is not cyclic.

## Generators and order

The order of a cyclic group equals the order of its generator. If $G = \langle g \rangle$ and $g$ has order $n$, then $|G| = n$.

### Example

In $Z_6$, the element $1$ has order $6$, so $Z_6$ is cyclic of order $6$.

## Examples of cyclic and non-cyclic groups

Many familiar groups are cyclic, but direct products and symmetric groups often are not.
