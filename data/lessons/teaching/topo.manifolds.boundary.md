# Manifolds with boundary

## Charts with Half-Space

A manifold with boundary is locally homeomorphic either to Euclidean space or to a half-space. Points of the second type form the boundary. The definition is local, so a single space can have interior points and boundary points at once.

The closed interval is the simplest example. Its interior is an open interval, and its boundary is the two endpoints. The closed disk is a two-dimensional manifold with boundary, and its boundary is the circle.

The boundary of an n-dimensional manifold with boundary is an (n-1)-dimensional manifold without boundary. This dimension drop is the key structural fact and is used constantly in Stokes' theorem and in cobordism theory.

## The Boundary

The boundary is a topological invariant of the manifold with boundary. A point cannot be both interior and boundary, because the half-space and Euclidean space are not homeomorphic. That statement is a nontrivial theorem, not a definition.

Boundary components can be empty. A manifold without boundary is a manifold with boundary whose boundary is empty, so the terminology is inclusive. Spheres, tori and projective spaces are examples.

The boundary operation is compatible with products. The boundary of a product is the union of the product of one boundary with the other factor and the product of the other boundary with the first factor, with overlaps counted once.

## Products and Examples

The product of two manifolds with boundary is a manifold with boundary. The closed square is the product of two closed intervals, and its boundary is the four sides. The closed cube is the product of three intervals.

The n-dimensional cube has 2n boundary faces. The n-dimensional ball has boundary the (n-1)-sphere. These two families supply most of the concrete examples in a first course.

Orientation behaves well for manifolds with boundary. An orientation of the manifold induces an orientation of the boundary, and the induced boundary orientation is used in the statement of Stokes' theorem. Reversing the orientation of the manifold reverses the boundary orientation.
