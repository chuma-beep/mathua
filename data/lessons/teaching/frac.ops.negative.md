# Negative Fractions

## Where the Sign Lives

A negative fraction carries its sign in the numerator, in the denominator or in front of the whole fraction. The three forms describe the same value. Writing the sign in front is usually clearest.

The rules for negative numbers apply unchanged. Adding a negative fraction is subtraction, and multiplying two negative fractions gives a positive result. Only the sign changes, not the arithmetic on the magnitudes.

## Adding and Subtracting

To add fractions with opposite signs, find a common denominator and combine the numerators with their signs. The result takes the sign of the larger magnitude.

For minus three quarters plus five sixths, use twelfths. The terms become minus nine twelfths and ten twelfths, whose sum is one twelfth. The positive term is larger, so the answer is positive.

## Multiplying and Dividing

Multiply the numerators and denominators as usual, then decide the sign. An odd number of negative factors gives a negative product. An even number gives a positive product.

Division follows the same rule after replacing the divisor by its reciprocal. The reciprocal of a negative fraction is negative, so the sign count includes it.
