# Graphs of Proportional Relationships

## A Line Through the Origin

Two quantities are proportional when their graph is a straight line through the origin. The constant of proportionality is the slope.

A point such as (4, 10) means that four units of the first quantity correspond to ten of the second, so the constant is 10/4 = 2.5.

A line that does not pass through the origin describes a relationship with an initial amount, not a proportional one.

## Reading Points

Each point on the graph is a pair of corresponding values. To predict a value, locate the known coordinate and read the other from the line.

The unit rate is the y-coordinate when x equals one. It can be read directly from the graph or computed from any point.

Tables, equations and graphs describe the same relationship. Convert between them to check that the constant agrees.

## Comparing Relationships

Two proportional graphs can be compared by their slopes. The steeper line has the larger constant of proportionality.

If one graph passes through (1, 3) and another through (1, 5), the second represents a faster rate.

A graph that starts at a positive y-intercept is not proportional, even when it is a straight line.
