# Cardinality: How Many?

**Cardinality** means "how many" — the size of a group of objects. When you count a group, the last number you say is the cardinality.

**Example 1:** 🍎 🍎 🍎

Counting: "One, two, three"
Cardinality: 3 (there are three apples)

**Example 2:** 🐕 🐕 🐕 🐕 🐕

Counting: "One, two, three, four, five"
Cardinality: 5 (there are five dogs)

The cardinality does not depend on how the objects are arranged. A group of 5 objects has cardinality 5 whether they are in a line, in a pile, or scattered.

**Example 3:** Which group has a larger cardinality?
- Group A: ● ● ● ● ● ● ● (7 dots)
- Group B: ● ● ● ● ● (5 dots)

Group A has cardinality 7, which is larger than 5. So Group A has more dots.

Cardinality is one of the most important ideas in early mathematics — it connects counting to the concept of "how many" that we use in everyday life.
