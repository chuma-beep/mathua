# Frequency and Period

## Period from the Coefficient

For y = sin(bx), the period is 360/b degrees, or 2 pi/b radians. A larger b compresses the graph horizontally.

For y = cos(bx), the same rule applies. The coefficient b does not change the amplitude.

The period is always positive, so use the absolute value of b.

## Frequency

Frequency is the number of cycles in one unit of the horizontal variable. It is the reciprocal of the period.

A period of 180 degrees corresponds to two cycles in 360 degrees, so the frequency is two per full turn.

In applications, frequency is often measured in hertz, which counts cycles per second.

## Comparing Graphs

Two sine graphs with different b values have different periods but the same shape and amplitude.

The graph with the larger coefficient completes more cycles in the same window. Count the cycles to estimate the ratio of the coefficients.

A phase shift changes where a cycle starts but not how long it lasts.


Period and frequency describe the same feature from opposite directions, so a question may give either one. Convert first, then compare graphs by counting cycles in a fixed window rather than by comparing coefficients directly.
