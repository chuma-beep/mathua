# Borrowing with Mixed Numbers

## When the Fraction Is Too Small

Subtracting mixed numbers can fail in the fractional part. If the fraction being subtracted is larger than the fraction above it, one whole must be borrowed and converted into a fraction.

Borrowing one whole from the whole-number part adds a fraction equal to one. For five and one quarter, borrowing gives four and five quarters. The total value is unchanged, but the fractional part is now large enough.

## Borrowing Across a Whole Number

The borrowed whole is written with the same denominator as the fraction beside it. Borrowing from seven and two thirds gives six and five thirds, because one whole is three thirds and two plus three is five.

After the borrow, subtract the fractions and then the whole numbers. Reduce the result if the fractional parts share a factor. The answer should be checked by adding it to the subtrahend.

## Borrowing From an Integer

Subtracting a mixed number from a whole number borrows from the integer first. Six minus two and three quarters becomes five and four quarters minus two and three quarters.

The difference is three and one quarter. Converting both mixed numbers to improper fractions is an alternative that avoids borrowing, and it gives the same result.
