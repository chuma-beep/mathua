# nth Roots and Radical Notation

## The Meaning of a Root

The nth root of a number a is a value whose nth power is a. In symbols, the nth root of a is written with a radical sign and an index n. When the index is two it is usually omitted and called the square root. The number under the sign is the radicand. Evaluating a root means finding the base that produces the radicand when raised to the index, so the cube root of 27 is 3 because 3^3 = 27.

## Roots and Exponents

Radicals and rational exponents are two notations for the same operation. The nth root of a is a^(1/n), and the nth root of a^m is a^(m/n). The denominator of the exponent is the index and the numerator is the power. This conversion is useful because exponent rules then apply: multiplying powers with the same base adds exponents, and raising a power to a power multiplies them. Simplifying a radical often means pulling out perfect nth powers.

## Domain and Sign

An even root of a negative number is not a real number, because no real number raised to an even power is negative. An odd root of a negative number is negative, since a negative base raised to an odd power stays negative. The domain of a radical expression therefore depends on the index and on whether the radicand can be negative. These sign rules are the usual source of errors when solving radical equations, where a computed value must be checked in the original equation.
