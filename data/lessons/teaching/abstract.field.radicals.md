# Solvability by radicals

## Radical Expressions

A polynomial is solvable by radicals when its roots can be built from the coefficients using field operations and root extraction.

Galois theory turns this into a property of the Galois group.

The group must be solvable for such a formula to exist.

## The Quintic

The general polynomial of degree n has Galois group the symmetric group on n symbols.

The symmetric group is solvable up to degree four and not solvable from degree five.

That failure is why there is no general radical formula for the quintic.

## Degree Is Not the Whole Story

Special polynomials of high degree can still be solvable.

The criterion depends on the particular Galois group.

A common question asks for the largest degree covered by the general formula.

Solvability depends on the Galois group rather than on the degree alone. Compute or identify the group first, then test solvability. A group with a composition series of abelian factors is solvable while a simple nonabelian factor blocks a radical formula.
