# Points, Lines, and Planes

Geometry begins with three undefined building blocks: the **point**, the **line**, and the **plane**. Every other geometric object is described in terms of these.

## The Basic Objects

A **point** names an exact location. It has no size and no shape — we draw it as a dot and label it with a capital letter, like point $A$.

A **line** is a straight path of points that continues forever in both directions. A line through points $A$ and $B$ is written $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$. Any two points determine exactly one line.

A **ray** starts at one endpoint and extends forever in one direction. Ray $\overrightarrow{AB}$ starts at $A$ and passes through $B$.

A **segment** is the part of a line between two endpoints. Segment $\overline{AB}$ consists of points $A$ and $B$ together with everything between them.

A **plane** is a perfectly flat surface that extends forever in two dimensions. Three points that are not on the same line determine exactly one plane.

## How Points and Lines Interact

- Points are **collinear** if they lie on the same line, and **noncollinear** otherwise.
- Two lines in a plane either intersect at exactly one point or are **parallel** and never meet.
- When two lines cross, they share a point called the **intersection**.

## Example

**Example**

Draw segment $\overline{AB}$, then extend it past $B$ forever. Describe what you drew.

*Solution*

Extending segment $\overline{AB}$ past $B$ in one direction gives ray $\overrightarrow{AB}$. Extending it past both $A$ and $B$ gives the full line $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$.

## Summary

- A point is a location; a line is a straight path extending forever in both directions; a plane is a flat surface extending forever in two dimensions.
- A ray has one endpoint; a segment has two.
- Two points determine a line; three noncollinear points determine a plane.
