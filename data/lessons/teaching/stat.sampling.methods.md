# Sampling methods

A sample is a subset of a population. The sampling method determines whether the sample can support a general claim about the whole population.

## Simple and systematic samples

A simple random sample gives every unit the same chance of selection and makes selections independent.

A systematic sample selects every $k$th unit from a list after a random start. If the list has $N$ units, the sampling interval is often $k=N/n$. The number selected is about $N/k$.

Systematic sampling is easy to administer. It can fail when the list has a repeating pattern that matches the interval.

## Cluster samples

A cluster sample divides the population into clusters, selects whole clusters at random and studies every unit inside the selected clusters.

If each cluster has $m$ units and the design selects $c$ clusters, the sample size is $cm$. Cluster sampling is cheap when the clusters are already grouped geographically.

It usually has a larger design effect than simple random sampling because units inside a cluster tend to be similar.

## Stratified samples

A stratified sample divides the population into strata and samples inside every stratum. Proportional allocation matches each stratum's share of the sample to its share of the population.

If a stratum is one quarter of the population and the sample size is 120, the stratum contributes 30 units.

Stratification can improve precision when the strata are more homogeneous than the population. It differs from cluster sampling: strata are all sampled, while clusters are sampled as whole groups.
