# Complementary and Supplementary Angles

Pairs of angles often work together to fill out a right angle or a straight line. Two special relationships come up so often that they have names.

## The Definitions

Two angles are **complementary** if the sum of their measures is exactly $90^\circ$. Each angle is called the *complement* of the other.

\[x + y = 90^\circ \quad \text{(complementary)}\]

Two angles are **supplementary** if the sum of their measures is exactly $180^\circ$. Each angle is called the *supplement* of the other.

\[x + y = 180^\circ \quad \text{(supplementary)}\]

Memory aid: **C**omplementary comes before **S**upplementary alphabetically, and $90^\circ$ comes before $180^\circ$ numerically.

Complementary angles need not touch; supplementary angles that share a side form a **linear pair** and together make a straight line.

## Example

**Example**

Find the complement and the supplement of a $35^\circ$ angle.

*Solution*

Complement: $90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$.

Supplement: $180^\circ - 35^\circ = 145^\circ$.

**Example**

Two angles are complementary. One measures $2x$ and the other measures $3x$. Find both angles.

*Solution*

The measures must add to $90^\circ$:

$2x + 3x = 5x = 90^\circ$, so $x = 18^\circ$.

The angles measure $36^\circ$ and $54^\circ$, and indeed $36^\circ + 54^\circ = 90^\circ$.

## Summary

- Complementary angles: two angles whose measures add to $90^\circ$.
- Supplementary angles: two angles whose measures add to $180^\circ$.
- To find a complement, subtract from $90^\circ$; to find a supplement, subtract from $180^\circ$.
