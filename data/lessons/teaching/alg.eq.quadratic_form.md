# The Quadratic Formula in Standard Form

## Standard Form

The quadratic formula solves any equation that can be written in standard form ax^2 + bx + c = 0 with a not zero. The solutions are x = (-b +/- sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a). Standard form matters because the formula reads the coefficients directly, and a term moved to the wrong side or a missing sign changes the answer. Always write the equation with zero on one side and collect like terms before identifying a, b and c, including the signs.

## Applying the Formula

The expression under the square root, b^2 - 4ac, is the discriminant. Computing it first makes the arithmetic shorter and predicts the result. A positive discriminant gives two distinct real solutions, zero gives one repeated solution, and a negative discriminant gives a conjugate pair of complex solutions. When the discriminant is a perfect square the roots are rational, which also means the quadratic factors over the integers.

## Reading the Solutions

After substituting, simplify the square root and divide every term in the numerator by 2a. Leaving the answer as a single fraction avoids rounding errors and makes the structure visible. A double root means the parabola touches the axis at its vertex, and a negative discriminant means the parabola never meets the axis. Checking the solutions in the original equation catches sign slips that are easy to make in the substitution.
