# Local Compactness

**Locally compact:** $X$ is locally compact if every $x$ has a compact neighbourhood (a compact set containing a neighbourhood of $x$). Every compact space is locally compact; $\mathbb R^{n}$ is locally compact via closed balls.

## Local vs Global

### Euclidean Spaces
$\mathbb R$ is locally compact: $x$ has compact neighbourhood $[x-ε,x+ε]$. Similarly $\mathbb R^{n}$.

### Non-Examples
$\mathbb Q$ is not locally compact — no neighbourhood in $\mathbb Q$ is compact. Infinite-dimensional Hilbert space unit ball is not compact, so not locally compact.

## Example

One-point compactification: locally compact Hausdorff $X$ embeds densely in compact Hausdorff $X\cup\{\infty\}$, e.g. $\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}\cong S^{1}$.
