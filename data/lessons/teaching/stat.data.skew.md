# Skewness and distribution shape

Skewness describes an asymmetry in a distribution. A right-skewed distribution has a longer right tail, while a left-skewed distribution has a longer left tail.

## Symmetry and tails

A symmetric distribution has tails of similar length. A right-skewed distribution has a few unusually large values that stretch the right tail. Income and house prices are often right-skewed.

A left-skewed distribution has a longer left tail. Exam scores with a ceiling effect can look left-skewed because many scores cluster near the top.

## Mean and median under skew

In a right-skewed distribution the mean is usually larger than the median, because extreme large values pull the mean upward. In a left-skewed distribution the mean is usually smaller than the median.

The direction of the pull is a useful check, not a proof. Compare the mean and median together with a graph instead of relying on one summary.

## Reading shape from a graph

A histogram shows the tails, the centre and the number of peaks. Two separated peaks mean the data may mix two groups, and a single summary can hide that structure.

Skewness is a property of the whole distribution. A mean, median or standard deviation alone cannot tell you the shape.
