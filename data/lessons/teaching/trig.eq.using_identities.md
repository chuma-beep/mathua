# Solving Equations with Identities

## Replace Before Solving

Use a Pythagorean, sum or double-angle identity to rewrite the equation in a single trigonometric function.

For an equation containing sine squared and cosine, replace cosine squared by one minus sine squared, or the other way round.

Choose the replacement that leaves the simplest equation; both directions are valid but one may factor more easily.

## Factoring and Solving

After substitution, factor the equation or treat it as a quadratic in the remaining function.

If the equation becomes 2u squared minus u minus one equals zero, factor it and solve for u before returning to the angle.

Reject any value outside the range of sine or cosine, since those functions never take such values.

## Checking in the Original

A solution produced through an identity must satisfy the original equation, not only the rewritten one.

Squaring both sides can introduce extraneous solutions, so substitution is not optional in those cases.

List the solutions in the requested interval and state the period if the equation is not restricted.
