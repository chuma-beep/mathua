# Area of a Triangle

The **area** of a triangle is the number of unit squares needed to cover it. Any triangle is half of a rectangle, which gives the formula:

\[A = \frac{1}{2}bh\]

where $b$ is the **base** — the length of one side, usually drawn at the bottom — and $h$ is the **height**, the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex.

## Why Half?

Take a rectangle with the same base $b$ and height $h$. Its area is $bh$. Drawing one diagonal cuts the rectangle into two identical (congruent) triangles, so each triangle holds exactly half the squares: $\frac{1}{2}bh$.

This works for every triangle, not just ones sitting on their bottom side. Any side may serve as the base as long as the height is measured perpendicular to it.

## Example

**Example**

Find the area of a triangle with base $10$ cm and height $6$ cm.

*Solution*

\[A = \frac{1}{2}(10)(6) = \frac{1}{2}(60) = 30\]

The area is $30\text{ cm}^2$.

**Example**

A triangle has sides $8$, $5$, and a height of $3$ drawn to the side of length $8$. Find its area.

*Solution*

Only the base that matches the given height matters; the third side is extra information.

\[A = \frac{1}{2}(8)(3) = \frac{1}{2}(24) = 12\]

The area is $12$ square units.

## Units

Base and height are lengths, so area comes out in square units: if the lengths are in inches, the area is in square inches ($\text{in}^2$).

## Summary

- Triangle area: $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$.
- The height must be perpendicular to the chosen base.
- Any side can be the base; pair it with its own height.
- Area units are always square units.
