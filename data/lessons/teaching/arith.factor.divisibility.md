# Divisibility Rules

## Rules for Small Divisors

A divisibility rule tests a factor without doing the division. A number is divisible by 2 when its last digit is even, by 5 when it ends in 0 or 5, and by 10 when it ends in 0.

The rule for 3 uses the digit sum. Add the digits and test whether the sum is divisible by 3. The rule for 9 is the same with divisibility by 9. These rules work because ten leaves remainder one when divided by 3 or 9.

## Rules Built From Others

Rules combine when the factors are coprime. A number is divisible by 6 when it is divisible by both 2 and 3. It is divisible by 15 when it is divisible by both 3 and 5.

The combination fails when the factors share a factor. Being divisible by 2 and by 4 does not prove divisibility by 8, because 2 and 4 are not coprime. Test the rule that matches the factor directly.

## Using Rules to Find Factors

Divisibility rules speed up factor searches. Test the small primes in order and stop at the square root of the number. Every factor below the square root pairs with one above it.

The rules also explain why a number such as 1,234,567 is not prime. Its digit sum is 28, which is not divisible by 3, but its last digit is odd, so the test moves on to 7, 11 and the remaining primes.
