# Unit Vectors

## Length of a vector

The length of (a, b) is the square root of a^2 + b^2.
Length is always zero or positive.
A vector of length 1 is called a unit vector.

Length comes from the distance formula, so a vector and the arrow from the origin to its tip have the same length. Squaring the components removes negative signs before they are added.

## Dividing by the length

Divide each component by the length to get a unit vector.
The result points in the same direction as the original vector.
Check the result by squaring its components and adding them.

Normalising a vector keeps its direction and changes only its length. If the original vector is zero the division is impossible, so the zero vector has no unit direction. For every other vector the result has length one.

## Direction angles

A unit vector at angle theta is (cos theta, sin theta).
The components are the cosine and sine of the direction angle.
This form links vectors to polar coordinates.

The pair (cos theta, sin theta) is the unit vector at angle theta. Reading a direction as an angle and as a pair of components therefore uses the same information in two forms. This connection is used later for polar coordinates and for resolving forces.
