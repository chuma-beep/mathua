# Simple Harmonic Motion

## The Motion Model

Simple harmonic motion is described by a sinusoidal displacement such as d = A cos(omega t). The amplitude is A.

The angular frequency omega controls how quickly the motion repeats, and the period is 2 pi divided by omega.

The equilibrium position is the midline of the motion, not necessarily zero.

## Amplitude and Period

The maximum displacement from equilibrium equals the amplitude. The time for one complete cycle is the period.

If the displacement ranges from -4 to 4, the amplitude is 4 and the midline is 0.

Frequency is the number of cycles per second, the reciprocal of the period in seconds.

## Reading a Motion Graph

A maximum occurs when the cosine term equals one, and a minimum when it equals minus one.

The velocity is zero at the extremes and greatest at equilibrium, which is why a pendulum pauses at each end.

Check the units of time in the model; the period is measured in the same unit as the input.


Real motion loses energy, so a true physical oscillation is damped rather than perfectly sinusoidal. The undamped model is still the right first approximation, and it names the amplitude and period that a damped graph gradually loses.
