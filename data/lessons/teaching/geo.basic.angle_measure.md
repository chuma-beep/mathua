# Measuring Angles in Degrees

Angles are measured with a unit called the **degree**, written $^\circ$. One degree is $\frac{1}{360}$ of a full circle, so a complete turn is $360^\circ$, a straight line is $180^\circ$, and a square corner (a right angle) is $90^\circ$.

## Using a Protractor

A **protractor** is the tool for measuring angles. To measure $\angle ABC$:

1. Place the protractor's center point on the vertex $B$.
2. Line up the $0^\circ$ mark with side $BA$.
3. Read the scale where side $BC$ crosses the protractor. Choose the scale that starts at $0^\circ$.

## Comparing Angle Measures

The measure lets us sort angles precisely:

- Acute: $m\angle < 90^\circ$
- Right: $m\angle = 90^\circ$
- Obtuse: $90^\circ < m\angle < 180^\circ$

We write $m\angle ABC$ for "the measure of angle $ABC$." Two angles are **congruent** if they have equal measures.

## Example

**Example**

A protractor shows ray $BA$ at $0^\circ$ and ray $BC$ crossing the scale at $65$. Classify $\angle ABC$.

*Solution*

$m\angle ABC = 65^\circ$, which is less than $90^\circ$, so the angle is acute.

**Example**

An angle measures one-fourth of a full turn. What is its measure, and what type is it?

*Solution*

One-fourth of $360^\circ$ is $\frac{360^\circ}{4} = 90^\circ$. The angle is a right angle.

## Summary

- Angles are measured in degrees ($^\circ$); a full turn is $360^\circ$.
- A protractor measures angles by aligning its center with the vertex and its zero mark with one side.
- $m\angle$ notation records an angle's measure.
