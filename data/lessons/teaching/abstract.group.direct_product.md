# Direct products of groups

## Ordered Pairs

The direct product consists of pairs with coordinatewise multiplication.

The identity is the pair of identities, and inverses are taken coordinate by coordinate.

The order of the product is the product of the factor orders.

## Element Orders

The order of a pair is the least common multiple of the coordinate orders.

The exponent of the product is the least common multiple of the factor exponents.

This is why a product of cyclic groups need not be cyclic.

## When the Product Is Cyclic

A product of two cyclic groups is cyclic exactly when the orders are coprime.

The Chinese remainder theorem is the classical number-theoretic case.

Checking coprimality is usually the fastest route to the answer.

When a product is presented as several cyclic factors, collect the orders and take the least common multiple for the exponent. If the question asks whether the product is cyclic, test pairwise coprimality rather than searching for a generator.

The same reasoning extends to any finite number of factors. Take the least common multiple of all coordinate orders for an element, and of all factor exponents for the whole product.
