# Estimating Quotients

## Rounding Before Dividing

An estimate replaces the dividend and divisor with nearby friendly numbers. Round the divisor to a multiple of ten and the dividend to a number that divides easily. The quotient of the rounded values is the estimate.

For 372 divided by 18, round 18 to 20 and 372 to 380. The estimate is 380 divided by 20, which is 19. The exact quotient is close to that value.

## Choosing Useful Numbers

Not every rounding helps. The rounded dividend should be a multiple of the rounded divisor when possible. Adjusting by a small amount is allowed because the goal is a quick estimate rather than a rule.

A compatible number is chosen for the division rather than for the nearest place. For 4,180 divided by 58, use 4,200 divided by 60, which gives 70. The numbers are close and the division is mental.

## Using the Estimate

An estimate checks the exact answer. If the estimate is 19 and the written answer is 190, a place-value error is likely. If the answer is 2, the estimate is far enough away to justify a second look.

An estimate also predicts the size of a word-problem answer. A quotient of about 20 means a plan that needs 18 groups is plausible while a plan that needs 180 groups is not.
