# Average Rate of Change on an Interval

## Secants and Average Rate

The average rate of change of a function on an interval measures how much the output changes per unit of input. It is the slope of the secant line through the two endpoints of the interval. If f is a function and the interval runs from x = a to x = b, the average rate is (f(b) - f(a)) / (b - a). The numerator is a change in the output and the denominator is a change in the input, so the quotient answers a question about a rate rather than about a single value.

## Computing from a Table

When the function is given by a table, the same formula applies. Locate the rows for the two input values, subtract their outputs in the same order, and divide by the difference of the inputs. Keeping the order consistent matters: reversing both differences leaves the answer unchanged, but reversing only one changes the sign. A positive rate means the output rises across the interval, a negative rate means it falls, and a rate of zero means the endpoints have equal outputs even if the graph moves in between.

## Units and Interpretation

The units of an average rate come from dividing the output unit by the input unit. A distance in metres over a time in seconds gives metres per second; a cost in dollars over a quantity in items gives dollars per item. Naming the units is part of the answer because a bare number does not say what is changing. Average rate is also the bridge to calculus: shrinking the interval makes the secant slope approach the tangent slope, which is the derivative.
