# Probability from a density curve

## Density curves



A density curve is nonnegative and encloses total area one.

Probability corresponds to area under the curve.

The total area condition fixes the normalising constant.

The total area condition is what makes a density a probability model. Multiplying an arbitrary positive curve by the right constant produces a valid density.

## Probability as area



An interval probability is a definite integral over that interval.

Tail probabilities are improper integrals.

Symmetry can replace a difficult tail calculation.

Areas translate directly into probabilities, so integration replaces counting outcomes. Tails are handled with limits and therefore become improper integrals.

## The normal density



The normal density is a bell-shaped curve centred at its mean.

The area within one standard deviation is about 68 percent.

Standardising converts any normal area to the standard normal.

The normal density has no elementary antiderivative, so tables or standardised values are used. The empirical rule gives quick estimates without a table.
